# 1 — Cosmic mythology: the complete causal chain

## 1.1 The two primordial powers [CONFIRMED foundation; creator's expanded draft 2026-10-12]

**[[Aethyr]] and [[Nhal]] were primordial essences, not gods in the later sense.** Their creative and destructive energies were more fundamental than any laws or realms. Before the [[First Sundering]] there was no Physical Realm, no Void, no Veil, no space and no time. Thus “eons” describes an immeasurable conflict using a later metaphor, not an actual pre-creation calendar.

Aethyr sought to bring forth and sustain existence. Nhal sought to unmake anything that might be brought forth. Their struggle settled into a stalemate that direct force could not break. Aethyr eventually **conceived a new strategy**: it did not need to annihilate Nhal if it could **imprison Nhal apart from what Aethyr created**. Such a separation required making the very realms and boundary that did not yet exist.

Aethyr deliberately **shattered its own primordial unity**. In the resulting **First Sundering**, the eruption of primordial energy simultaneously brought into being **the Physical Realm** and **the Void**. The force propelled Nhal into the newly formed Void and dispersed Aethyr's shards into the Physical Realm. As its last protective act Aethyr formed **the Veil between the two realms**, so that Nhal could no longer freely unmake physical creation. This was Aethyr's purpose, not an accidental byproduct of a desperate explosion.

The Physical Realm became the place where space, time, matter and the fundamental laws of magic could exist. The Void is a realm of negation rather than a physical demon kingdom. Nhal survived there as a primordial essence but was **confined**. Aethyr's unified consciousness did not survive; from its shattered energies seven equal, distinct divine beings awakened within the Physical Realm, lacking full knowledge of their origin.

**Authorial causation:** stalemate → Aethyr's revelation that confinement could succeed where destruction could not → conscious decision to pay the cost of self-shattering → creation of both realms → displacement of Nhal and Aethyr's shards → deliberate Veil → protected creation → the Seven awakening without memory of the plan. This is the chain that all later histories should preserve.

**Editorial qualification:** This expansion comes from the user's explicit 2026-10-12 **draft clarification**. The causal foundations are intentional creative direction, while the final wording and the Veil's precise mechanics remain open to revision. The earlier confirmed choice that Aethyr shattered itself deliberately is preserved. No older names or discarded light-versus-dark mythology are restored.

## 1.2 The Seven are successors, not servants [CONFIRMED concept; metaphysical specifics WORKING]
The Seven emerged from Aethyr and are independent persons, not puppets or seven full copies. The original primordial struggle is largely unknown to them, while Nhal remembers more. They begin as equals with no high god. [[Magic]] was not invented by gods; they learned its laws. Rare acts combine several gods' powers. The seven primordial correspondences considered were continuity/Guardian, understanding/Seeker, will/Warrior, making/Shaper, order/Judge, boundary/Keeper, potential/Ascendant. These are interpretive correspondences, not seven mortal magical disciplines.

The long-term narrative question of *whether recovering Aethyr requires annihilating the individuality of the Seven* was floated as an ethical dilemma, NOT resolved.

## 1.2a The Seven's separate awakenings and Ascendant's arrival [USER-CONFIRMED DIRECTION, 2026-10-12]

The Seven awaken at different times and encounter one another progressively. **Ascendant awakens independently but joins the other gods only after their relationships and mortal creation efforts are already developing.** The user explicitly selected this over the alternative that Ascendant only came into existence after all other gods had created their races. His independent period gives him time to discover his powers and establish a philosophy before entering a divine community that already has shared history. His outsider status and envy therefore arise partly from circumstance and interpretation, not necessarily intentional ostracism.

His later desire for a unique legacy makes collaboration with Keeper and advocacy of the Kel'vari project more emotionally significant. Keeper's knowledge is needed for the joint troll creation, and Ascendant's unauthorized changes damage their trust. **Not decided:** precise awakening order of the other six, duration of Ascendant's solitude and exact early encounters. **Later decided:** Ascendant alone created the Kel'vari using Keeper's research beyond permission. **Now decided:** elves, orcs, dwarves, humans and halflings existed before his arrival. The **Guardian–Warrior philosophical disagreement and Seeker–Shaper discovery-first partnership are now confirmed**. Only the precise awakening order of the first six gods and the detailed first encounters remain open.

## 1.2c Origin of mortal life and six-species chronology [USER LOCKED · 2026-10-12]

The user approved the **Seeker–Shaper discovery-first** model and explicitly chose that they **refrain from creating independent mortal life because their understanding is incomplete**. Their partnership emerges from shared discovery: Seeker investigates fundamental magical principles; Shaper gives them form. They identify how independent consciousness may become possible but cannot fully account for its autonomy and continued stability. Seeker insists on further understanding, while Shaper experiences frustration at the delay.

Guardian and Warrior, already bonded through an initial philosophical disagreement over preservation versus resilience, take that research further and successfully create **the first two mortal races: Guardian's elves, then Warrior's orcs**. Their creations express different but equally legitimate visions of independent life. Shaper, inspired by mortals who can themselves create and transform, **then creates dwarves**. Seeker remains investigator/teacher, **not** creator of any of the six original species. Judge **then creates humans** in response to questions of free will, law and social responsibility; Keeper **then creates halflings** with magical stability and resilience in mind.

**Ascendant independently awakened earlier but joins the community only after these five races exist.** This is now an explicit chronological lock, superseding the earlier open question about how many races had been made before his arrival. He finds established friendships and creations and experiences outsider status, although deliberate exclusion is not established. Ascendant later seeks Keeper's knowledge and they **jointly create trolls as the sixth people**, before Ascendant's unauthorized alterations damage trust. The **Kel'vari** arise later through **Ascendant's sole divine creation**, using Keeper's research beyond the limited permission he had been granted. **Later creator override:** Nhal did not influence Ascendant's creation. Nhal observed but could not interact; only later Kel'vari scholars' accidental [[First Resonance]] enabled limited manipulation.

The **precise awakening order of the first six gods**, their first conversations, and the details of Seeker and Shaper's original discovery remain open as dramatic particulars. The structural order above is confirmed, and must not be undone by an older speculative draft.

## 1.3 The Void and the boundary [working detail]
Void means no conventional space, time, geography, matter, ordinary life or ambient physical magic. It must not become a conventional demon kingdom. **The Veil was deliberately formed by Aethyr during the First Sundering** as the boundary protecting the Physical Realm from the newly created Void. Its existence and purpose are explicit in the creator's 2026-10-12 draft; its exact mechanics remain working, and it is not a new magical school. Ordinary spells do not damage it. **Nhal can observe the Physical Realm from the Void but cannot interact directly.** Exceptional six-field workings can accidentally resonate with the Veil without breaching it. **After the first such resonance**, Nhal gains an extremely limited ability to influence similar experiments, not universal mind control or free speech. Repeated resonant research may strengthen the influence, and the later First Anchor converts the resonance into a persistent breach; anchors allow limited physical effects and eventually an attempted stable incarnation. Nhal is neither omniscient nor able to dominate any person at will. Its strength lies in providing real but selective knowledge and exploiting preexisting personality flaws.

**OPEN:** precise mechanics, which anchors survive ancient times, limits of mind interference and what a final victory requires.

## 1.4 Ascendant's sole creation of the Kel'vari [LATER USER DECISIONS LOCKED]

**Supersedes the earlier multi-god account.** Ascendant alone created the Kel'vari. Keeper had granted **limited access** to advanced stability research as an attempt to rebuild trust after the troll creation. Ascendant secretly reconstructed and extended knowledge beyond that permission, using his own amplification and synthesis to produce independent mortal beings with an extraordinary understanding of relationships among magical disciplines. The other gods learned of the completed creation later; Seeker and Shaper did not directly co-create this people. Ascendant was motivated by the desire for an unshared legacy, but did **not** knowingly serve Nhal.

**Original Q40 is retained as superseded source history, not current canon.** The later creator decision explicitly rejects Nhal's involvement in the Kel'vari's creation. Ascendant independently achieved unprecedented six-field synthesis through dogged ambition and his own research. The resulting potential for an exceptional descendant to resonate accidentally with the Veil was **unforeseen, remote and not an implanted defect**. Nhal could observe the Physical Realm from the Void but **could not act** until later Kel'vari researchers, ignorant of both Nhal and the Veil, produced the First Resonance. That resonance was not a breach; it permitted only faint influence on subsequent similar experiments. Members of the Kel'vari are not inherently evil, invulnerable or possessed of unlimited magic. They alone can have children with any of the six original species, reflecting Ascendant's boundary-crossing design.

Their scholars later built the **First Anchor**, believing it would establish lasting contact with Ascendant. Nhal-influenced researchers secretly redirected the construction toward the Veil. Its activation initiated the **[[Second Sundering]] catastrophe**, devastating their homeland and fracturing the ancient world. The Anchor became entangled with the breach and could not be safely destroyed. The [[Divine War]] then escalated the losses; **[[The Binding]]** finally contained the breach within the Shroud.

**Kel'vari survival:** multiple small independent enclaves survive inside the Shroud; some inhabitants were transformed by Nhal's unrelenting influence, without all being conscious [[The Unmade|Unmade]] operatives. Some Kel'vari researchers were abroad or escaped before The Binding, and their descendants assimilated into the six races through unique interracial compatibility. Their confirmed species name is **Kel'vari**; **Aethren** was rejected for resembling Aethyr.

## 1.4a Nhal's observation and the accidental First Resonance [LOCKED · 2026-10-12]

Nhal's imprisonment in the Void did **not** prevent observation of the Physical Realm. The Veil prevented Nhal from entering, influencing mortals, altering magical experiments or communicating directly. Nhal could watch the Seven awaken, study their relationships, and witness the creation of all seven mortal races. Observation did **not** confer omniscience, knowledge of hidden thoughts or foresight.

Ascendant's Kel'vari was an **independent achievement**: his own amplification and synthesis allowed mortals to combine the **six fundamental magical fields** at a depth the earlier races could not match. Nhal recognized the remote possibility that such expertise might eventually disturb the Veil but **could not influence Ascendant, implant any property, or guarantee the outcome**. The unusual ability itself was not corruption. The eventual resonance arose through a mixture of chance, unprecedented magical aptitude, exceptional scholars' persistent research and the unknowable limits of Ascendant's foresight.

Generations later, a rare experiment combining the six fields at exceptional precision **accidentally resonated with the Veil**. [[Kel'vari]] researchers did **not** know the Veil or Nhal existed. The resonance caused no open breach and released no region-wide corruption. **Only from this point** could Nhal weakly favour certain experimental outcomes through that specific interaction. Its long observation of the scholars' goals allowed it to choose which subsequent experiments to encourage. It sought the possibility of converting a faint resonance into an actual breach, but did not know the entire future. The researchers' later pursuit of a permanent connection to Ascendant became the opportunity Nhal manipulated into the **First Anchor**.

**Explicit supersession:** Earlier Q40 interpretations that Nhal corrupted the Kel'vari *during its creation* are no longer canon. Retain the historical answer in the source register but use this later creator decision for all present and future narrative work. The exact physics of observation and resonance remain to be developed without contradicting these limits.

## 1.5 The Divine Schism [LOCKED broad sequence; detailed motivations WORKING]

The First Anchor's catastrophe preceded the Divine War. The gods divided over Ascendant's unauthorized creation, responsibility for the breach, the fate of innocent Kel'vari survivors, restrictions on dangerous research and how to contain an unknown influence. Guardian prioritised innocent lives; Warrior demanded honourable judgement; Judge accountability; Seeker investigation; Shaper preservation of knowledge; Keeper containment and secrecy; Ascendant protection of his people and recognition of his achievement. Nhal's adherents mixed genuine evidence of Ascendant's misconduct with false accusations of deliberate service to the Void.

The **[[The Guardians|Guardians]]** were founded during this war, before The Binding, by Kel'vari investigators and trusted members of the other six races who knew an **unknown influence manipulated research** but did not know Nhal's identity. The Unmade descended from the opposing researchers who chose to pursue that influence. Mortal orders and civilizations fought in shifting alignments, not the later [[Covenant]]/[[Dominion]] binary. **The Binding** ultimately confined all Seven in sustaining the Shroud; Keeper knew this outcome with certainty and deliberately withheld it. Exact divine wartime sides and the final fate of Nhal remain open.

## 1.6 The Second Sundering and The Binding [NEWLY CONFIRMED CORE · 2026-10-12]

The creator's developing historical sequence places the Kel'vari's **First Anchor** and the devastating breach of the Veil before the final containment. The Anchor's activation and the ensuing Divine War devastate the ancient world; the Kel'vari's homeland becomes the centre of what is eventually known as the **Shrouded Lands**. The exact sequence of geographical fractures, the fate of every individual Kel'vari-person and the timing of the Divine War remain under narrative refinement.

**The Binding** is the name of the final divine containment event. The Seven combine substantial portions of their divine essences to create and continuously maintain the Shroud around the breached region. The Shroud is a containment **within** the Physical Realm, distinct from Aethyr's primordial **Veil** between the Physical Realm and the Void.

**Keeper's conscious deception is LOCKED:** Keeper knows **with certainty**, before The Binding begins, that all seven divine essences and consciousnesses will be bound to sustaining the Shroud and cannot simply withdraw after contributing. The other six believe they are making a **limited contribution of essence** and will remain free afterward. Keeper **deliberately withholds** the true permanent/indefinite cost to secure their participation. This is the betrayal at the heart of the historical event, not its official name. The Seven are not killed; their essences and identities persist, constrained by their obligation to sustain the Shroud. This preserves the possibility that they could later reunify willingly into one primordial essence to confront Nhal if it emerges. That possible final convergence is **not yet locked as the ending**.

This overrides the earlier **working** interpretation in which Keeper and Guardian secretly commandeered shared magical infrastructure as the central Great Betrayal, and earlier proposed individual deaths or disappearance as irreversible loss of divine essence. Some gods may be considered “fallen” in mortal myth because they are bound, diminished or absent, rather than dead.

**Still open:** The precise mechanics of The Binding, Keeper's full reasoning and possible alternatives, the extent of each god's limited influence while bound, the precise timeline of the Anchor's activation and divine conflict, and how the Shroud could eventually be broken or replaced. Keeper's knowledge and intentional nondisclosure are **not** open.

The idea that the catastrophe damaged ancient magical infrastructure, restricted complex magic and later contributed to the awakening remains useful connective lore. The awakening itself is not evil.

## 1.6a Why players can destroy modern Anchors [LOCKED · 2026-10-12]

The **First Anchor** was created by Kel'vari researchers under Nhal's covert influence, ostensibly to connect with their divine creator. Its activation produced a **persistent breach in the Veil**. It became physically/metaphysically entangled with that breach: smashing the artifact would **not** reliably close the connection and could instead eliminate the remaining structure that prevents uncontrolled expansion. The gods chose **The Binding** and the **Shroud** to contain it, not because the First Anchor was immune to physical destruction, but because destroying it was catastrophically unsafe.

The Unmade lost the complete Kel'vari science, specialists and infrastructure in the catastrophe. They retain **incomplete designs, notes and artifacts**, allowing only inferior modern derivatives. These do not each create an independent permanent breach. They exploit the **existing connection/damage associated with the First Anchor**, establishing secondary, unstable, locally supported resonances. Such Anchors can be safely disabled or destroyed after identifying and disconnecting their supporting magical structures. Mishandling can cause local catastrophes, but need not trigger another Sundering.

**Progression tiers (story categories, not tuned combat numbers):** early **Minor Resonators** create local anomalies and are often directly destructible; mid-game **Regional Anchors** require multiple supporting structures to be neutralized; rare late-game **Major Anchors** require coordinated multi-stage dungeon/raid operations. Only a **coordinated network of advanced Anchors** seriously strains the Shroud and The Binding. Minor devices are not automatically world-ending. Guardians investigate connections and safe dismantling; The Unmade use distributed Anchors to extend Nhal's influence across both continents and gradually restore the path to the First Anchor. Covenant and Dominion pursue separate faction-loyal questlines, including level-banded overseas incursions.

**The original is unique and remains unresolved:** Destroying modern Anchors is a meaningful victory against the Unmade's network; it does not destroy the First Anchor, close the primordial breach, free the Seven or settle the possible final reunification.

## 1.6b Surviving Passage gates — continuous low-capacity transit [LOCKED · 2026-10-12]

The ancient Kel'vari homeland had Passage connections to other civilizations. The Binding and the Shroud reduced the power and throughput of these gates enormously, but **some gates remained continuously operational** in a weakened state across the Age of Silence. They did **not** function only during occasional magical alignments. Surviving links allowed individuals, small groups, limited artifacts and supplies to travel, with far fewer usable routes and no capacity for sustained army movement or military logistics.

The Unmade exploited some surviving gate links to maintain covert movement between the Shrouded Lands and both modern alliance continents. This helped them infiltrate and influence institutions rather than openly invade. Surviving Unmade cells outside the Shroud also had ordinary means of recruitment and movement. **A gate between physical locations does not provide direct communication with Nhal**; the loss of Nhal contact after The Binding remains consistent with these routes being active.

During the modern awakening, improved understanding and restored magical infrastructure can permit major gateways to be opened to organized Covenant and Dominion expeditions for Shrouded Lands PvP, without replacing shipborne overseas incursion outposts. The Shroud's primary containment remains in force until the larger plot challenges it. Small-scale passage of transformed Kel'vari creatures is a possible later narrative choice, not established by the user's gate decision. Precise capacity and route map remain open.

## 1.6c The Shroud contains corruption rather than removing it [USER CLARIFICATION · 2026-10-12]

The Shroud's divine containment protects the **rest of the Physical Realm** from the First Anchor's continuing breach, but **within the Shrouded Lands Nhal's corruption remains relentless**. The original Kel'vari homeland is therefore the continuing epicentre of the hidden threat and the **historical and operational heartland of The Unmade**. This is not a claim that Nhal has fully entered the world: the Seven still hold the Shroud to stop the corruption spreading beyond the contained region.

The Unmade arose among the Kel'vari's compromised researchers and retained an ancient centre of artifacts, knowledge and sanctuaries associated with the First Anchor. Its cells outside the Shroud infiltrated the later alliances, using continuously weakened Passage routes for individuals, artifacts and small groups, not armies. **Corrupted Kel'vari inhabitants are not automatically Unmade operatives**: many are transformed victims or fragmented defenders of ancestral structures, potentially including NPC stronghold reclamation. Ongoing corruption is distinct from **clear direct instructions from Nhal**; the long post-Binding silence of reliable communication remains consistent. Exact interior factions and the extent of purposeful direction remain open.

## 1.6d Surviving Kel'vari enclaves [USER-LOCKED · 2026-10-12]

The Kel'vari did not entirely disappear. **Multiple small independent enclaves of uncorrupted survivors** endure inside the Shroud, resisting the Unmade and the unrelenting local corruption. They are not a single surviving kingdom with a central monarch or command. Their small fortifications and ancient protected structures offer possible independent entrance outposts for later Covenant and Dominion Shrouded Lands campaigns, but the exact gateway agreements are still working design. Enclaves have not been made immortal or invulnerable.

**Later confirmed:** The Guardians originated during the Divine War as a multiracial coalition of Kel'vari investigators and trusted members of the other six races, before The Binding. Their original knowledge was limited to an unknown influence manipulating research. This founding proposal was subsequently approved: the Guardians were a multiracial coalition including Kel'vari researchers and trusted members of the six races.

## 1.6e The Kel'vari alone can have children across species [LOCKED · 2026-10-12]

**Superseding rule:** The original six mortal species cannot ordinarily have biological children with one another across species boundaries. Elven and troll cultural branches remain compatible within their respective species. **Only the Kel'vari** have the unique biological/magical compatibility to have children with members of any of the six species. Ascendant's design of the unusually adaptable Kel'vari provides the in-world explanation; this trait is not exceptional combat power, immortality or guaranteed high magical reserves.

**Historical consequence:** Kel'vari researchers and other survivors who had reached the outside world before The Binding could form families within the six races. Over generations, their descendants integrated culturally, adopted new names, lost language and records, and became largely indistinguishable from their communities. This explains the absence of a recognizable Kel'vari diaspora without requiring an impossible total erasure of its members. **Some modern individuals may unknowingly have direct Kel'vari ancestry**, usually without distinctive powers; rare unusual magical affinities are possible but not a universal inherited perk. The isolated Kel'vari enclaves inside the Shroud continue to preserve a distinct culture.

**Not decided:** Precise genetics, whether all possible descendants remain fertile with all six species, appearance and inheritance ratios, who retains genealogical records, and which NPC families carry this ancestry. The proposed mixed founding of the Guardians is compatible with this history but Guardian membership need not be tied to bloodline.

All Seven persist as distinct consciousnesses with their essences bound into maintaining the Shroud. Their former freedom to act in the Physical Realm is lost or profoundly limited. # 3 — Magic as a physical and cultural system

## 3.1 Energy and casting laws [CONFIRMED]
Magic is a primordial foundation of creation, not a resource owned by gods. Intent plus learned technique directs effects. Mortal casting spends from a finite **internal/personal reserve**, shared across disciplines. Aptitude/bloodlines influence capacity and affinity; training enhances capacity and efficiency. Recovery is natural, supported by ambient power and focused meditation. When reserves are depleted, casting normally stops safely. Attempting too-complex effects can overload the mind or lose control. Knowledge, precise discipline mastery and concentration commonly matter more than raw strength. Unstable natural/historical zones change risk and reliability.

## 3.2 Exactly six disciplines [names working, count confirmed]
**Elements & Energy** handles flame, cold, wind, light and other energetic effects. **Matter** manipulates physical materials and transformation; true permanent matter from nothing is ancient/lost. **Life** governs bodily/natural/spiritual operations; resurrection, if possible, needs severe conditions such as available body, consent, timing and extraordinary cost. **Form** governs magical patterns, conditions, wards, relations and structural stability. **Mind** concerns perception, illusion and emotion; mundane free will cannot be simply overridden at low cost. **Passage** concerns spatial transfer and links; long-distance teleportation is largely lost.

Elementary effects use one discipline; advanced two; extraordinary ancient techniques may require multiple. **One wielder must personally know every involved discipline. Normal multi-mage co-casting cannot simply stack participants to bypass expertise.** This does not ban coordinated armies, distinct independently built mechanisms, or exceptionally joint *divine* creative acts.

Enchantments normally have stored/rechargeable reserves; very rare self-sustaining enchantments are a lost craft. Durable creation is difficult and often ancient.

## 3.3 Culture and economics [confirmed choices]
Magic is common in major urban centres, less common in remote settlements. Traditional and magical engineering **compete** rather than one universally replacing the other. Medical traditions differ by people and locality. Magicians are military supports, integrated unit members and sometimes exceedingly powerful rare battle specialists. Warriors remain formidable without magic. Counter-mage traditions include protection/gear, anti-mage tactics and trained specialists. Magical and martial institutions compete for funding, respect and political leverage.

Racial specializations affect access to instructors, distinctive eventually learnable techniques and rare bloodline affinities, but no race is locked out of the disciplines. This is essential for player freedom. Classes, skills, spell lists, cooldowns, resources, stats, numbers and exact balance remain undecided.

# 5 — Chronological reconstruction of mortal history

## 5.1 Divine Age and orders [confirmed historical framework]
Ancient magic was far more sophisticated than today. **Three or four** major independent magical orders (the user's answer was **"Three or four great orders"**, not a fixed four) existed with numerous smaller schools. Orders differentiated by *philosophy + speciality + practical mission*, had several divine influences, shared libraries, research meetings and advanced passages, and openly questioned gods. They were politically independent but interconnected. Unlike the Seven, **most orders remained cooperative until war forced division**.

Surviving masters initially suppressed individual dangerous advanced workings (not whole disciplines). War destroyed institutes, killed wielders/bloodlines, scattered scholars, and caused deliberate restrictions. Some orders hid, were destroyed or fled. Survivors created royal academies, guild/religious schools and independent modern orders. Modern institutions may show direct continuity, fragmentary descent or complete reconstruction. Academic competition is central today.

**Exactly one original great order survives secretly**, but which one, its exact purpose, protection mechanisms, archives and relationship with the Guardians remains unresolved. Earlier assistant-proposed orders of Understanding, Making, Preservation, Horizons were **descriptive placeholders**. Do not finalize Preservation as survivor without approval.

The Guardians, founded during Divine War, are **not automatically the same** as the one surviving older magical order.

## 5.2 The First Anchor, Divine War and Binding [latest creator chronology]

The **First Anchor** was built by Kel'vari researchers seeking contact with Ascendant; Nhal-influenced investigators secretly redirected it into a breach in the Veil. Its activation **began the Second Sundering catastrophe**, devastating the Kel'vari homeland and destabilizing ancient magical and Passage infrastructure. The **Divine War followed**, worsening the catastrophe as gods and mortals fought over responsibility, dangerous research and how to contain the breach. The conflict was not the modern Covenant–Dominion war. During it, before The Binding, Kel'vari investigators and trusted members of the six races founded the Guardians, initially knowing only that an unknown influence manipulated magical research.

**The Binding** was the final containment. Keeper knew with certainty that all Seven would be bound indefinitely to maintaining the Shroud and deliberately withheld this from the others, who believed they were contributing only limited essence. The First Anchor could not be **safely** destroyed once entangled with the breach. The Shroud protects the wider world but does not remove the relentless corruption inside. All Seven survive as bound, conscious essences. The Kel'vari survive in multiple independent enclaves and through assimilated descendants beyond the Shroud.

The postwar world preserves competing myths, not one reliable eyewitness account. Exact divine wartime allegiances, the geographical sequence of ruptures and the eventual resolution of The Binding remain open.

## 5.3 Recovery and alliance formation
Postwar migrations used now-mostly-failed magical passageways. Ancient surviving dwarf and ancestral troll centres retain genuine fragments of Divine Age infrastructure and scholarship, but authenticity and interpretations vary.

Covenant's origins: human/dwarf pact mutually assured defence and economic cooperation (agricultural plains with mountain engineering). Dwarven autonomy is explicit. The human royal cadet branch governs Covenant, while the human monarch governs a distinct nation. Its multicultural capital is coastal. The exact treaties and names like “Treaty of Crown and Stone” or “War of Broken Roads” were **assistant working lore**.

Dominion's origins: orcs offered halflings protection/refuge during a crisis; partnership evolved around orcish security and halfling guild ingenuity. The Great Clans and guilds retained sovereignty. Later the leadership trials integrated nominations by all four member civilizations. The exact disaster that displaced halflings and founding date are **working**.

The **Aelari/Vaelari** rupture: shared original elven civilization and original capital, then religious/magical schism, city destroyed, new forest and mountain-lake governments, continuing hostility and incompatible histories. Guardian patron for Aelari; Seeker for Vaelari. **Magical responsibility** is the approved principal issue; specifics involving a laboratory accident, civil-war battle, research-complex explosion remain **working**.

The **Korrath/Vorrath** rupture: ancient Keeper-linked highland civilization, religious division centred on the two co-creators, exile of the population later called Vorrath, sacred storm-coast new capital, mutual resentment and different interpretations. Korrath chiefly Keeper; Vorrath chiefly Ascendant. Details of outlawed rituals and who unjustly altered chronicles remain **working**.

The modern war draws strength from territorial/strategic competition, conflicting ancestral claims, repeated reprisals and political legitimacy; it is not solely caused by Nhal. Both alliances are politically and morally complex and roughly balanced. No one side is canonically evil.

# 6 — Shattered Delegation, The Unmade, the Guardians and secret war

## 6.1 Historical event [explicitly CONFIRMED central cause]
Covenant and Dominion, despite entrenched hostility and lack of normal diplomacy, made **an exceptional attempted peace negotiation**. **The Unmade** orchestrated a deliberate attack that destroyed the delegation, prevented peace, caused both alliances to blame the other and reignited/intensified war. **This is not an independent mortal hardliner conspiracy**; such interests might have been exploited but The Unmade planned the event. Exactly how, which delegates died, and who commanded it remain **working**.

Working expansion: infiltrators compromised both delegations' protection; a hidden magical device catastrophically attacked the negotiation site; forged evidence implicated enemy agents to opposite survivors. Alaric and Draven each lost people they valued and carry conflicting assumptions. An ancient Veil-resonance detecting instrument was proposed as a second theft objective; its three-way fragmentation is **NOT confirmed**.

This is the strongest specific historical through-line tying current political anger to Nhal's goals. Nhal exploits but does not wholly cause the otherwise genuine war. The Unmade prefers ongoing war because fragmented power cannot investigate shared reality, military secrecy hides research, and leaders pursue hazardous advantage.

## 6.2 The Guardians [FOUNDING LOCKED · structure details WORKING]

**Origin during the Divine War, before The Binding:** The Guardians were established by a coalition of **surviving Kel'vari investigators and trusted members of the other six mortal races**. Some Kel'vari researchers had been abroad through the ancient Passage network when the First Anchor activated; others fled their homeland before the Shroud was raised. They had observed **an unknown influence manipulating magical research** and preserved evidence of falsified or inexplicable findings. **They did not know the name Nhal, its primordial nature or the full cosmology.** The First Anchor catastrophe and Divine War made that evidence both essential and dangerous.

**Founding mission:** Preserve evidence; investigate the hidden influence; prevent dangerous research from being repeated; protect survivors; and ensure no god, state or academy could monopolize the truth. The order was clandestine from its beginnings and **multiracial by founding**, not an exclusively Kel'vari order that later learned to recruit others. Guardians are not identical with The Kel'vari as a people or The Unmade as a conscious Nhal-serving movement. Membership depends on trust and mission, not bloodline. The single surviving original great magical order is **a distinct institution** unless a future decision establishes an explicit relationship.

**The Binding and the split:** Cells and hidden repositories existed inside and outside the Kel'vari homeland before The Binding. The Shroud separated them; surviving Passage gates remained continuously usable only at low capacity, preventing mass evacuation or army movement. Inside, Guardian-aligned survivors might help defend the small independent Kel'vari enclaves. Outside, the Guardians pursued containment and protection of the wider world. The competing needs could strain relationships across the boundary.

**Concealment [LOCKED]:** The earliest Guardians **deliberately concealed the survival and locations of the Kel'vari enclaves** to protect them from persecution, exploitation, unwanted expeditions and Unmade attack. Over generations, dispersed cells, restricted archives, lost records, warfare and cultural assimilation eroded even the order's own knowledge. **Many modern Guardians no longer know the full truth**; some custodians may preserve fragments. This is both a defensible act of protection and a lasting moral contradiction for an organization founded to preserve evidence. Outside the Shroud, descendants of Kel'vari researchers gradually integrated into the six races through the Kel'vari's unique interracial compatibility. Most have no unusual powers or knowledge of their lineage.

**Modern role:** Completely secret order, protecting selected ancient knowledge, monitoring anomalies and preventing Nhal's return. Operatives can be deeply embedded within both Covenant and Dominion under native identities; no public cross-alliance cooperation or third player faction. The Guardians may have failed to prevent the [[Shattered Delegation]], and members disagree over whether secrecy is protection or dangerous paternalism.

**WORKING institutional detail, not locked:** Offices called **Custodians** (archives/policy), **Watchers** (infiltration), **Wardens** (containment/intervention), **Chroniclers** (history). The original founding meeting's location, participants, precise surviving family genealogies, which senior Guardians know the enclave locations, and exact chain of command remain open.

## 6.3 The Unmade [CONFIRMED name and Nhal allegiance]
User selected **The Unmade**, replacing earlier “The Hollow” working label. Roots in ancient researchers choosing Nhal's influence; infiltrate royalty, commanders, guilds, temples, magical colleges, archaeological expeditions, quartermasters and supply networks in BOTH alliances. Highest leadership knowingly serves Nhal's return. Lesser members might believe they pursue academic privilege or political gain, without recognizing whom they serve.

They are not a playable third alliance. Some agents are native Covenant humans/dwarves/Aelari/Korrath; others native Dominion orcs/halflings/Vaelari/Vorrath. Their infiltration does not create public legal double allegiances.

Working subdivisions: outer circle unwitting followers, inner circle committed occult operatives, core leadership personally committed to Nhal. A hidden planner called **the Architect** was floated as the author of the Shattered Delegation and modern ancient-network activation, still without fixed race/identity.

## 6.4 How players discover the secret
Players enter as ordinary adventurers, not prophesied chosen ones. Local corruption, ambushed patrols, missing research and failed wards generate authentic stories. As players progress, irregularities link to Shattered Delegation and clandestine works. Guardian NPCs can appear under innocent public occupations and later confide limited secrets. **Contact with Guardians as narrative allies does not mean a joinable secondary faction**. Players can sabotage Unmade cells without being able to join Dominion/Covenant opposite sides.

Nhal remains a future primordial revelation. By the level-60 launch climax a player can identify the Unmade and its role in the negotiations, disrupt a regional Void anchor and learn ancient Divine War accounts are false/incomplete, but should not hear the entire true cosmology.

### Later creator-locked clarification (12 October 2026): recurrent resonance and continental separation
The Kel'vari researchers perceived only an unexpected result of their exceptional six-field experiment; they did not recognize its hidden resonance with the Veil. Continued experiments combining all six magical fields periodically reproduced the particular resonant alignment, each instance giving Nhal a fleeting chance to bias outcomes and thereby encourage further work. The researchers initially mistook the results for their own progress; a minority eventually pursued the suspected intelligence. This influence was entirely subsequent to Ascendant's independent creation of the Kel'vari.

The First Anchor's activation began the Second Sundering by physically tearing apart once-connected landmasses, creating the separated continental heartlands later occupied by Covenant and Dominion. The Kel'vari homeland was shattered and isolated, becoming the region later enclosed by the Shroud. The collapse of ancient Passage and civic magical networks spread disaster; the Divine War and The Binding compounded it. Exact geography is still open.


## 2026-10-13 creator LOCK — The Corrupted Conclave and five leadership roles

**[[The Corrupted Conclave]]** is the ancient inner council directing **The Unmade** beneath Nhal. Its founding leadership arose from surviving Kel'vari researchers and followers who knowingly pursued the alien intelligence before the First Anchor catastrophe. Following the loss of reliable communication with Nhal, they assumed responsibility for preserving the Unmade's purpose, research, sanctuaries and covert operations during the Age of Silence. They were not personally appointed by Nhal; their authority arose from their surviving knowledge, influence and decision to continue. Their willing pursuit preceded their corruption; not every transformed Kel'vari inhabitant belongs to this leadership or to the Unmade.

**Five approved leadership titles and character foundations:**
- **The First Voice:** Principal interpreter of Nhal's will; authoritative and devoted, with legitimacy increasingly challenged by the long silence.
- **The Architect:** Master of forbidden research and Anchor construction; brilliant, obsessive and determined to recover lost techniques.
- **The Veiled Hand:** Oversees infiltration and covert networks; patient, calculating and adept at manipulating mortal institutions.
- **The Harbinger:** Maintains doctrine, devotion and recruitment; charismatic, fanatical and willing to elevate fragmentary instructions into sacred teaching.
- **The Warden:** Military protector of the First Anchor and Unmade strongholds; formidable, disciplined and relentless.

**Later creator lock superseding the hybrid succession model:** All five founding Conclave members are the principal Kel'vari scholars who knowingly helped redirect and activate the First Anchor. During its breach, Nhal deliberately channelled escaping primordial energies into these willing followers, transforming their bodies and magical structures. All five endure into the modern era with extraordinary longevity, magical capacity and resilience; they are not divine, invulnerable or unkillable. Their personal names are locked as Vaer'uun, Thes'var, Su'vareth, Oma'vira and Khor'vun. Their exact limitations, costs, appearances and individual powers remain open. Their five offices may pass to successors if a founder is killed, but no founder's death or replacement is established. These leaders may serve as major storyline or raid antagonists, but exact encounters are not yet canon. Nhal's ongoing corrupting pressure within the Shroud must remain distinct from direct communication, which ceased reliably after the catastrophe and Binding.

## 2026-10-13 creator LOCK — Kel'vari race and Oth'vaeri language

The people's official name is **[[Kel'vari]]** (both singular and plural: one Kel'vari scholar; the Kel'vari people). Their ancestral language is **[[Oth'vaeri]]**. Oth'vaeri naming convention uses **apostrophes**, not hyphens, for meaningful sound boundaries in joined names; the intended sound is melodic, elegant and subtly unfamiliar, distinct from elven naming. The Kel'vari and Oth'vaeri names are **locked**. The five Conclave founders' personal names, Oth'varen's name and the five significant enclave names are also **locked** by the creator decisions below. A complete language grammar remains open.

## 2026-10-13 creator LOCK — Kel'vari federation and authorised First Anchor project

The Kel'vari civilization before the Second Sundering was a **federation of largely autonomous cities**, with local civic councils, scholarly assemblies and a federal assembly coordinating intercity law, shared infrastructure, defence and consequential research. Its population included ordinary families, farmers, builders, merchants, artists, healers and craftspeople as well as exceptional scholars; magical aptitude did not make every Kel'vari a professional mage. Its cities depended on sophisticated but finite magical infrastructure and ordinary work.

The **First Anchor began as a legitimate, publicly supported and authorised project** intended to establish a stable connection with the Kel'vari's creator, Ascendant. Appropriate federal and scholarly authorities approved that legitimate objective. The five celebrated principal researchers — Vaer'uun, Thes'var, Su'vareth, Oma'vira and Khor'vun — exploited divided oversight and scholarly trust to secretly alter sensitive workings toward a breach of the Veil under Nhal's subtle influence. They knowingly pursued the alien intelligence but **Nhal concealed the full destructive consequences** from them. Other Kel'vari investigators noticed discrepancies, preserved evidence and later helped found the multiracial Guardians during the Divine War. The broader Kel'vari population and government were not collectively complicit. Specific institutional titles, legal procedures, political leaders, project milestones and the five scholars' exact knowledge remain to be developed.

## 2026-10-13 creator LOCK — Oth'varen, the Sixth Scholar and Last Witness

**[[Oth'varen]]** is the original Kel'vari name of **the Sixth Scholar**, later known as **the Last Witness**. He was the sixth principal scholar of the First Anchor project, responsible for independent verification of six-field stability and a former trusted colleague and friend of the five who became the Corrupted Conclave. He identified concealed modifications and pleaded with them to halt the activation while present in its central chamber. Nhal deliberately tried to corrupt him during the breach as it transformed the other five, seeking to claim the leading opponent of its influence. Oth'varen's extraordinary will and resistance prevented the corruption of his allegiance, though primordial energies still transformed him: he gained extraordinary longevity and magical capacity, remained killable, and carries an enduring pull toward Nhal that he must resist. He is the principal surviving **non-aligned-to-Nhal** member of the original six and a firsthand witness to the activation, not an omniscient source of primordial truth.

During the Divine War he became a principal founding member of the **multiracial Guardians**, alongside other surviving Kel'vari investigators and trusted members of the six older races. The Guardians are not his personal monarchy, and knowledge that he still lives is restricted. He can become a central modern source of firsthand testimony and a recurring figure in the Guardian/Unmade story. His exact abilities, limitations, public identity and encounter sequence remain open. The five Conclave founders retain their existing locked names and roles.

## 2026-10-13 creator LOCK — the Six Reunited, progressive corruption and chronological narration

After The Binding, Oth'varen confronts all five original Corrupted Conclave scholars at a ruined Kel'vari research sanctuary within the Shroud. They are no longer reasonable colleagues capable of a calm reconciliation: years of Nhal's subtle influence before the First Anchor and their deliberate transformation during its breach have progressively warped their logic, ambitions and loyalties. Vaer'uun rationalises transcendence through destruction; Thes'var treats the catastrophe as a technical failure; Su'vareth justifies deception as necessary; Oma'vira interprets corruption as enlightenment; Khor'vun has twisted protection into defence of the Conclave against its victims. The five seek to draw Oth'varen into their shared transformed resonance. His own lingering corruption responds, but he painfully resists and escapes after disrupting the sanctuary's wards. This is **not** fresh direct communication or an order from Nhal: reliable contact was lost after the catastrophe and Binding. The five remain formidable, intelligent, dangerous and ideologically corrupted. Oth'varen remains vulnerable to the pull without surrendering his will.

**Narrative rule:** tell these events chronologically. In pre-activation scenes use the six scholars' personal names, never future-facing labels such as 'First Voice-to-be'; titles and the Corrupted Conclave are introduced only after their actual formation. The expanded confrontation dialogue is approved as the intended dramatic direction; the Chronicle currently contains a condensed adaptation.

## 2026-10-14 creator LOCK — Nhal's voice between the rupture and Binding

Before the First Anchor breached the Veil, Nhal could only subtly influence particular six-field experimental resonances, not speak directly. **Between the rupture and The Binding**, the open breach and primordial corruption allowed Nhal to communicate selectively through thoughts, whispers, guiding impressions, brief commands and seemingly spontaneous knowledge, especially to the five transformed willing scholars. The communication was neither continuous nor absolute mind control; other willing Unmade followers received weaker, inconsistent guidance near corrupted workings. This enabled coordination and deeper ideological corruption during the Divine War. **Oth'varen** occasionally perceived the same presence through his transformed nature but resisted it and did not accept its authority.

**The Binding immediately severed dependable communication**, both within and beyond the Shroud. The five experienced the sudden loss of the voice; attempts to restore contact failed. Their corrupted beliefs and transformed abilities endured, as did the primordial pressure near the First Anchor, but pressure was not direct instruction. Oth'varen experienced relief without losing the persistent corruption within him. The resulting prolonged silence later became the Age of Silence. Nhal's selective communication resumes only in the modern magical awakening. Avoid portraying Nhal as an ordinary conversational god or implying it controlled all minds.

## 2026-10-14 creator LOCK — Anchor network, awakening and resonance inversion

The Conclave developed lesser Anchor technology across the **Age of Silence**, without reliable guidance from Nhal: **Minor Resonators** first proved that faint resonance with the original breach could be produced from afar; generations of refinement yielded **Regional Anchors** linking effects across wider areas; finally **Major Anchors** enabled synchronisation of multiple Regional Anchors. The first Major Anchor alone did not cause the awakening. Only when sufficient linked installations crossed a **cumulative resonance threshold** did their combined pressure weaken the Shroud enough for **Nhal's selective whispers to return**. The same network altered the **distribution/accessibility of existing primordial magical energy** across Theia, causing beneficial and dangerous magical awakening effects. It did not create energy, and Nhal is not the source of the world's magic. The Conclave's goal was restored contact and eventual escape, not universally improved magical ability. The Shattered Delegation remains an independently orchestrated Conclave operation **before** Nhal's renewed communication.

**Resonance inversion is a locked metaphysical possibility, not yet a completed historical achievement.** A properly reconfigured lesser Anchor could potentially oppose the destabilising resonance and **strengthen the Shroud**, while preserving at least some beneficial ambient-magic redistribution. This is not a trivial polarity switch: it requires difficult six-field reconstruction and safe handling of supporting links, increasingly challenging from Minor to Regional to Major. Oth'varen's stability expertise and Guardian records could contribute to discovery. Inversion may lessen the Seven's burden but is **not guaranteed to free them or permanently replace their essences**. Whether, when, how, and at what cost the player-facing discovery succeeds remains open. Destruction remains possible but may sacrifice local benefits; inversion offers a more demanding strategic alternative. The network can continue to be built or expanded in the modern story, and disabling one Major Anchor does not automatically end the entire awakening or silence Nhal.

## 2026-10-15 creator LOCK — Unmade hostility, corruption and conditioning

**The Unmade's hostile forces have three overlapping origins, not three new races or exclusive factions:** (1) **Willing** followers who knowingly embrace Nhal's purpose for belief, ambition, knowledge or power, and fight deliberately for Unmade objectives; (2) **Corrupted** mortals, wildlife or other beings whose prolonged exposure to unstable primordial influence has altered magical structure, perception, cognition, instincts or bodies, sometimes producing dangerous aggression without willing allegiance; (3) **Bound** soldiers, captives, creatures and constructs conditioned or constrained through Unmade Mind workings, rituals, altered six-field structures or enforced instructions. A willing follower may become corrupted; a corrupted victim may be bound. Some constructs also continue ancient defensive duties without ever serving Nhal.

**Exposure is not automatic allegiance or guaranteed hostility.** Outcomes vary from temporary symptoms through persistent influence and transformation to profound loss of control. Corruption is not universal mind control; **Oth'varen** is the central example of resistance. Many Kel'vari and transformed inhabitants of the Shroud are innocent or non-hostile. Not every hostile being can be rescued during an attack, but combat, containment, rescue and breaking compulsions should remain distinct possibilities. Avoid making all corrupted inhabitants or all Kel'vari enemies.

**Oma'vira, the Harbinger**, originally a scholar of Mind, consciousness and perception, is the Conclave's leading authority on devotion, mental influence and magical conditioning. Her centuries of work support recruitment and the transformation of willing recruits or unwilling captives into dangerous conditioned forces. Her teachings alone do not explain every hostile NPC. **Khor'vun, the Warden**, commands and deploys organised defensive forces and protects Anchor approaches and Unmade strongholds. He may favour disciplined fighters over unstable conditioned troops, creating a potential methodological tension with Oma'vira. The other founders provide doctrine, research, covert logistics and strategic purpose. Outside the Shroud, players mainly encounter organised operatives, hidden facilities, Anchor defenders and local corruption; within it, older corruption, transformed victims, wildlife, ancient constructs and enduring strongholds produce a distinct ecology of threats.

This is a **locked world and MMO combat foundation**, not approval of particular enemy designs, irreversible corruption mechanics, specific rescue outcomes, or the full previously proposed Oma'vira eight-milestone character arc. Develop those separately.
