## The human through-line

The player begins by making a dangerous road safe for neighbours. By sixty, they have learned how much a ruler, a scholar or a soldier can hide behind the claim of protection. The question is practical: **who can be trusted with a dangerous truth, and what makes that trust deserved?** The answer develops through witnessed actions, independent evidence and shared responsibility. The player earns standing through service, never a prophecy, special ancestry or immunity to corruption.

Elara teaches responsibility for people the player can name. Mara teaches that confidence is not corroboration. Aldren must distinguish loyalty to a friend from loyalty to a comfortable account of his death. Garrick learns to adjust military plans without abandoning legitimate defence. Corven must make decisions without treating four sovereign peoples as stores of interchangeable resources. [[Oth'varen]] must let others preserve knowledge he can no longer safely keep alone.

The war remains real. [[Dominion]] officers have understandable motives and conduct hostile operations; discovering the [[The Unmade|Unmade]] does not make those officers secretly friendly or innocent of every wartime action. [[Covenant]] also makes decisions with civilian costs. Neither alliance gains control of the other's campaign.

All place names introduced here are working names. The four allied racial capitals are Valecrown (human), Kar Durn (dwarf), Sylvaren ([[Aelari]]), Drom Kar ([[Korrath]]). Tidehold is the **separate coastal alliance capital**. A formal visit has a local problem, consequential assistance and an institutional decision; none is just a letter handoff. Physical distances and compass directions await a world map.

The story route is optional for ordinary levelling. Dependencies below concern knowledge and plot state, not permission to earn XP, learn professions or visit allied cities. Each act's climax has a solo story version; enemy-continent and Shroud objectives have PvE story routes. WvW, raids and hostile-player participation never supply an indispensable clue.

## Act I — The Crown and the Betrayal (1–20)

### 1. A road people depend on

At Millhaven, Brenna Holt's missing caravan means more than an unpaid invoice. [[Heddan Pike]] cannot finish farm tools without its iron stock; [[Sister Anwen]] is running short of clean supplies; [[Tomas Greymere]] is trying to meet a grain requisition without stripping families of seed. The village has been living with war for years. It has not been waiting for a cosmic revelation.

Elara asks for three ordinary acts of service before entrusting the caravan search: help [[Darian Cole]] secure a predator-haunted stretch of road, locate livestock scattered near a damaged crossing, and bring a delayed medical delivery to Anwen. Darian is eager to prove himself and initially mistakes speed for usefulness. The player succeeds by checking the route and helping people, not by outperforming him in a humiliation contest.

Tracks lead to a human outlaw camp near an abandoned waystation. The raiders take food like ordinary thieves, but someone pays unusually well for marked fittings, sealed cases and official correspondence. Their leader, [[Iven Rusk]], knows a buyer's instructions and meeting place, not the Unmade's purpose. The player breaks up the camp and recovers goods, survivors and a damaged document bearing a royal administrative seal.

Elara secures the document. She neither recognises a cult emblem nor declares the court corrupt. Brenna can show which goods are missing, Heddan can identify an unusual fitting, and the seal requires someone with authority to inspect it.

**Scene:** Darian asks whether this is finally a real military matter. Elara answers, “It was real when the medicine failed to arrive.” That lesson returns at the campaign's end.

### 2. What the receipts conceal

The search moves upriver to Stoneford, where Brenna's warehouse business connects the small crime to a regional economy. A local grain dispute remains exactly that: wartime requisitions and poor administration, without a hidden cult behind every shortfall. Helping resolve it wins cooperation from people who otherwise have little reason to trust another armed investigator.

The merchant [[Perrin Vale]] admits purchasing diverted material but claims legitimate subcontracting. The player compares his stock book with a courier's delivery docket and a warehouse tally. The books do not agree. Perrin has enriched himself and fears his anonymous employers, but his claims are neither all lies nor a substitute for evidence. He identifies a royal authorisation routed through a reorganised office.

A second cache contains a payment instruction whose office designation is recent, although its purported authority belongs to Edric Halewyn. Elara recognises the limits of her warrant. She sends the player with sealed evidence and a witness account to [[Mara Thorne]] in Valecrown. Brenna's deputy keeps her shop open while she gives testimony; the investigation does not remove essential services.

### 3. A king's account

Valecrown is a working city before it is a throne room. The player meets record clerks, craftspeople, soldiers and people who disagree about the war. Mara will not take a countryside allegation to the King without checking it. Archive clerk [[Lysa Marden]] provides dated office registers; a warehouse keeper confirms a consignment; a courier identifies a handover without claiming to know its final purpose.

Only then does Mara request an audience. Aldren speaks of Edric as a person whose objections he trusted, not a convenient dead adviser. Edric helped him support negotiations he already feared. The deaths and the apparent Dominion evidence hardened that fear into conviction.

Cassian offers access to files and a plausible explanation: enemy intelligence has reused captured credentials. His courtesy is useful, his documents partly genuine, and his account fits what the King already believes. Garrick's public opposition to peace makes him an easy suspect. Mara insists that disagreement with Edric is not evidence of murder.

The player investigates Cassian's suggested military lead. Garrick supplies authentic logs despite resenting the insinuation. Those logs place an authorisation before the supposed enemy request. They also establish that an office format attributed to Edric was introduced after his death. A witness recognises that a later interviewer suggested the identification they had repeated.

### 4. The office he wanted

Mara separates three propositions: Cassian used compromised administrative routes; he knowingly supplied delegation security information; he expected Edric's death to secure the office. None alone establishes everything that happened at the negotiations. Together, independent records, a surviving intermediary's testimony and authenticated payments establish his complicity. His own eventual admission corroborates rather than creates the case.

Aldren receives the evidence privately. Cassian calls it Dominion forgery, but cannot reconcile the dates or account for an authorisation kept outside his control. The King orders detention. Cassian flees into a covert transport passage beneath an administrative storehouse because he believes his contacts still owe him escape.

**Act dungeon: [[The Hidden Passage]].** Mara and royal guards enter with the player. Smugglers and hired fighters defend the outer route. Inside, disciplined operatives are burning records and preparing to kill Cassian. His promotion made him useful; his exposure makes him disposable. The player secures a witness alcove, interrupts the destruction of a dispatch ledger and defeats the extraction captain, [[Varric Sorn]]. Mara and the player capture Cassian alive. The essential ledger survives in a separately held copy if the combat encounter must be retried.

Cassian does not suddenly know [[Nhal]]. He believed he had bought a targeted killing from people whose larger design he did not understand. He remains culpable. Aldren removes him from office and places him under a lawful inquiry; there is no summary execution, pardon or reinstatement. The final sentence is not needed to close Act I.

The King commissions further investigation. The player returns to Elara with news that the village's losses helped expose a genuine betrayal. The food and road problems still need work. Becoming useful to the crown has not made home irrelevant.

**Knowledge at exit:** a culpable court collaborator and a wider unidentified network. No Conclave roster, Nhal, proven Dominion exoneration or Guardian initiation.

## Act II — Shadows of Conflict (20–30)

### 5. Obligations at the coast

Mara traces a shipping reference from the recovered ledger to Tidehold. Garrick needs reliable observers for an expedition on the Broken Coast, where Covenant already maintains a fortified landing on Dominion territory. The same voyage can serve a legitimate military mission and a separately controlled investigation. Neither officer treats the player as a disposable courier.

Corven's first audience is brief and difficult. The four peoples have different obligations, and the human King's commission cannot compel a dwarf hold or Aelari institution. Corven wants verifiable losses and a sustainable supply plan before approving reinforcements. The player helps harbourmaster [[Ysra Keel]] audit missing stores and stabilise a damaged provision depot. Some shortages are ordinary theft and weather losses. One chain of false requisitions matches the recent case.

The case points to replacement fittings purchased from Kar Durn, the dwarven capital. Garrick also needs those fittings to keep the landing's defences serviceable. The capital visit has two purposes that cannot be solved from a ship's manifest alone.

### 6. What the mountain holds

Engineer Brynja Flintwake's workshop is accused of supplying faulty parts. Her drawings show a military broker changed a specification after inspection. The player protects a mine-access route, retrieves a missing measuring assembly and helps identify who altered the order. A pressure failure in a feeder gallery was worsened by neglected maintenance, not caused by an Anchor. Dwarf law requires the hold's own officers to inspect the evidence.

The human–dwarf treaty becomes tangible: human grain supports the holds; dwarf skill keeps roads and defences working; neither partner can simply requisition the other's labour. Hold arbitrator [[Rurik Anvilmark]] authorises an audit after the player assists the affected workers. [[High Thane Bromund Stonevein]] appears in this draft using the archive's existing **working** name; the office and precise title remain review material. He permits a restricted commission, not unrestricted access to ancient vaults.

Brynja identifies the copied fitting as a component housing, not a siege upgrade. Tool wear links it to a consignment already sent overseas. She joins the expedition as a civilian engineer under an agreed contract. Dwarf vendors and trainers remain at home; a deputy maintains her workshop.

### 7. The enemy shore

At [[Northwatch Landing]], the Covenant expedition base on the Broken Coast, [[Commander Nessa Ward]] gives the player a clear military task: secure the route to a stranded supply party and discover how Dominion scouts predict landing movements. Darian now serves with a support detachment. His assignment follows training and consent, not an unexplained promotion.

The Dominion defence includes orc [[Captain Rukhan Graal]] and halfling quartermaster [[Nemi Brindlewick]]. Their patrols protect their own coast and supply routes. Neither is automatically Unmade. Rukhan's units attack an invasion force; Nemi is trying to keep a garrison supplied. The player sees an evacuation from a distance and can choose a route that avoids civilian buildings. No friendly quest giver appears inside the enemy town.

A captured depot record shows a private consignment was removed before a Covenant raid and charged as a war loss afterwards. Halfling-built hauling equipment has legitimate serial numbers; its tampering implicates a broker, not the halfling people. Investigating an abandoned coastal signal house leads to Guardian field contact [[Sera Venn]], outwardly a Covenant surveyor. She warns of unstable equipment without naming the whole order.

### 8. The signal below the fighting

Brynja compares the recovered housing with an installation beneath the signal house. An enigmatic specialist, using only the public designation **the Grey Surveyor**, advises Sera by a guarded exchange and later a brief meeting. He is Oth'varen; the player is not yet told this. He does not impersonate another species through a newly invented permanent magical disguise. A hooded appearance, limited access and an assumed occupational identity suffice; his unusual features can remain unexplained.

**Act dungeon: [[The Silent Signal]].** The player enters with Sera and Brynja. Hired guards defend a recently adapted service network; local creatures occupy undisturbed side chambers. Willing Unmade factor [[Halvek Dorn]] has installed a small resonance device and is testing its effects, concealing shipments behind military losses. Evidence of organised hidden activity is now direct. Halvek's operational orders mention the Veiled Hand's intermediaries, without revealing a primordial patron.

The player secures controls, prevents a specimen cache from being carried away and holds off Halvek while Brynja mechanically isolates the housing. The Grey Surveyor's written sequence permits safe shutdown of this modest working. NPCs perform separate tasks, not a joined six-mage ritual. The device is disabled, a few affected defenders can be freed from conditioning, and irreducibly hostile attackers can be fought.

Northwatch survives a conventional Dominion attack through its own defences and the player's earlier supply work. The hidden installation does not explain every tactical setback. Garrick reports a limited victory; he does not claim the coast conquered.

Back at Millhaven, Anwen's improved healing has genuinely helped patients, while a nearby old ward has become erratic. The player can repair the immediate hazard with Oren without being told all awakening effects are evil. Elara asks that the inquiry distinguish the people who need help from the thing causing harm.

**Knowledge at exit:** one deliberately operated device, a recurring six-field arrangement, covert intervention and a Guardian contact whose full institution remains undisclosed. The awakening is not yet conclusively explained worldwide.

## Act III — The Hidden Design (30–40)

### 9. A forest is not a specimen

The Silent Signal records describe a biological stabilising material. Mara's inquiry reaches Sylvaren, where Aelari wardkeeper [[Caelith Rowan]] has already challenged the use of harvested living material in military equipment. [[Queen Seralyth]] allows an investigation only after the player helps affected ward-tenders and honours restrictions protecting inhabited woodland.

The forest's danger is not one great conspiracy. Territorial predators remain predators; a neglected branch-ward endangers travellers; a legal dispute concerns how much material the alliance may take. The player restores a nursery route and rescues workers before studying a deliberately altered ward on an abandoned research boundary. Caelith's former assistant, [[Veylan Istren]], has sold restricted observations to an intermediary. He rationalises the sale as breaking human military and royal monopolies; his political criticism does not justify his knowing concealment of a dangerous working.

Seralyth distinguishes inquiry from unrestricted access. She authorises Caelith to compare the recovered pattern and share a limited sample. A historical Aelari account disputes a [[Vaelari]] interpretation, but does not prove the exact cause of the elven schism or establish a present friendship with Selrion.

### 10. A second crossing

Veylan's correspondence points to a relay cache near Dominion's coastal expedition routes. The player sails again from Tidehold through Northwatch, then joins a small Covenant reconnaissance party at an accessible coastal pocket. This is a second expedition, not a teleport into an enemy capital or an unexplained army inland.

Vaelari researcher [[Ilyss Varen]] is studying field instability under a legitimate Dominion military contract. Her notes reject a broker's attempt to substitute unstable components. The player obtains a separately stored inspection copy from an abandoned observation post after fighting its military escort or using a PvE stealth route. Ilyss does not become a friendly companion; her recorded objections are useful evidence. A military commander would still regard the player as an intruder.

An Unmade courier tries to remove both her objections and Veylan's procurement chain. Recovering the dispatch reveals that someone is suppressing accurate findings on both continents. Mara can establish a common operation, not that every academy is infiltrated. The player brings back observations that require the Korrath's long-maintained ward histories to interpret.

### 11. What the elders did not copy

At Drom Kar, [[First Elder Dromak]] receives the player alongside chronicle-custodian [[Urodan]] and Passage surveyor [[Tarek Oss]]. The capital has survived since the ancient age; that survival has required repair, adaptation and decisions about what to preserve. It is not a pristine store containing every answer.

The player helps restore a highland water route and recover records from a store whose structural supports remain unsafe. Old damage and local cave fauna explain those hazards. Urodan discovers a modern copy omitted a warning appended by an earlier custodian. The omission arose from institutional caution, not proof that Dromak personally fabricated history. Restoring the reference gives the player a partial account of Kel'vari investigators and a containment called [[The Binding]].

Sera now acknowledges the [[The Guardians|Guardians]] privately. She can show evidence of their multiracial origins during the [[Divine War]], before The Binding, but admits her cell had no reliable knowledge that Kel'vari communities still live inside. The player is an entrusted associate, not automatically an oath-bound recruit. Dromak permits a small, supervised investigation of an outer Passage site. The sovereign council retains authority over its records.

The Grey Surveyor recognises the warning too quickly and initially withholds the reason. Sera challenges his authority to refuse a field team information it needs to survive. He provides a stability sequence but not yet his identity.

### 12. The price of an incomplete warning

Measurements from the three allied capitals and overseas show linked devices feeding a Regional Anchor at an old freight works below the highland approach. The structure is not the First Anchor. Guardian custodian Halden Marr's cell prepares to destroy it using a preserved instruction stripped of its supporting diagrams. His fear is justified; his interpretation is not.

**Act dungeon: [[The Broken Measure]].** The player halts the premature attempt, rescues the exposed team and secures three support stations. Halden initially argues; he changes course when the omitted measurements reproduce a dangerous surge. Aelari, dwarf and Korrath expertise supports separate observations, extraction and physical isolation. The Grey Surveyor individually handles the final combined working he is qualified to understand. The player decides the safe sequence under attack and keeps the retreat available.

The local Unmade overseer escapes only if the player must abandon pursuit to save the team; the standard proposed outcome captures the overseer's records and defeats them without assigning a founder's death. The installation is isolated and its external links stopped. A nearby low-capacity Passage connection remains usable because it is an older physical transit system, not powered by this Regional Anchor.

The Grey Surveyor admits he helped establish the restrictions Halden inherited. He authorises copies of the missing safety material to all members of this expedition. This is his first concrete change, not an instant reform of the order.

The Passage can carry a small reconnaissance group into the Shroud. It has been weakly operational for centuries; the discovery is of a route and a safe procedure, not a once-in-a-century alignment. Increased expedition throughput remains a later engineering problem. The act closes with a real local victory and an informed invitation to enter the frontier.

**Knowledge at exit:** Guardians, the Kel'vari's historical existence, the First Anchor as a different ancient catastrophe, The Binding as containment and Regional Anchor interdependence. Keeper's knowing concealment, [[Aethyr]] and Nhal's full nature remain unknown.

## Act IV — Echoes of the Sundering (40–50)

### 13. Beyond the boundary

Corven approves a limited Covenant reconnaissance mission after receiving the Korrath survey and guarantees of an extraction plan. Aldren supplies human personnel but cannot authorise the other sovereign peoples' participation. Garrick assigns Nessa to a small frontier post; Elara stays responsible for the Heartlands. Supplies enter in modest consignments. A great army does not appear through a weak gate.

The first Shroud landing, [[Lantern Redoubt]], occupies a surveyed outer ruin with permission from a nearby independent Kel'vari community connected to [[Thuma'rei]]. Gate steward **[[Aru'ven]]** negotiates maintenance and boundary terms for this site alone; they do not speak for all five enclaves. [[Oth'vaeri]] documents are translated by people with relevant training. The player cannot suddenly read the language because of ancestry or quest progress.

The first encounter with living Kel'vari is a repair and rescue problem. A water run has failed; transformed scavengers and an ancient defensive construct threaten maintenance workers. Some affected inhabitants retreat when the danger passes. Some constructs defend an old remit unrelated to the Unmade. The player assists before requesting access to a deeper route.

A separate mission tours the supply depot, an observation ridge and an NPC exercise ground. It explains the dedicated WvW frontier's territorial stakes: supply, overextension, ownership and contested routes. A Dominion expedition is visible across the frontier, with its own approach and interests. The player may join WvW or complete the tutorial through the NPC exercise. No player kill, territorial capture or siege attendance is required.

### 14. People left outside the report

The sight of living Kel'vari changes the investigation. Urodan wants accurate copies; Caelith wants to prevent an alliance expedition from exploiting survivors; Brynja asks how much maintenance the gate can bear. Aru'ven demands that access agreements have limits. They have lived here for generations and are not a grateful remnant waiting to be ruled.

At a protected archive approach associated with [[Oth'irun]], archivist **[[Sel'ora]]** allows inspection of a narrow set of records after the player helps recover a missing copyist. The record names six principal researchers, including an independent stability verifier. An image and a personal verification sequence match the Grey Surveyor. Sel'ora does not donate the enclave's whole archive or become an omniscient historian.

Confronted privately, Oth'varen gives his name. He identifies his former colleagues and describes what he witnessed at activation: concealed alterations, a refusal to halt and a catastrophe whose full scale he did not predict. The player can demand corroboration. His testimony is compared with independent records before Mara relies on it. He acknowledges his transformed state and the corruption he still resists. He cannot tell the player everything the Seven thought or what existed before their awakening.

Su'vareth's threatened exposure of a field cache forces a hard immediate choice: preserve a convenient secret or evacuate the people at risk. Oth'varen releases the restricted route map to the small authorised team so it can rescue the custodians. Aru'ven still forbids its public circulation. Accountable disclosure does not mean publishing vulnerable enclave locations to the world.

### 15. A third enemy coast

A captured Shroud dispatch links a new control assembly to a Dominion storm-coast depot. Before leaving, the player returns to the Heartlands: Darian's support unit has wounded returning home; Tomas is resisting a requisition that would consume winter stores. Aldren accepts a revised levy after verified need, making the player's home experience change an actual decision. Elara will train a limited relief detachment, not empty the village.

The third Dominion expedition travels by Covenant ships from Tidehold to an existing coastal staging anchorage beyond Northwatch. A small launch reaches an abandoned landing site within operational range. [[Vorrath]] warden Shorvek Tal is defending sacred coastal ground from intruders and from a concealed theft. The player meets his patrols as enemies. A route avoids occupied shrines and civilian pilgrimage groups; his records can be recovered from an Unmade cache without asking him for help.

An Unmade shipping agent, [[Maelin Voss]], has attributed stolen ward components to Covenant raids. Shorvek's requisition objections, the depot tally and Voss's coded receipt establish diversion. They do not prove a definitive version of the Vorrath exile or reveal that their faith is inherently corrupt. The military objective remains a captured Covenant survey team, which the player rescues before pursuing the hidden consignment.

### 16. Names beneath the accusation

The new assembly is bound for a Shroud research station outside the protected enclave approaches. It also carries a damaged but authentic transaction archive: Voss's chain inherited the same security-reconnaissance subcontract that enabled the [[Shattered Delegation]] attack. Mara must compare it with Cassian's case, not simply accept an enemy document.

**Act dungeon: [[The Witness Vault]].** At the outer research station, willing Unmade commandant [[Teren Malrec]] uses conditioning procedures derived from the Harbinger's work. Captives include people taken from several regions; they are not automatically friendly soldiers of the opposing alliance. The player can release noncombatants through a separate exit and break feasible compulsions without requiring all attackers to be rescued mid-fight.

[[Oma'vira]] intervenes through an installation-local prepared projection, a **proposed** Mind/Form technique with a finite range, not global telepathy. It can be interrupted. She recognises Oth'varen and provokes his lingering corruption. He withdraws from the active focus, asks the player to execute the previously taught isolation sequence and chooses resistance while the player protects the others. She does not possess him, cleanse him or become a defeated raid boss.

The player defeats Malrec, isolates the conditioning focus and secures the transaction archive. The corroborated chain now connects Cassian's payment and security leak, the attack's operational preparations, false attribution instructions and the Veiled Hand's network. Oth'varen separately identifies [[Su'vareth]] from the archive's signature and earlier records. No single confession or magical memory supplies the whole proof.

Mara reports to Aldren and Corven. The investigation can now confidently attribute the Shattered Delegation's orchestration to the Unmade. Aldren accepts that his strongest account of Dominion authorship was manipulated. This does not absolve every participant of each wartime crime or give Covenant a reliable diplomatic channel. The King orders the false attribution removed from his own case records and reviews resulting prosecutions; he does not announce peace unilaterally.

Cassian is confronted with the corroborated record in lawful custody. He admits he chose not to ask what the intermediaries intended beyond Edric's murder. His ignorance is narrower than innocence. His investigative role ends here: he is deposed, the prosecution has independent evidence, and the wider case no longer depends on his cooperation. His trial reaches its conclusion in the Act V epilogue.

**Knowledge at exit:** living independent Kel'vari; Oth'varen's firsthand identity and limits; the five scholars' names disclosed through an earned historical comparison; the Unmade's Delegation operation. Nhal is at most the network's unidentified beyond-boundary patron until Act V establishes more.

## Act V — The Severed Chain (50–60)

### 17. What the enemy has built

Evidence from all preceding sites shows a new Major Anchor connection at the outer Shroud complex called the [[Chainworks]]. It would synchronise a growing branch of Regional Anchors near the expedition approaches, increasing pressure on containment and enabling a stronger local channel. This is an expansion of a network already sufficient for selective whispers, not the first Major Anchor suddenly awakening the world.

At Tidehold, Corven convenes authorised delegates from the four Covenant peoples. Mara presents a redacted operational account; Oth'varen gives restricted technical testimony to cleared specialists. Neither the Guardians nor enclave locations become public institutions. Corven appoints a limited expedition with named scope, supply limits and review duties. Aldren provides his own commitment as the human King, without becoming High Ruler.

Separate recorded observations establish that the devices redistribute existing energy and that the network has enabled intermittent communication with an intelligence called **Nhal** beyond the Veil. The player learns the name, hostile purpose and confinement, not the complete primordial Aethyr narrative. The Shroud contains the First Anchor's breach; Passage travel crosses physical space. None of the modern devices is the original wound.

The named founders' functions now fit observed evidence: Su'vareth's logistics and political interference, Thes'var's engineered patterns, Oma'vira's conditioning, Khor'vun's layered defence and Vaer'uun's coordinating directives. Their limits become as important as their reach. Voss diverted real supplies, not every ship. Malrec conditioned a finite group, not every soldier. The player has stopped identifiable undertakings, not explained the entire world away.

### 18. Returning is part of the commission

Before the final expedition, the player completes a home-region relief circuit. The crossing repaired at the start now carries wounded and winter goods; Brenna organises a route through allied territories; Heddan repairs returned equipment; Anwen teaches safe limits to newly capable healers; Tomas chairs a dispute with facts the player helped gather. Darian volunteers for the final operation as a trained rescue and extraction soldier. He has become more useful by becoming less reckless.

Aldren visits the investigative office rather than summoning everyone to a celebration. He reads one of Edric's earlier arguments for verification and authorises restoration of records damaged by Cassian's office. He cannot undo the lives lost to accusations. He can change how future accusations are handled. Mara accepts a continuing warrant with safeguards for independent archives and witnesses.

The fourth Dominion expedition returns through Northwatch into the Broken Coast's supported hinterland. Rukhan has strengthened the garrison; Nemi has repaired its supply accounting after earlier losses. The coast reflects previous events without changing into Covenant land. A legitimate Dominion counterattack can occur alongside the hidden operation.

The player targets a coastal Unmade relay and retrieves timing tables and a specialist assembly needed to identify the Chainworks' external support. They also rescue Darian's trapped extraction team. Rukhan's troops remain a military danger; Nemi's records distinguish an Unmade requisition from ordinary garrison supply. The team withdraws by sea rather than conquering an enemy capital.

### 19. A purpose a structure was not built to serve

Oth'varen proposes inversion after studying stabilisation principles and modern designs together. He offers a hypothesis, not an ancient switch everyone forgot. A small isolated Minor Resonator at the Covenant test station provides the first controlled trial. Brynja measures mechanical strain, Caelith watches independent biological indicators, Tarek surveys spatial stability and the player manages physical isolation and the emergency cutoff. Each performs their own work. Oth'varen alone reconstructs the combined six-field working within his competence.

The trial produces a small, short-lived counter-resonance and preserves one measured ambient benefit. It does not demonstrate unlimited scale, permanent safety or liberation of the Seven. Oth'varen exhausts his reserves and must rest. The team records the observed limit instead of presenting success as proof of everything it hopes.

Halden argues for safe decommissioning of the Chainworks. Sera supports a bounded test if the fallback remains viable. Both have defensible positions. The player may ask questions and choose which **final stabilisation procedure** to support after the dangerous links have been severed. No branch demands a chosen class, player crafting profession, special ancestry or PvP success.

[[Thes'var]] appears at an exposed observation station during preparations, protected by his own retreat route. He recognises Oth'varen's revised diagrams and understands the possibility. His objection is purpose: reinforcing containment would turn his life's work against the intelligence he serves. A brief confrontation ends with the player preserving measurements and his withdrawal to the Chainworks. He is formidable, finite and not invulnerable by fiat; the scenario presents a defensible withdrawal rather than an unexplained immunity phase.

### 20. The chain that can be severed

**Act dungeon: The Severed Chain.** The solo story operation targets one Major Anchor installation and its reachable support branch in the outer Shroud. It does not enter the First Anchor's central breach or conquer the region. Covenant's NPC crews stage independently of Dominion; there is no joint command or shared player group.

The opening task evacuates coerced labourers and cuts the external courier link identified overseas. Garrick's detachment secures approaches while Aru'ven enforces the agreed boundary protecting nearby residents. Nessa commands the field operation; Corven remains responsible for alliance commitments; Aldren does not replace either.

The player then disables three independently functioning support stations in a demonstrated safe sequence. Separate NPC crews hold already isolated stations; no simultaneous multiplayer interaction is required. Voss attempts to destroy the timing ledger, giving the player a final grounded confrontation with the human logistics chain. She is captured with her records in the proposed outcome. There is no sudden redemption or claim that she commanded Su'vareth.

[[Khor'vun]] directs the defence but does not become a required kill. His local commander, **[[Sareth Keld]]**, layers reinforcements and wards to protect the synchronising chamber. The player defeats Keld after making each defence support unavailable. A recorded directive from [[Vaer'uun]] orders preservation of the synchronising core while Thes'var attempts an emergency sequence that would abandon part of it to retain his research. The disagreement creates an exploitable timing gap; it does not turn either founder into an ally.

In the last chamber, Thes'var forces an early synchronisation and tries to recover his design materials. The player stops that immediate attempt, controls the chamber cutoff and keeps the evacuation corridor secure. Oth'varen identifies the reconstruction needed for a bounded inversion. The player now chooses between two procedures that both complete the full essential story:

**Safe severance.** The player commits the mechanical isolators in the established sequence. Oth'varen safely decommissions the core as a separate qualified working. The new branch ceases to strain containment, captives escape and the local offensive fails. Some direct benefits tied to this branch's operation diminish, requiring ordinary recovery work. The small inversion trial remains valid research for later campaigns. Oth'varen honours the player's decision to accept a known, limited protection.

**Bounded inversion.** The player preserves a measured subset of isolated supports for Oth'varen's reconstruction. The operation briefly produces a local counter-resonance while the player defends the emergency cutoff. When the measurements exceed the tested duration, the team deliberately severs the remaining link. The result is a documented, limited demonstration at greater scale, not permanent Major Anchor mastery. Some local redistributed benefit remains for observation; long-term effects are unproven. A combat failure is retryable; an unmanageable reading automatically routes to safe severance and still finishes the story.

The branch changes the case report, dialogue and a local follow-up, not the world's total magic supply or faction war. Both routes leave the installation isolated, stop its expansion and preserve copies of what was learned. Thes'var abandons the core and withdraws using his prepared route; no Conclave founder dies or is redeemed. Khor'vun's remaining forces retreat to their own strongholds. The broader network survives elsewhere and Nhal is not silenced universally.

At the final pressure point Oth'varen cannot personally manage the cutoff, protect the witnesses and save the research. He gives the player the complete operational instruction instead of asking for blind obedience. Darian and the extraction crew bring out the injured. The player makes and executes the decision; the ancient scholar does not finish the adventure on their behalf.

### 21. A commission brought home

The expedition returns with a limited victory it can substantiate. Corven acknowledges each people's contribution and confirms that the enclave agreement remains bounded. A permanent army-scale gate is not announced. Optional WvW continues on the frontier with territorial outcomes independent of story completion.

Aldren receives Mara's final human case: Cassian's culpability, the independent evidence of Unmade orchestration and the practical reforms already begun. The royal court concludes Cassian's trial, finds him guilty of conspiring in Edric's murder and knowingly compromising the delegation's security, and sentences him to permanent imprisonment in royal custody. This verdict and sentence are a **Needs Review proposal**, completing the disposition left open by the approved Act I document; the player does not act as judge or executioner. Aldren orders a public account of the human official's crimes and an appropriately redacted correction of the earlier attribution. It names the concealed criminal network where safe; it does not publish Guardian cells, protected witnesses or Kel'vari gate maps. The war continues because the evidence of sabotage does not resolve territory, ideology, prior violence or the lack of trust between alliances.

Mara's relationship with the player becomes a continuing investigative partnership rather than an endless demand to fetch another missing page. Garrick commends their judgement in protecting the retreat as much as their success in combat. Elara receives the player in the village militia yard, where a recruit is practising the same road patrol that began the story. Darian takes time to help that recruit check their kit before volunteering for anything grander.

Brenna's caravan leaves with ordinary tools, medicine and letters. Tomas has enough evidence to contest the next requisition. Anwen has better techniques and a reason to use them carefully. Oren has an approved copy of a warning he once could not interpret. The people have lives after the main quest.

Oth'varen gives Sera, Halden and the cleared technical team duplicate safety records and a duty to correct one another. His change is real and unfinished. He still carries corruption; the Guardians still have secrets; the enclaves remain independent. Their safer relationship is an earned outcome, not universal reconciliation.

**Final exchange:** Elara asks what the crown wanted in the end. The player can answer “Proof,” “Protection,” or “Someone to answer for it.” She replies, “Then keep asking for all three.” The player receives a human service commendation and the right to return to the ongoing world. No crown, divine mandate or alliance command is bestowed.

The human launch story is complete: the local theft became a lawful exposure of betrayal; the betrayer's wider network was traced and disrupted; the ruler corrected his account; home endured; the player exercised earned responsibility. Later campaigns inherit dangers and relationships, not an unfinished prerequisite for this ending.

## What this ending deliberately leaves for the saga

Nhal remains confined beyond the Veil, selectively communicative through surviving network branches. The First Anchor is not destroyed. Keeper's knowing concealment of the permanent cost of The Binding, the Seven's ultimate fates, Aethyr's full history, the identity of the one surviving great magical order and a permanent solution to containment remain unresolved. This draft does not select answers to the original unanswered Q436–444.

The other seven playable races retain their own substantial openings and racial conclusions. The connected material supplies what the human route needs without claiming those campaigns have been written or approved.
