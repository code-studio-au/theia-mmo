## 1. Creative pillars [CONFIRMED]

Theia is a large-scale high-fantasy MMORPG with layered, unreliable historical traditions, ancient lost magical achievement, living mortal civilizations, and a gradually reawakening existential danger. It should evoke the depth of an epic fantasy saga while remaining original.

The setting must be rewarding independent of the main quest. Launch progression aims at level 60 (**working target**), with a classic open-world MMO model: optional principal campaign chapters, racial starting arcs, substantial self-contained regional adventures, dungeons, professions, exploration, alliance war, and PvP. The entire saga should be planned as creator-facing history before detailed quests are produced. A satisfying launch finale should not disclose every cosmic mystery.

**Hard faction boundary:** [[Covenant]] and [[Dominion]] are two permanently hostile, politically separate playable alliances. A character's alliance membership is permanent. No ordinary cross-alliance citizenship, trade, friendly quests, public diplomacy, military service, or cooperative gameplay. Exceptions are *deeply clandestine* networks whose agents outwardly retain their native alliance affiliations. The rare historical peace negotiation was a failed exceptional attempt, **not** evidence of ordinary diplomacy.

## 2. Primordial cosmology [CONFIRMED foundation; expanded creator draft 2026-10-12]

**Primordial status:** [[Aethyr]] and Nhal were supreme primordial essences, categorically greater and more fundamental than the Seven. They existed **before space, time, realms or physical laws**. The later descriptions of their struggle lasting “eons” are figurative: time did not yet exist to measure it.

- **Aethyr** embodied the power and will of existence, creation, possibility and becoming.
- **Nhal** embodied negation and unmaking, the power and will to destroy what Aethyr sought to bring into existence.

Their opposed creative and destructive wills produced an immeasurable **stalemate**. Neither could overcome the other by direct force; while Nhal could reach Aethyr's creations, none could be assured of enduring.

**Aethyr's revelation and objective:** Instead of destroying Nhal, Aethyr conceived a means of **confining Nhal apart from creation**. It would establish two separate realms, place Nhal beyond the realm in which creation could develop, and maintain a boundary preventing unmaking from reaching across. The plan would require Aethyr's deliberate self-destruction.

**The [[First Sundering]]:** Aethyr shattered itself by choice. The resulting eruption of primordial energy **created both the Physical Realm and the Void**—neither realm existed before this event. The force drove Nhal into the newly created Void and Aethyr's shattered energies into the Physical Realm. In its final act Aethyr established the **Veil between those realms**, deliberately protecting the Physical Realm from Nhal's unmaking. The Physical Realm thereby acquired space, time, matter and fundamental magic. The Void is a realm of negation, **not** an ordinary physical world with its own preexisting geography. Nhal endured within it because of its primordial nature, but was confined by the Veil.

**Nhal's observation and the First Resonance [LATER CREATOR LOCK]:** The Veil confines Nhal's power, not its awareness. Nhal can **observe** the Physical Realm, the Seven, their relationships and mortal civilizations from the Void, but cannot directly interact, influence experiments, speak to mortals or alter creation. Observation is not omniscience or knowledge of the future. Nhal watched Ascendant's ambitious **independent** creation of the Kel'vari and recognized the potential of mortals who could combine all six fundamental magical fields at unprecedented depth. **Nhal did not help design, corrupt or empower the Kel'vari.** Their aptitude resulted from Ascendant's ingenuity and desire to outshine the other gods. The **First Resonance** occurred only when later exceptional Kel'vari researchers, unaware of the Veil or Nhal, accidentally combined the six fields in a configuration that disturbed the Veil without breaching it. **Only then** could Nhal exert minute influence on similar experiments, encouraging otherwise possible results and eventually steering researchers toward the First Anchor. The exact physical mechanism remains OPEN, but this sequence is LOCKED. It supersedes the earlier Q40 interpretation of Nhal's interference during creation.

**The Seven:** Seven independent equal divine consciousnesses subsequently awakened from Aethyr's shards within the Physical Realm. They were not Aethyr reborn as a single mind, did not inherit its full memories or understand the primordial sacrifice, and had no supreme ruler. [[Magic]] is part of the physical reality made possible by the First Sundering; the Seven learned its laws rather than inventing it.

**Status distinction:** The user's 2026-10-12 clarification explicitly establishes the *intentional imprisonment plan*, the *simultaneous creation of two realms*, Nhal's *forced displacement*, and the Veil's *deliberate creation as Aethyr's final protective act*. ## 4. Divine and mortal history

The Seven discovered magical laws and cooperated in creating mortal races. Multiple gods could combine power for very rare creative acts; this is a **divine** exception, not a general multi-player spellcasting rule.

The **Divine Age / Age of Wonders** featured extraordinarily sophisticated magical civilizations, several great independent magical orders, broad knowledge sharing, libraries, scholarly exchanges, and ancient Passage networks. Divine influence took the form of founding teachings, rare direct instruction and mortal intermediaries. Mortal scholars could openly challenge divine ideas.

The Seven's schism centred on Ascendant's secret creation of the extraordinarily magically capable **Kel'vari**, made using knowledge Keeper had only partly entrusted to him. Nhal subtly manipulated individuals and evidence, exploiting genuine values and mistrust. The [[Divine War]] then drew mortal civilizations into complex, changing conflicts; it **was not** simply modern Covenant versus Dominion.

**The Binding — CONFIRMED creator-facing truth (2026-10-12):** After the catastrophe surrounding the First Anchor and the ensuing divine conflict, the Seven join their divine essences to create and sustain **the Shroud**, containing the breach and the devastated Kel'vari's homeland. **Keeper knew with certainty before the ritual began that all Seven would become bound to maintaining the Shroud, unable to freely withdraw or resume their former independence. Keeper deliberately concealed this fact.** The others believed they were contributing a limited portion of their essence, not committing themselves to indefinite confinement. Their participation therefore lacked informed consent. The historical event is called **The Binding**; **Keeper's betrayal** is its hidden moral truth, not the event's public title. All seven essences and individual consciousnesses survive within the binding, preserving the possibility of a later **voluntary** reunification against Nhal. Whether that reunification occurs remains OPEN. The precise magical mechanism, dialogue, and Keeper's other alternatives remain subject to further development.

The original source decision that some gods “fell” should be understood in light of this later decision: they may have fallen from active freedom or influence, **not perished and lost their essences**.

The Divine War devastated ancient civilizations, destroyed or transformed many magical structures and passageways, triggered migrations, and reduced surviving magical knowledge. The two primary modern continental heartlands and central war-ravaged **Shrouded Lands** emerge from this history; the precise former single-landmass geography is **WORKING** rather than finally mapped.

Postwar societies preserved partial, conflicting accounts. Distinct ancient ancestry and altered religious patronage explain the eight modern civilizations. Different races preserved different fragments of magical knowledge.

## Anchor taxonomy and player destruction rules [LOCKED · 2026-10-12]

**First Anchor (ancient, unique):** Built by the Kel'vari's greatest researchers, believing it would create a lasting connection with divinity; Nhal's influence redirected its design. Activation established a persistent breach in Aethyr's **Veil**. The original structure became **entangled with the breach itself**. It is **not physically indestructible**: breaking it while the breach remains could remove the only remaining structure restraining uncontrolled expansion, potentially worsening the catastrophe. This is why the Seven contained the site through **The Binding** and the **Shroud** instead of simply destroying the First Anchor. The original site lies in the devastated Kel'vari's homeland beneath the Shroud.

**Modern Unmade Anchors (derivative, destructible):** [[Kel'vari]] civilization's complete designs, specialist institutions and practical knowledge were lost or isolated in the [[Second Sundering]]. The Unmade preserved fragments, diagrams, artifacts and partial procedures but cannot reliably reproduce the First Anchor. Their modern devices are **imperfect secondary resonators**, exploiting the **pre-existing damage and connection** associated with the First Anchor rather than opening an independent permanent Void breach each time. They can extend Nhal's influence locally, but their connections are limited and dependent on vulnerable physical/magical support structures. **Players can disable or destroy these devices**, sometimes only after disconnecting supports and stabilizing the site. A failed or reckless dismantling can cause a local hazard, but does not automatically repeat the Second Sundering.

**Escalation and gameplay:** (1) **Minor Resonators**: early-game local disturbances and corrupted creatures, typically destructible after defeating defenders; (2) **Regional Anchors**: mid-game installations in ruins, mines, temples or research bases, requiring safe disconnection of supports; (3) **Major Anchors**: rare late-game/dungeon/raid objectives assembled from stronger artifacts and sophisticated reconstructions, requiring staged stabilization and destruction. These are **narrative and progression categories**, not finalized numerical tuning, mandatory zone names or fixed instance counts.

**Strategic effect:** Minor devices create local influence; regional devices extend it; **only sophisticated coordinated networks of major Anchors** can substantially strain The Binding and threaten the Shroud. This supports geographically distributed Guardian/Unmade questlines and independent Covenant/Dominion campaigns on both continents without making every low-level device an extinction event. The Unmade seek to reconstruct enough ancient knowledge to coordinate a network, weaken the Shroud, access the First Anchor and ultimately allow Nhal's emergence. Guardians know why unsafe destruction is dangerous and can teach players to sever links before destroying Anchors. The First Anchor's final resolution and the Seven's possible reunification remain **OPEN endgame decisions**.

**Do not conflate:** First Anchor ≠ modern Anchor; the Veil ≠ the Shroud; local resonator destruction ≠ destroying the original breach; and destroying modern Anchors ≠ automatically destroying or freeing the Seven.

## The ancient Passage gates after The Binding [LOCKED · 2026-10-12]

**Continuous low-capacity operation is confirmed.** Before The Binding, the Kel'vari's homeland was a major hub in an ancient network of sophisticated **Passage** gates used for scholarship, commerce, travel and the movement of substantial groups and goods. The Binding and the Shroud drastically weakened the gates linked to the contained homeland, **but did not shut down every surviving gate**. A limited number of connections remained **continuously operational at low capacity** throughout the intervening centuries; they did **not** depend on rare alignments or periodically opening magical windows.

**Restrictions:** Surviving routes can support discreet travel by individuals or small groups and the movement of limited artifacts and supplies, but cannot sustain army-scale transfers, large military supply lines or open conquest. The network's useful destinations and throughput are greatly reduced, and its operation may be hazardous even though some routes remain continuously available. Passage transit is **between physical locations**; it is not itself a fresh breach of Aethyr's Veil or a means of transporting Nhal bodily into the Physical Realm. The Shroud still contains the First Anchor.

**Historical consequence:** The Unmade preserved knowledge of some surviving routes and exploited them to move operatives, instructions, artifacts and small consignments between the Shrouded Lands and sites on the Covenant and Dominion continents. This enabled long-term covert infiltration, not Unmade armies. Cells already outside the Shroud could also persist and recruit locally. The Unmade's loss of reliable direct communication with Nhal after The Binding is compatible with the gates' continued **physical transit**: access to a Passage gate does not equal a direct connection to the primordial intelligence.

**Modern consequence:** Awakening magic and renewed research may improve access and eventually enable Covenant and Dominion to **independently restore major gateways** for organized expeditions into the Shrouded Lands. These are distinct from conventional overseas shipborne military incursions, which remain part of the war on both continents. ## 5. Magic [CONFIRMED]

Fundamental magic permeates existence. A practitioner channels it through **intent and learned techniques**, spending a **finite personal/internal reserve** shared across disciplines. Natural aptitude, bloodlines and training affect potential, capacity and efficiency. Recovery via natural regeneration, ambient magic and trained meditation. Running out normally prevents casting safely; overreaching complex techniques risks loss of control and mental overload. Skill and precision matter more than raw reserve.

There are **exactly six provisional discipline names**:
1. **Elements & Energy:** elemental and energetic phenomena.
2. **Matter:** shaping/manipulating physical materials; permanent matter from nothing is a lost ancient art.
3. **Life:** biological, spiritual and natural effects; extraordinary resurrection may be possible under severe constraints (time, consent, body and cost).
4. **Form:** magical patterns, structures, conditions, wards and connections.
5. **Mind:** illusion, emotion and magical perception; no casual unrestricted mind control.
6. **Passage:** spatial transfer, links and travel; extensive long-distance teleportation mostly lost.

Basic effects tend to use one discipline; complex effects combine two; extraordinary ancient arts may combine more. **One individual must master all disciplines used in their working; ordinary cooperative multi-mage spellcasting is not available.** Permanent stable magical creation is particularly difficult/lost. Enchantments can be temporary, rechargeable or rarely self-sustaining; true self-sustaining techniques are largely lost.

Every race can eventually study every discipline. Racial/region/bloodline culture changes teachers, common applications, specialties and heritage affinities, **not** exclusive spell access. Conventional engineering and magic compete in society. Magic is widely available in major cities but less accessible in outlying settlements; medical traditions vary by civilization.

In armies, mages can be supports, integrated personnel and rarely exceptional battlefield powers. Martial elites are viable alongside magical elites. Anti-mage counterplay uses equipment, coordinated tactics and specialist formations. No class system, spell catalogue or combat numerical balance is final.

## 8. Great magical orders and ancient knowledge [CONFIRMED]

The Divine Age had **three or four** great magical orders, distinguished by **philosophy, specialization and purpose**. They were independent but interconnected, drew influences from **several** gods, and their scholars could openly challenge divine teachings. They were mostly cooperative until the Divine War, notwithstanding disputes over ethics, knowledge and political influence.

Great archives, exchanges and magical passageways shared knowledge. Surviving masters initially suppressed **specific dangerous advanced techniques**, not whole disciplines. Destruction of institutions, deaths of practitioners and suppression account for lost knowledge. Postwar survivors founded academies, royal institutions and smaller secret orders. Today's academy histories range from genuine continuity through fragments to later reconstruction. Academic rivalry is prominent.

**Exactly one original great magical order still secretly survives.** Which ancient order it was and its exact surviving knowledge are **OPEN** (earlier assistant guesses about a named Order of Preservation must not be silently locked).

Modern magic knowledge is fragmentary. Rediscovery is promoted by stronger wielders, reawakening sites and recovered teachings. Institutions are divided between progress and catastrophic risk.

## 2026-10-12 canon consolidation — Kel'vari, Anchor, Shroud and Guardians

**Later user decisions supersede older collaborative-creation descriptions.** Ascendant is the **sole divine creator** of the Kel'vari. After Keeper offered limited access to advanced stability research as an attempt to restore trust, Ascendant secretly reconstructed and extended restricted principles. The Kel'vari civilization before the Second Sundering was a **federation of largely autonomous cities**, with local civic councils, scholarly assemblies and a federal assembly coordinating intercity law, shared infrastructure, defence and consequential research. Its population included ordinary families, farmers, builders, merchants, artists, healers and craftspeople as well as exceptional scholars; magical aptitude did not make every Kel'vari a professional mage. Its cities depended on sophisticated but finite magical infrastructure and ordinary work.

The **First Anchor began as a legitimate, publicly supported and authorised project** intended to establish a stable connection with the Kel'vari's creator, Ascendant. Appropriate federal and scholarly authorities approved that legitimate objective. The five celebrated principal researchers — Vaer'uun, Thes'var, Su'vareth, Oma'vira and Khor'vun — exploited divided oversight and scholarly trust to secretly alter sensitive workings toward a breach of the Veil under Nhal's subtle influence. They knowingly pursued the alien intelligence but **Nhal concealed the full destructive consequences** from them. Other Kel'vari investigators noticed discrepancies, preserved evidence and later helped found the multiracial Guardians during the Divine War. The broader Kel'vari population and government were not collectively complicit. Specific institutional titles, legal procedures, political leaders, project milestones and the five scholars' exact knowledge remain to be developed.

## 2026-10-13 creator LOCK — Oth'varen, the Sixth Scholar and Last Witness

**[[Oth'varen]]** is the original Kel'vari name of **the Sixth Scholar**, later known as **the Last Witness**. He was the sixth principal scholar of the First Anchor project, responsible for independent verification of six-field stability and a former trusted colleague and friend of the five who became the Corrupted Conclave. He identified concealed modifications and pleaded with them to halt the activation while present in its central chamber. Nhal deliberately tried to corrupt him during the breach as it transformed the other five, seeking to claim the leading opponent of its influence. Oth'varen's extraordinary will and resistance prevented the corruption of his allegiance, though primordial energies still transformed him: he gained extraordinary longevity and magical capacity, remained killable, and carries an enduring pull toward Nhal that he must resist. He is the principal surviving **non-aligned-to-Nhal** member of the original six and a firsthand witness to the activation, not an omniscient source of primordial truth.

During the Divine War he became a principal founding member of the **multiracial Guardians**, alongside other surviving Kel'vari investigators and trusted members of the six older races. The Guardians are not his personal monarchy, and knowledge that he still lives is restricted. He can become a central modern source of firsthand testimony and a recurring figure in the Guardian/Unmade story. His exact abilities, limitations, public identity and encounter sequence remain open. The five Conclave founders retain their existing locked names and roles.

## 2026-10-13 creator LOCK — the Six Reunited, progressive corruption and chronological narration

After The Binding, Oth'varen confronts all five original Corrupted Conclave scholars at a ruined Kel'vari research sanctuary within the Shroud. They are no longer reasonable colleagues capable of a calm reconciliation: years of Nhal's subtle influence before the First Anchor and their deliberate transformation during its breach have progressively warped their logic, ambitions and loyalties. Vaer'uun rationalises transcendence through destruction; Thes'var treats the catastrophe as a technical failure; Su'vareth justifies deception as necessary; Oma'vira interprets corruption as enlightenment; Khor'vun has twisted protection into defence of the Conclave against its victims. The five seek to draw Oth'varen into their shared transformed resonance. His own lingering corruption responds, but he painfully resists and escapes after disrupting the sanctuary's wards. This is **not** fresh direct communication or an order from Nhal: reliable contact was lost after the catastrophe and Binding. The five remain formidable, intelligent, dangerous and ideologically corrupted. Oth'varen remains vulnerable to the pull without surrendering his will.

**Narrative rule:** tell these events chronologically. In pre-activation scenes use the six scholars' personal names, never future-facing labels such as 'First Voice-to-be'; titles and the Corrupted Conclave are introduced only after their actual formation. The expanded confrontation dialogue is approved as the intended dramatic direction; the Chronicle currently contains a condensed adaptation.

## 2026-10-14 creator LOCK — Nhal's voice between the rupture and Binding

Before the First Anchor breached the Veil, Nhal could only subtly influence particular six-field experimental resonances, not speak directly. **Between the rupture and The Binding**, the open breach and primordial corruption allowed Nhal to communicate selectively through thoughts, whispers, guiding impressions, brief commands and seemingly spontaneous knowledge, especially to the five transformed willing scholars. The communication was neither continuous nor absolute mind control; other willing Unmade followers received weaker, inconsistent guidance near corrupted workings. This enabled coordination and deeper ideological corruption during the Divine War. **Oth'varen** occasionally perceived the same presence through his transformed nature but resisted it and did not accept its authority.

**The Binding immediately severed dependable communication**, both within and beyond the Shroud. The five experienced the sudden loss of the voice; attempts to restore contact failed. Their corrupted beliefs and transformed abilities endured, as did the primordial pressure near the First Anchor, but pressure was not direct instruction. Oth'varen experienced relief without losing the persistent corruption within him. The resulting prolonged silence later became the Age of Silence. Nhal's selective communication resumes only in the modern magical awakening. Avoid portraying Nhal as an ordinary conversational god or implying it controlled all minds.

## 2026-10-14 creator LOCK — Anchor network, awakening and resonance inversion

The Conclave developed lesser Anchor technology across the **Age of Silence**, without reliable guidance from Nhal: **Minor Resonators** first proved that faint resonance with the original breach could be produced from afar; generations of refinement yielded **Regional Anchors** linking effects across wider areas; finally **Major Anchors** enabled synchronisation of multiple Regional Anchors. The first Major Anchor alone did not cause the awakening. Only when sufficient linked installations crossed a **cumulative resonance threshold** did their combined pressure weaken the Shroud enough for **Nhal's selective whispers to return**. The same network altered the **distribution/accessibility of existing primordial magical energy** across Theia, causing beneficial and dangerous magical awakening effects. It did not create energy, and Nhal is not the source of the world's magic. The Conclave's goal was restored contact and eventual escape, not universally improved magical ability. The Shattered Delegation remains an independently orchestrated Conclave operation **before** Nhal's renewed communication.

**Resonance inversion is a locked metaphysical possibility, not yet a completed historical achievement.** A properly reconfigured lesser Anchor could potentially oppose the destabilising resonance and **strengthen the Shroud**, while preserving at least some beneficial ambient-magic redistribution. This is not a trivial polarity switch: it requires difficult six-field reconstruction and safe handling of supporting links, increasingly challenging from Minor to Regional to Major. Oth'varen's stability expertise and Guardian records could contribute to discovery. Inversion may lessen the Seven's burden but is **not guaranteed to free them or permanently replace their essences**. Whether, when, how, and at what cost the player-facing discovery succeeds remains open. Destruction remains possible but may sacrifice local benefits; inversion offers a more demanding strategic alternative. The network can continue to be built or expanded in the modern story, and disabling one Major Anchor does not automatically end the entire awakening or silence Nhal.

## 2026-10-15 creator LOCK — Unmade hostility, corruption and conditioning

**The Unmade's hostile forces have three overlapping origins, not three new races or exclusive factions:** (1) **Willing** followers who knowingly embrace Nhal's purpose for belief, ambition, knowledge or power, and fight deliberately for Unmade objectives; (2) **Corrupted** mortals, wildlife or other beings whose prolonged exposure to unstable primordial influence has altered magical structure, perception, cognition, instincts or bodies, sometimes producing dangerous aggression without willing allegiance; (3) **Bound** soldiers, captives, creatures and constructs conditioned or constrained through Unmade Mind workings, rituals, altered six-field structures or enforced instructions. A willing follower may become corrupted; a corrupted victim may be bound. Some constructs also continue ancient defensive duties without ever serving Nhal.

**Exposure is not automatic allegiance or guaranteed hostility.** Outcomes vary from temporary symptoms through persistent influence and transformation to profound loss of control. Corruption is not universal mind control; **Oth'varen** is the central example of resistance. Many Kel'vari and transformed inhabitants of the Shroud are innocent or non-hostile. Not every hostile being can be rescued during an attack, but combat, containment, rescue and breaking compulsions should remain distinct possibilities. Avoid making all corrupted inhabitants or all Kel'vari enemies.

**Oma'vira, the Harbinger**, originally a scholar of Mind, consciousness and perception, is the Conclave's leading authority on devotion, mental influence and magical conditioning. Her centuries of work support recruitment and the transformation of willing recruits or unwilling captives into dangerous conditioned forces. Her teachings alone do not explain every hostile NPC. **Khor'vun, the Warden**, commands and deploys organised defensive forces and protects Anchor approaches and Unmade strongholds. He may favour disciplined fighters over unstable conditioned troops, creating a potential methodological tension with Oma'vira. The other founders provide doctrine, research, covert logistics and strategic purpose. Outside the Shroud, players mainly encounter organised operatives, hidden facilities, Anchor defenders and local corruption; within it, older corruption, transformed victims, wildlife, ancient constructs and enduring strongholds produce a distinct ecology of threats.

This is a **locked world and MMO combat foundation**, not approval of particular enemy designs, irreversible corruption mechanics, specific rescue outcomes, or the full previously proposed Oma'vira eight-milestone character arc. Develop those separately.

## Creator-approved zone population principles (15 October 2026)

Every levelling zone supports independent combat, exploration and gathering without requiring the main Unmade storyline. NPC dispositions include friendly/protected, neutral attackable (yellow), and aggressive (red); these are behavioural categories rather than moral judgements.

Humanoid populations must fit local geography, ancestry, culture and politics. Foreign military groups require an explanation such as a coastal incursion or established outpost. Local outlaw or renegade groups are possible but not mandatory in every zone. Political disagreement alone does not make an entire clan or people hostile. Wildlife, territorial creatures and environmental hazards provide ordinary combat independent of the Unmade.

Unmade activity is uneven and grounded in actual research, infiltration, supply and Anchor operations. Early areas may have none; the Shroud contains a distinct mixture of ancient threats, independent Kel'vari communities and Unmade strongholds. Exact enemy species, locations, levels and encounter mechanics remain open.
