# THEIA — ZONE POPULATION AND ENCOUNTER FRAMEWORK

**Status:** Working design proposal, 15 October 2026. The geographic, cultural and independent-levelling principles are creator-approved and recorded in the World Bible. Specific distributions, level bands, creatures, settlements, enemy groups and numbers below are **proposals, not locked canon**.

## Design objective

A player who ignores the main narrative must still find a functioning MMORPG world: native wildlife, neutral attackable creatures, aggressive threats, repeatable combat, exploration, gathering, local quests and occasional elites. [[The Unmade]] is a consequential hidden antagonist, **not the explanation for every hostile encounter**.

### Disposition and behaviour

- **Friendly/protected:** townspeople, traders, guards, allied institutions and protected civilians. PvP and opposing-alliance combat are governed separately.
- **Neutral/attackable (yellow):** ordinary fauna, some scavengers and independent creatures. Do not initiate combat under ordinary conditions; provocation, territory and specific mechanics can change that.
- **Hostile/aggressive (red):** predators, dangerous fauna, hostile outlaws, aggressive magical creatures, territorial defenders and purposeful enemy combatants. Aggression is not proof of moral guilt.
- **Dynamic disposition (proposal):** nesting, guarding young, proximity to protected sites, faction reputation or an environmental event may change response. Keep behaviour legible to players.

## Five checks before placing any humanoid enemy

1. **Origin:** Which people or culture do they belong to? Does their presence make sense here?
2. **Arrival:** Are they local, displaced, trading, raiding, invading, exiled or stationed here? How did they reach the location?
3. **Motivation:** What do they want: food, territory, artifacts, resources, political leverage, protection or a particular mission?
4. **Disposition:** Why do they attack this player rather than remain neutral? Political dissent alone is insufficient.
5. **Sustainability:** Where do they live, obtain supplies, recruit and retreat? A camp should not exist merely as a spawn cluster.

For example, human bandits in the human inland plains require little special explanation; a human military group deep in orc grasslands needs a supported route and objective. An overseas [[Covenant]] outpost on a [[Dominion]] coast can be justified by the established war, but its reach inland must follow logistics.

## Population layers

1. **Ambient life:** non-combat background inhabitants, herds, birds, insects, domestic animals and settlement activity.
2. **Ordinary neutral encounters:** huntable fauna, independent scavengers, local resource creatures and territorial wildlife outside defended areas.
3. **Ordinary aggressive encounters:** predators, dangerous cave inhabitants, hostile scavengers, local criminal groups and environmental hazards.
4. **Distinctive local threats:** ancient constructs, rogue magical workings, dangerous ruins, political outlaws or rare species fitting the region.
5. **Story-connected threats:** occasional Unmade operatives, conditioned defenders, corrupted wildlife near installations and evidence of Anchor operations.
6. **Elites and group objectives:** apex predators, leaders of justified hostile groups, ruin guardians, local magical anomalies and eventually significant Unmade commanders.

No fixed quota applies to any layer. Some starter zones can have no Unmade presence and no humanoid enemies.

**Political constraints:** The Aelari and Vaelari are separate civilizations, as are Korrath and Vorrath. Avoid treating those divisions as blanket permission for indiscriminate hostile populations. Orc Great Clan rivalry does not make whole clans default red NPCs. Halfling guild disputes do not automatically create hostile criminal armies.

## Zone progression model — illustrative, not final level bands

**Introductory area:** Safe settlements, neutral fauna and readable predators. Teach combat and gathering through local problems; avoid cosmic exposition.

**Early regional area:** Broader ecological range, local humanoid threats where credible, small ruins and optional elites. The Unmade may be absent or hidden behind an ordinary mystery.

**Developing region:** More dangerous predators, cave systems, territorial groups, old structures and some deliberately concealed Unmade operations where appropriate.

**Advanced region:** Larger military or magical threats, organised enemy camps and stronger elites. Minor Resonators or linked installations appear only in zones that logically contain them.

**High-level/endgame region:** Fortified Regional/Major Anchor operations, serious corrupted territories, elite Unmade forces and major encounters. The Shroud has its own ancient ecology and [[Kel'vari]] inhabitants; it is not one continuous hostile army camp.

## Independent levelling loop

- **Combat:** ordinary mobs grant useful experience and loot, independent of quest completion.
- **Exploration:** discover caves, routes, rare creatures, ruins, gathering sites and local events.
- **Professions:** materials and resources fit local habitats and industries.
- **Optional local quests:** clarify ecological or political context without forcing participation in the central story.
- **Story quests:** connect selected anomalies to Unmade activity through earned evidence.
- **Group play:** elite sites and local bosses provide optional cooperation.

Balance must avoid making main-story participation mandatory for ordinary progression, while still rewarding players who engage with narrative content. Respawn density, XP rates, rare drops and level scaling require playtesting.

## The Unmade placement test

Before placing Unmade enemies, specify: which Conclave operation is present; why this location matters; how agents, supplies or devices arrived; what they are protecting or acquiring; why exposure is worth the risk; and whether local populations are willing followers, manipulated intermediaries, corrupted victims or bound defenders.

**Outside the Shroud:** small cells, concealed facilities, compromised local institutions, covert supply routes and concentrated Anchor defences. Open armies are exceptional and require strategic reasons.

**Inside the Shroud:** longstanding strongholds, original research remnants, dangerous ancient constructs and corruption, alongside independent and potentially friendly Kel'vari enclaves. Some transformed residents are victims, not Unmade.

## Worked example: an orc grassland zone

A Great Clan settlement serves as a friendly hub. Nearby herds and scavengers are mostly neutral; predators and territorial beasts create predictable combat routes. A small band of dishonoured orc raiders threatens caravans for supplies, with its own camp, motive and local history. A cave contains dangerous creatures and a rare elite. Players can hunt, gather, explore and defeat raiders without hearing of [[Nhal]].

An optional investigation follows unusual thefts of particular metal components. Only after comparing shipments and discovering a hidden intermediary might players find an Unmade-linked cache. Its guards could be local willing recruits or conditioned defenders with a grounded reason for being there. No unexplained human bandit camp is needed.
