# THEIA — HUMAN HEARTLANDS: SETTLEMENT AND NPC REGISTER

**Status:** Creator-approved settlement-services framework and recurring Heartlands cast (15 October 2026). This register distinguishes locked roles from **working settlement names, precise locations, NPC inventories and quest implementation**, which remain open.

**Companion sources:** [Human Act I](THEIA_HUMAN_ACT_I_STORY.md), [Zone Population Framework](THEIA_ZONE_POPULATION_FRAMEWORK.md), [Modern Story Timeline](THEIA_MODERN_STORY_REVELATION_TIMELINE.md).

## Approved design principles

The human Act I world includes a small rural starting village, an Upper Heartlands market town and the inland human racial capital, with distinct services appropriate to settlement size. Every racial starting region must provide convenient access to basic equipment, repairs, food and drink, essential class training and introductory professions. Larger towns and capitals provide broader inventories, specialist trainers, crafting stations, storage, transport and trade. Basic services must not disappear or become inaccessible because of story phasing.

Existing characters should perform useful services when their established responsibilities fit. Not every vendor requires a large character arc, but named recurring NPCs should have consistent homes, professions, motives and appearances. The settlement economy must make sense independently of the [[The Unmade|Unmade]]. Human local criminals, wildlife, predators and old structures provide ordinary combat.

## Settlement A — Lower Heartlands starting village

**Working identity:** A productive agricultural village with farms, a small market, militia post, smithy, inn, healing house and surrounding grazing land. Suggested play band: 1–7. Exact name and map position are open.

| **[[Brenna Holt]]** | Merchant, general goods, caravan operator | Stocks basic supplies; missing caravan; maintains trade with market town | Approved recurring local character |
| **[[Sister Anwen]]** | Healer, medical goods, introductory Life-magic contact | Healing-house services and later awakening effects | Approved recurring local character |

**Environmental population:** Grazing animals and scavengers (neutral/attackable); local predators and dangerous scavengers (hostile); local human outlaws where the caravan theft story justifies them. The player can level, gather and explore without pursuing the royal conspiracy.

**Possible independent stories:** bridge repairs, missing livestock, farm protection, a mill dispute and minor village problems. These are working quests, not final locked quest chains.

## Settlement B — Upper Heartlands market town

**Working identity:** A larger commercial town at a river crossing, with warehouses, caravan yards, markets, workshops, transport services and regional authorities. Suggested band: 7–13. Name, distance and layout open.

| NPC / service | Role | Narrative connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Brenna Holt** | Warehouse/trading presence | Reappears naturally as her caravan business operates between settlements |
| **Master Oren Bell** | Visiting scholar and research contact | Optional ancient ward and ruined watchtower inquiries |
| **Weaponsmith** | Expanded weapon sales and repairs | Specialist craft and supply economy |
| **Armoursmith** | Expanded armour, shields and repairs | Distinct from village all-purpose smith |
| **General goods / food merchants** | Supplies, tools, consumables | Regional market |
| **Innkeeper** | Rest point, food and drink | Social and traveller hub |
| **Profession trainers and material merchants** | Intermediate instruction and recipes | Accessible progression |
| **Stable / coach service** | Regional travel | Road connection to capital |
| **Courier / mail service** | Correspondence and delivery | Supports economy and investigation |
| **Storage keeper** | Limited personal storage if system supports it | Convenience without replacing capital services |
| **Regional administrators** | Trade registers and legal records | Possible sources for the smuggling investigation |

**Enemy context:** Wolves and other predators in the surrounding woodland; river and cave creatures; human outlaws and smugglers tied to actual routes and storage sites. The optional abandoned quarry contains native cave dangers and perhaps damaged equipment, **not a compulsory Unmade laboratory**.

## Settlement C — Human inland racial capital

**Working identity:** A permanent major city, distinct from Covenant's multicultural coastal alliance capital. Available for normal travel before the formal story audience; becomes central around levels 10–20 and remains relevant through Act V.

| District | Principal services | Story presence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market district | General goods, food, banking, auction house, major merchants | Merchant and shipment records |
| Artisan district | Specialised weaponsmiths, armoursmiths, crafting stations, profession trainers | Materials, repairs and equipment |
| Scholarly quarter | Magical training, archives, specialist reagents | Research and historical context |
| Military quarter | Combat trainers, military suppliers, barracks | **[[Garrick Ashford]]** and royal military offices |
| Royal quarter | Court, administration, investigative offices | **[[King Aldren Vaelmont]]**, **[[Mara Thorne]]**, **[[Lord Cassian Veyr]]** |
| Transport district | Coaches, stables, couriers and routes | Connections to other [[Covenant]] territories |

The King is not a routine vendor; his audiences are major narrative events. Mara works from an investigative office rather than permanently waiting in the throne room. Garrick has military headquarters and later provides overseas mission contacts. Edric Halewyn appears through records, testimony and legacy, not as a living NPC.

Returning players should see changes appropriate to their progress, but core merchants and trainers must remain accessible. Avoid shared-world contradictions: quest-specific changes can use dialogue, local instancing or other carefully designed methods.

## Design questions still open

1. Name and place the village, market town, river, ruins, quarry and capital.
2. Define class roster and exactly which trainers teach what.
3. Define professions, vendor inventories, item tiers, repair economy and storage rules.
4. Name the blacksmith, innkeeper and specialised market-town service NPCs.
5. Design fauna species and ordinary enemy placement by habitat.
6. Determine XP, respawn and travel pacing.
7. Design local quests and the optional quarry dungeon without making them compulsory.
8. Determine shared-world phasing and NPC schedule constraints.

**Next step:** Establish the geography and named settlements before assigning detailed quest objectives and NPC schedules.
