## Memory I — The Six Reunited

**Placement:** After [[The Binding]], during the earliest reorganisation of the [[The Unmade|Unmade]], within the Shroud.

**Participants:** [[Oth'varen]], [[Vaer'uun]], [[Thes'var]], [[Su'vareth]], [[Oma'vira]], [[Khor'vun]].

**Status:** Approved dramatic scene; preserve the full dialogue rather than substituting the Chronicle's condensed summary.

Oth'varen had followed the traces of forbidden magic through the ruins of a research sanctuary he had once known well.

The building stood within the Shroud, its outer halls fractured by the catastrophe and its protective wards distorted beyond recognition. The familiar patterns of its construction had been altered, twisted into configurations that made his transformed senses recoil.

Yet he recognised the work.

Thes'var.

Oth'varen had hoped the five might have perished in the catastrophe. Not because he wished them dead, but because the alternative was almost too terrible to contemplate.

Now he knew they had survived.

He passed through the broken entrance and entered the central chamber.

Five figures waited among the remains of the sanctuary's ancient instruments.

He recognised them, although the sight of what they had become filled him with dread.

Their bodies bore the marks of the primordial energies unleashed by the First Anchor. Their magical structures had changed in ways no ordinary [[Kel'vari]] could have endured. The power surrounding them was immense, but it was not merely the extraordinary magic they had once commanded.

Something else had become part of them.

Vaer'uun stood at the centre.

He regarded Oth'varen with an expression that might once have been affection.

"You survived."

Oth'varen looked at the five.

"What have you done?"

Vaer'uun's expression hardened.

"We have survived what the gods could not comprehend."

Oth'varen gestured towards the ruined sanctuary.

"The world is broken. Our cities are gone. Our people are dying behind a prison they did not choose."

"And yet we remain," Vaer'uun answered. "Six minds that have crossed the boundary of what was believed possible. Six who have touched the source of a greater truth."

"Five," Oth'varen said.

The silence that followed was immediate.

Oma'vira moved forward.

"You still resist what you have become."

"I resist what made us this."

She regarded him with something approaching pity.

"You felt it. You cannot deny that. For a moment, you understood what lay beyond the limits of our existence."

Oth'varen remembered the rupture. The power entering him. The terrible invitation to surrender.

He remembered how close he had come.

"I understood that something was trying to claim us."

"Claim?" Oma'vira repeated. "You speak as though we were taken. We were chosen."

Thes'var turned from a fractured instrument.

"The Anchor was incomplete," he said. "Our calculations were constrained by principles we now know to be insufficient. The breach was uncontrolled because the structure could not sustain what it reached."

Oth'varen stared at him.

"The breach destroyed our civilization."

"Then the structure failed," Thes'var replied. "Not the principle."

There was no uncertainty in his voice.

Oth'varen recognised the reasoning. It was the same relentless logic that had once made Thes'var a brilliant engineer. But something essential had changed. The destruction of their homeland had become, in his mind, merely evidence of a technical limitation to be overcome.

"You would build it again?"

Thes'var looked almost offended.

"Would you abandon the greatest discovery in our history because its first application failed?"

"Failed?"

Oth'varen's voice rose.

"Thes'var, look around you!"

The ancient instruments trembled as power gathered around him.

He forced himself to release it.

Across the chamber, Su'vareth watched the exchange without moving.

"You have not come merely to accuse us," she said. "You have been gathering survivors. Preserving records. Seeking those who questioned our work."

Oth'varen turned towards her.

"Someone must preserve the truth."

"The truth?" Su'vareth asked. "Or the account you intend to place before those who would destroy us?"

"You concealed the alterations."

"We protected discoveries that lesser minds would have buried."

"You deceived the assemblies."

"We prevented fear from ending the work."

"You helped destroy our people."

For the first time, her composure faltered.

Only briefly.

"Our people were already prisoners of their own limitations," she answered. "We sought to free them."

Oth'varen felt something cold settle within him.

He had expected denial. Perhaps remorse concealed beneath pride. Some acknowledgement that the catastrophe had been a terrible mistake.

Instead, each of them had found a way to make the destruction serve the beliefs that had led them to it.

Vaer'uun spoke again.

"The gods have bound themselves to this Shroud because they fear what lies beyond it. They would deny creation the very power from which it might ascend."

"They bound themselves to prevent the world from being consumed."

"They bound themselves because they could not master what we discovered."

Oth'varen shook his head.

"You still believe it was a gift."

Vaer'uun stepped closer.

"It made us what we are."

"It destroyed everything we were."

For an instant, Vaer'uun's expression changed.

Oth'varen glimpsed something of the scholar he had known, the friend who had spent countless nights debating the nature of magic and the possibility of reaching Ascendant.

Then the moment passed.

"Perhaps," Vaer'uun said quietly, "what we were needed to be destroyed."

The words struck Oth'varen harder than any threat.

Behind Vaer'uun, Khor'vun shifted.

He had remained silent throughout the exchange, standing beside the entrance to the sanctuary's inner chambers.

Oth'varen looked towards him.

Of all the six, Khor'vun had once been the most cautious about exposing others to danger. He had insisted upon safeguards, redundant wards and procedures intended to prevent even minor accidents.

"Khor'vun. You were responsible for protecting them."

"I still am."

"Then protect them from this."

Khor'vun's gaze did not waver.

"The world beyond the Shroud would destroy us if it could. The surviving Kel'vari would turn against us. The gods have imprisoned us. Our enemies gather even now."

"Because of what we did."

"Because they fear what we have become."

Oth'varen realised that Khor'vun no longer distinguished between protecting the innocent and defending those who had brought about their suffering.

His loyalty had survived.

Its purpose had been corrupted.

Vaer'uun extended a hand.

"You need not remain apart from us."

Oth'varen felt the power within his body respond.

It was subtle at first, a familiar resonance beneath his thoughts.

Then the pull strengthened.

The five stood before him, their transformed magical structures resonating with the same primordial influence that had entered him during the rupture.

For a moment, the chamber seemed to change.

He could feel the extraordinary power they possessed. The connections between their altered forms. The possibility of joining those connections and becoming something greater than any one of them.

And beneath it all, the lingering influence of [[Nhal]] stirred.

Not a voice.

Not an instruction.

An invitation.

Oth'varen fought to hold his thoughts together.

Vaer'uun watched him.

"There. You feel it still."

Oth'varen did not answer.

"You have spent these years resisting what you already are. Imagine what we might accomplish if you ceased fighting yourself."

Oma'vira stepped closer.

"You are suffering because you refuse to accept the transformation. We can help you."

Oth'varen understood then that they were not merely trying to persuade him.

Their combined presence was strengthening the corruption within him.

Whether deliberately or through the nature of their transformed existence, the five were drawing him towards the same surrender he had resisted at the First Anchor.

He stepped back.

The power followed.

He reached for the principles he had spent his life studying: the stability of magical structures, the independence of interacting fields, the necessity of maintaining boundaries between forces that should not be allowed to consume one another.

He forced his altered magical structure into a configuration that resisted the resonance gathering around him.

Pain tore through his body.

But the pull weakened.

Vaer'uun lowered his hand.

For the first time, anger appeared openly upon his face.

"You would deny us even now?"

"I will not become what you have become."

The chamber's protective wards began to awaken.

Thes'var turned towards the ancient instruments. Su'vareth withdrew into the shadows surrounding the chamber. Oma'vira's expression hardened from pity into contempt.

Khor'vun moved between Oth'varen and the entrance.

"You cannot leave with what you know."

Oth'varen looked at the five who had once been his friends.

There would be no reconciliation.

Not here.

Not while Nhal's influence held them so completely.

He raised his hands.

The wards surrounding the chamber shuddered as he disrupted the structures sustaining them.

Thes'var reacted immediately, attempting to restore their stability.

For a brief moment, the two scholars fought through the magical workings they had once designed together.

Oth'varen did not attempt to destroy the sanctuary.

He knew he could not defeat all five.

He needed only to break the containment long enough to escape.

The chamber erupted with fractured light.

Khor'vun advanced through the collapsing wards, his transformed body resisting forces that would have killed an ordinary mortal.

Oth'varen released one final working.

The floor between them split, and the ancient protections surrounding the entrance failed.

He fled into the ruins.

Behind him, Vaer'uun's voice followed.

"You cannot escape what you are, Oth'varen."

Oth'varen did not turn.

He knew Vaer'uun was right about one thing.

The corruption had not left him.

It might never leave him.

But he would not surrender to it.

And he would not allow the five to bring about another Sundering.
