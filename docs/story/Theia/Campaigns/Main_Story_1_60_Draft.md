# Theia main story from level 1 to 60

**Working narrative draft — Needs Review, 30 October 2026.** This version follows the human protagonist through a proposed five-act launch campaign ending with Nhal's shattering. Other playable peoples retain their own racial beginnings and faction-loyal routes. The existing level bands remain: Act I 1–20, Act II 20–30, Act III 30–40, Act IV 40–50 and Act V 50–60. Later acts contain progressively longer regional campaigns; ordinary exploration and regional adventures supply substantial progression between principal chapters.

The founders' agreed death order and circumstances, and Oth'varen's final sacrifice, are carried forward. Their placement within launch is proposed here: Oma'vira moves to Act II, first Shroud access moves to Act III, and Oth'varen's death and Nhal's shattering move from future campaigns into Act V. To keep that access sequence coherent, the working Chainworks site moves from the outer Shroud to a concealed mainland facility. These changes require approval before replacing earlier locks. Existing geographic names remain working names. New connections, encounters and the specific workings described below are draft developments. All essential story outcomes remain solo-accessible; optional group content can expand the associated operations.

## Act I — The Crown and the Betrayal — Levels 1–20

The story begins in the Lower Heartlands, where the war is felt through missing workers, rising levies and delayed caravans. Millhaven is an agricultural village with a militia yard, a smithy, an inn and a healing house. The player is someone Captain Elara Fenwick can trust with a road patrol, not someone a prophecy has singled out.

The first journeys introduce people whose lives continue throughout the campaign. Brenna Holt needs her caravan found because the village depends on its supplies. Sister Anwen is treating more patients with a recently strengthened gift she cannot fully explain. Heddan Pike needs ordinary iron for tools as well as militia equipment. Darian Cole wants to prove himself and initially mistakes enthusiasm for preparation. Tomas Greymere argues against a levy that would consume next season's seed.

The player spends the early levels among farms, woodland and river crossings, protecting travellers, tracking predators and helping neighbours settle practical disputes. In Elderwood, Master Oren Bell studies an old ward that has begun responding after generations of inactivity. He records the change without pretending to know its cause. The magical awakening is present in ordinary life before it becomes a cosmic mystery.

Around the middle of the act, the missing caravan leads to human outlaws operating from an abandoned waystation. Their thefts are unusually selective: particular fittings, sealed containers and administrative correspondence disappear while more easily sold goods remain. Defeating their leader, Iven Rusk, breaks one local operation but reveals an unidentified buyer with access to royal credentials.

The investigation follows Brenna's business into Stoneford, a larger river-crossing town. Warehouse records, an injured teamster and a separately preserved dispatch establish that the thefts are connected. The player also works through local troubles in Greyfall Quarry and along the coach road. The growing inquiry matters because it is disrupting an inhabited region, not because every hazard conceals an Unmade agent.

Elara sends the player to Valecrown with evidence she lacks authority to authenticate. Senior Royal Investigator Mara Thorne initially treats the accusation cautiously. The player earns her confidence by comparing records and testing accounts. Their investigation reaches credentials associated with the office once held by Lord Edric Halewyn, the King's counsellor killed at the Shattered Delegation.

King Aldren Vaelmont grants an audience. He describes Edric as a trusted friend and explains the apparent Dominion betrayal that destroyed the negotiations. Lord Cassian Veyr, Edric's replacement, appears helpful. He provides access and suggests that Dominion intelligence is using stolen credentials to undermine the kingdom.

Royal Marshal Garrick Ashford becomes a plausible suspect because he opposed the negotiations. Yet his independently retained military records contradict Cassian's account. Garrick's opposition to peace was genuine; involvement in murder was not. Mara insists that the distinction matters.

By the final stretch of Act I, the player has corroborated Cassian's role. He supplied security information to intermediaries who promised Edric's death and his own advancement. He expected a targeted assassination and did not understand the full operation. Aldren orders his arrest through lawful authority.

The act culminates in the Hidden Passage, a covert transport route beneath an administrative complex. Cassian discovers that his intermediaries intend to silence him. The player defeats the cleanup operatives, preserves the records and brings him into custody.

At level 20, the human opening has a complete victory: a betrayal exposed and a local supply chain restored. Cassian's complicity does not establish the whole Delegation's authorship. Mara has a shipping ledger that leads beyond the kingdom. Aldren commissions the continuing inquiry, and the player leaves knowing both why home matters and how easily a convincing account can be wrong.

## Act II — The Hidden Design — Levels 20–30

The ledger leads to Tidehold, Covenant's multicultural coastal capital. High Ruler Corven Vaelmont receives the investigation separately from Aldren's royal court. Human, dwarven, Aelari and Korrath representatives argue over ships, materials and military commitments. The player discovers that an alliance contains sovereign peoples with obligations of their own.

Mara follows the paper trail while Garrick arranges passage to Northwatch Landing, a Covenant foothold on Dominion's Broken Coast. Commander Nessa Ward needs the player to understand the landing's supply routes before attempting an inland operation. Darian reappears as a newly trained soldier, still learning what service means away from familiar fields.

Across the frontier, Captain Rukhan Graal defends Dominion settlements against a real invasion. His quartermaster, Nemi Brindlewick, maintains records that do not fit a reported battlefield loss. The human player obtains a separately stored copy during a hostile reconnaissance mission. Neither officer becomes a friendly quest giver. Their work nevertheless supplies evidence that someone is concealing theft behind the war.

The first deliberate magical device is found beneath a coastal signal station. It is small, carefully maintained and associated with disturbances beyond the facility. During the Silent Signal encounter, the player defeats its operators and follows a safe shutdown sequence. Some dangerous effects diminish, but a nearby beneficial awakening also weakens. Destroying these workings may have consequences for people who never knew they existed.

Back at Tidehold, a reserved surveyor offers an interpretation of the recovered diagrams. He will not explain how he knows so much. The player meets him repeatedly as an adviser whose warnings are useful and whose evasions become increasingly noticeable. This is Oth'varen under his working identity as the Grey Surveyor.

The trail then reaches Kar Durn. Brynja Flintwake's workshop stands accused of producing defective expedition fittings. Rurik Anvilmark requires the inquiry to proceed through dwarven authority. The player helps rescue workers from a neglected feeder gallery and recovers the measuring equipment needed to examine the disputed components.

The failure in the gallery is ordinary neglect. The substituted fittings are deliberate. Clearing Brynja's workshop distinguishes the two and earns her participation in the investigation. She brings a habit that will matter at the finale: a claim is only as strong as its measurements.

In Sylvaren, Caelith Rowan and Irielle Mossward face unstable woodland wards, threatened cultivation and demands for living materials from the alliance. The player helps reopen a nursery route and recover missing ward-tenders before seeking restricted research. Queen Seralyth authorises a limited inquiry after evidence distinguishes a deliberately altered installation from the forest's other problems.

That installation reveals a larger device linking several smaller workings: a Regional Anchor. The player also encounters Sera Venn and Halden Marr, clandestine Guardian contacts who disagree over how much knowledge can safely be shared. A Guardian shutdown attempt nearly becomes a catastrophe because its copied instructions omit the supporting connections.

The player rescues the trapped team. The Grey Surveyor supplies the missing stability principles and cannot dismiss the fact that secrecy helped cause the failure. He begins sharing methods rather than merely issuing warnings. The Guardians remain hidden, but their mission and internal disagreements become real to the player.

A second overseas expedition reaches a Vaelari observation station through supported coastal routes. Records preserved by researcher Ilyss Varen show that inconvenient results were suppressed on the Dominion side too. The player retrieves evidence from hostile custody rather than receiving friendly assistance. The findings establish that a concealed organisation operates within both alliances.

The name Unmade becomes credible through recovered instructions, physical evidence and several independent sources. Its leadership titles begin to recur. The devices redistribute access to existing magic while placing pressure on an ancient containment. They are parts of a network already operating before the player began the investigation.

At home, Anwen demonstrates why the awakening cannot simply be rejected: people are benefiting from abilities that have returned or strengthened. Brynja, Caelith and the Grey Surveyor prepare an isolated trial. The player secures the site and controls its emergency cutoff while the surveyor reconstructs a small six-field working. For a measured interval, it produces counter-resonance without losing every beneficial effect.

The act's final campaign targets the Chainworks, a concealed Major Anchor complex inland of Covenant's own coast, connected to the shipping and supply trail. In this restructuring it is a mainland facility, allowing the first Shroud expedition to remain a later milestone. Oma'vira's conditioning facility occupies part of the installation. Through a limited projection encountered earlier in the act, she has already challenged the surveyor's resistance and shown her ability to manipulate bound subjects.

Here the player reaches her actual stronghold. They confront willing commanders, interrupt conditioning systems and free captives where possible. Some victims remain dangerous or profoundly affected after the bindings break. Oma'vira dies defending the operation. Her death ends a founder's work without automatically undoing its consequences.

With the facility secured, the player and specialists capture the Major Anchor's chamber. Evidence from its supports makes a bounded reconstruction possible. The Grey Surveyor performs the qualified six-field work; Brynja and Caelith monitor their own measurements; the player protects the cutoff and evacuation route. The Anchor is successfully inverted and remains operational under continuing supervision.

The local threat recedes. Beneficial effects partly endure. The player has killed a Conclave founder and helped secure a lasting protection. Thes'var studies the captured measurements from afar and recognises that his own designs can now be turned against their purpose.

## Act III — Beneath the Shroud — Levels 30–40

The inverted Anchor draws attention to a continuously weak Passage connection in the Korrath highlands. The player travels to Drom Kar, where Urodan and Tarek Oss begin with a water-route failure and endangered civic records. The ancient capital survives through generations of maintenance. Its inhabitants are living custodians, not an untouched storehouse waiting for outsiders.

First Elder Dromak permits a restricted comparison of the recovered Anchor plans with archival warnings. A missing passage in an old copy explains the Guardian accident. Other records identify a route into the Shrouded Lands, but Tarek refuses to authorise greater throughput until an extraction path is surveyed.

Corven approves a small expedition. Nessa commands its field operations, and the player crosses to the outer site provisionally called Lantern Redoubt. Passage is limited: supplies must be rationed, specialist transport planned and return capacity protected. A separate optional frontier introduces territorial warfare without making its victories prerequisites for the story.

Aru'ven, a Kel'vari custodian, requires the expedition to respect local boundaries. The player helps restore a water run and recover residents from an ancient defensive installation. Some inhabitants are transformed, some are hostile, and others are simply trying to remain alive. The supposedly vanished people have homes, work and disagreements.

Access gradually expands through independent enclave agreements. Thuma'rei's custodians guard the surviving Passage routes. Oru'sava sustains cultivation and healing. Soru'vaeth's reclaimers recover materials from ruined districts. Veyr'athun protects threatened settlements and challenges any foreign expedition that treats defence as a right to occupy. At Oth'irun's protected archive approach, Sel'ora and Leth'ari supervise the examination of original project records.

Those records establish six respected scholars working on a publicly authorised project intended to contact Ascendant. The Grey Surveyor appears among them as Oth'varen, responsible for independent stability verification. He acknowledges his identity, the hidden redirection of the First Anchor and his failed attempt to stop activation. He also admits the corruption he still carries.

The player can trust him personally or demand more verification. In either case, records and other witnesses support the account. His testimony is invaluable without making him an authority on events he never saw.

The historical sequence becomes clear: the First Anchor breached the Veil and began the Second Sundering; the Divine War followed; the Seven created the Shroud through the Binding. Ascendant's misuse of restricted knowledge does not establish the Kel'vari's collective guilt. The five willing scholars were transformed during activation, while Oth'varen resisted.

The scholars' post-Binding reunion is recovered as a memory or dramatic reconstruction, with its presentation mechanism still to be decided. It gives the player a personal understanding of the enemies behind the titles. Oth'varen once knew their achievements and trusted their judgement. His grief has never made their choices less dangerous.

Meanwhile, Su'vareth attacks the investigation. An apparent Guardian contact directs a party into an ambush. Copies disappear from one repository while independent records survive elsewhere. False requisitions expose a Kel'vari supply party to danger. The player learns her methods through specific losses and prevented attacks rather than a confession that she controlled everything.

The investigation returns to the Dominion storm coast. A landing operation reaches a stolen-component depot near routes defended by Warden Shorvek Tal. His defence of military and sacred sites is legitimate from his own allegiance. Records retrieved from the depot reveal alterations made before Covenant's raid, connecting the original thefts to a larger undertaking.

Together with Cassian's records and protected operational archives, this evidence establishes the Unmade's orchestration of the Shattered Delegation. It includes the murder of the Dominion High Ruler's brother, a Great Clan chief who opposed peace but reluctantly attended as an official delegate. His opposition did not make him the author of the attack. Both alliances were given carefully manipulated grounds for vengeance.

Aldren accepts the corrected account. Mara protects witnesses and corroborating copies before the public correction. Proof of sabotage does not settle prior violence, contested territory or the reasons the alliances still distrust one another.

The final investigation reaches Su'vareth's concealed intelligence headquarters through several separately verified cells. The player blocks her escape arrangements and tests her last attempt to redirect blame. She dies in the confrontation. Some records burn, but enough survives to reveal the provisional Veilbreaker project and the routes supplying it.

Orders issued under her contingency arrangements continue after her death. The player recognises familiar methods without mistaking them for her survival. By level 40, the Conclave has lost its conditioning authority and its chief information broker. Its surviving leaders now concentrate on a device intended to destroy the Veil itself.

## Act IV — The Unveiling — Levels 40–50

The first half of Act IV is a campaign to reach and interrupt the Veilbreaker. The site lies in a heavily defended research region within the Shrouded Lands; its precise geography remains to be chosen. Access grows from recovered routes and local agreements, not an unexplained march through the homeland.

The player works with Soru'vaeth reclaimers to identify usable approaches, Veyr'athun defenders to protect nearby communities and Oru'sava workers to prepare refuge supplies. Kel'vari priorities shape the campaign. Saving an archive may matter to the expedition, while preserving cultivation matters to those who must remain after it leaves.

Khor'vun has reorganised his forces after Oma'vira's death. He relies increasingly on willing troops, existing bound forces and layered defences. Successive operations remove his observation posts, reinforcement routes and ward supports. Subordinate commanders provide substantial confrontations without replacing the founder's authority.

Thes'var modifies the project as its supply network comes under attack. Vaer'uun directs surviving cells towards activation. Su'vareth's old contingency instructions trigger diversions elsewhere, forcing Corven and the enclave authorities to make difficult commitments. The player cannot defend every threatened site personally.

The assault reaches the fortified approaches while activation is already advancing. Captives remain inside, expedition forces are cut off, and nearby settlements need warning. Khor'vun refuses withdrawal even as his own people become endangered. His determination to protect the undertaking finally consumes what he once claimed to defend.

The player kills him and opens an evacuation route. Further operations interrupt destructive secondary effects, preserve research and prevent some prepared refuges from failing. The main sequence, however, has passed its stopping point. The player sees why reversal would now worsen the catastrophe.

The Veil falls. The project also overwhelms the First Anchor's separate containment arrangement, breaking the working that binds the Seven and collapsing the Shroud. The exact engineering of that linked failure remains a development requirement. The two barriers are distinct, and their failures have different causes.

The Seven are freed as independent beings. Nhal gains access to the Physical Realm. The catastrophic transition is followed by a substantial survival campaign rather than an immediate final battle.

Nhal's influence spreads through concentrations and manifestations of its essence. Areas beyond the first footholds remain inhabited and defensible. The player protects return routes, rescues stranded teams and helps establish which phenomena can be contained. Inverted Anchors preserve pockets of stability, but adapting their workings after the Shroud's disappearance requires investigation.

The player returns to allied regions now under pressure. In Sylvaren, Caelith struggles to keep useful wards from propagating an unfamiliar disturbance. At Kar Durn, Brynja must choose safe isolation points without abandoning dependent settlements. Anwen treats displaced people in the Heartlands. Elara keeps local protection functioning despite demands for more militia. Darian's experience now makes him valuable in extraction work.

The gods begin appearing through bounded encounters. Guardian protects a threatened refuge; Warrior holds a contested approach while mortals withdraw. Seeker and Shaper disagree over incomplete evidence and immediate reconstruction. Their freedom has not supplied a shared plan or repaired their relationships.

Vaer'uun establishes himself at a major manifestation site, interpreting the catastrophe as fulfilment. Nhal's conduct exposes how little his followers' survival matters to it. He nevertheless chooses obedience and binds himself to the site's communication working, trying to preserve his claim to speak for its purpose.

The player dismantles its supporting installations, protects threatened captives and kills him. Disrupting the site constrains a major manifestation without defeating Nhal everywhere. Measurements reveal that its physical concentrations depend on relationships which can be contested. Vaer'uun's death ends the Conclave's central authority, leaving Thes'var as its last founder.

By level 50, several communities are secure enough to organise resistance. The world remains in danger, but the player has evidence that its new conditions impose limits even on Nhal. The inverted installations are also responding to one another in ways nobody predicted.

## Act V — The Alignment — Levels 50–60

The final act begins with investigation and repair. Oth'varen, Brynja, Caelith and Tarek compare records from surviving inverted Anchors. Installations that were designed to reinforce containment have developed a shared stable resonance across distance. Their earlier protections were one application of a deeper capability.

The player revisits sites where people have maintained those protections through the catastrophe. Some require rescue, replacement parts or the recovery of specialist records. Others remain operational because earlier agreements ensured that local workers understood the risks and had authority to halt unsafe experiments. The final network grows from places the player already knows.

Oth'varen's stability expertise helps turn observations into a hypothesis. The installations could coordinate distinct divine workings across all six energies while allowing the Seven to remain independent. The mortals operating different stations would perform their own specialist tasks; this is not a discovery that ordinary mages can pool power without limits.

Before the design can be attempted, the Seven must agree to participate. The player helps recover the evidence and protect the places where those discussions can occur. Their contributions give mortals standing in the argument without appointing the protagonist ruler over gods.

Keeper must disclose the hidden cost of the Binding. Keeper alone knowingly concealed that the Seven would be bound indefinitely. Guardian and the others were deceived; understanding that truth after the event did not mean they had consented beforehand.

Ascendant acknowledges the misuse of Keeper's knowledge while defending the Kel'vari's independent right to exist. Seeker must share uncertain findings instead of withholding them until certainty arrives. Shaper must accept that restoring every ancient working unchanged would repeat some old failures. Warrior must distinguish accountability from vengeance, and Judge must accept obligations that constrain divine participants as well as mortals. Guardian must accept protection that does not depend on bearing every burden alone.

Their cooperation emerges through difficult practical commitments. They need not forgive one another. Each understands the proposed working, the danger and the conditions under which it will be stopped. No concealed eternal imprisonment underpins the agreement.

Accounts from the Seven, tested against recovered records, reveal the larger primordial history. Aethyr shattered to create the Physical Realm and Void, establish the Veil and confine Nhal. The gods emerged from that inheritance without possessing Aethyr's complete mind. Nhal's opportunity came through later resonance, not through a role in creating the Kel'vari.

The final proposal exploits Nhal's manifested presence within an existing physical world. The distributed network can constrain the relationships connecting its concentrations; the Seven can coordinate their workings against that coherent presence. This gives them a prepared advantage unavailable in the original primordial stalemate. Exact metaphysical mechanics remain to be refined through this draft's research chapters.

Preparations take the player through the alliance's territories and back to its hostile overseas routes. Recovered Dominion measurements provide additional evidence, obtained through raids on Unmade stores and hostile intelligence operations. Separate Dominion agents and institutions pursue their own defensive objectives. The crisis does not create friendly cross-alliance questing or a shared government.

At Tidehold, Corven commits specified people and materials while respecting the racial authorities responsible for each installation. Aldren supports the human contribution. Mara protects independent copies of the safety procedures. Brenna organises allied supplies. Brynja's workshop remains staffed, Caelith's ward-tenders participate under local authority, and Tarek certifies the routes.

Within the former Shroud, Kel'vari communities decide which installations they will host and what protection they require. Oth'varen trains Guardian teams and shares the procedures widely enough that his absence would no longer destroy the undertaking. He expects to survive. He discusses future repairs and the responsibilities he will have to answer for afterwards.

Thes'var has followed the reconstruction and knows its vulnerabilities. Before the final confrontation, he faces Oth'varen as a former colleague. He recognises that inversion works. His refusal concerns its purpose: the other scholar has turned their knowledge towards protecting lives and freedoms Thes'var has spent centuries subordinating to Nhal's arrival. He chooses to destroy the achievement.

During the final operation, Nhal disrupts the developing alignment. Thes'var compounds the disturbance by attacking supporting connections at a critical junction. The player reaches him through installations and defensive systems whose functions have been established during preparation.

The player kills the last Conclave founder. His interference has already left cascading instability, and stopping the attacker does not complete the repair. Across the network, Guardian teams need corrections before failing sections endanger the whole working.

Oth'varen recognises the pattern of a catastrophe he once tried to stop. This time he gives others the complete instructions. The player carries out the decisive corrections and secures the transition while distributed crews perform their own tasks. Oth'varen remains at the central installation, stabilising it through exposure his transformed nature can endure only for a limited time.

The readings settle. The alignment holds beyond his personal contribution, and the other stations sustain it. Oth'varen sees that they have succeeded. His exhausted body fails before the final confrontation ends. He dies still corrupted, still resistant and without becoming a permanent source of power.

For a brief moment he has been the last living member of the six scholars. Their knowledge now rests with people able to question and correct one another.

The player must continue. The Seven coordinate their workings while the mortal teams maintain the network. Nhal's concentrations lose their ability to reinforce one another. Its coherent presence is threatened with destruction.

Recognising that defeat is imminent, Nhal deliberately shatters its own essence. Fragmentation lets scattered power escape the closing working, but breaks the connections that sustain its unified consciousness. The final mechanism must establish that this cannot be reversed: there is no intact Nhal hidden behind the victory.

The network protects some regions from the worst of the scattering. Others suffer new wounds. The player holds the final sequence long enough to protect operating teams and preserve the installations that remain useful. The primordial enemy is defeated, and the world survives changed.

## The return home — Level 60

The story continues beyond the moment of victory. At the surviving expedition settlement, the player meets Guardian teams accustomed to waiting for Oth'varen's judgement. They must now decide together how to preserve his work, including the record of his mistakes.

Kel'vari witnesses remember him as a person who knew their lost world. Brynja preserves measurements he would have insisted on checking. Caelith reports the living effects rather than allowing the result to become a flawless legend. The network is an achievement with continuing costs and responsibilities.

In Valecrown, Mara completes the human case. Cassian receives the proposed verdict and permanent imprisonment following lawful trial. Aldren publishes a protected correction of the Delegation account and establishes safeguards for reviewing accusations. The war continues, with its grief and grievances no longer reducible to the Unmade's deception.

Corven honours the contributions of the four sovereign peoples and the obligations owed to independent Kel'vari communities. The Seven remain separate beings, responsible for what they choose to do with freedom. Their participation has not settled all divine disputes or made the alliances one society.

The player returns to Millhaven. Elara is still responsible for its safety. Tomas is arguing over stores and levies. Brenna's caravan carries ordinary tools and letters. Anwen treats people who need care after the fighting. Darian helps a new recruit prepare for the road.

These people have survived through their own work as well as the player's. Home is recognisable, altered by losses and by the possibilities the awakening has preserved.

Only after the homecoming does a small report reach the player: somewhere beyond a protected region, a concentration of scattered essence has produced an unfamiliar effect. It is not Nhal speaking again. Its meaning remains unknown.

The launch ends with a complete victory and a future to explore. Oth'varen is gone, the five founders are dead and Nhal's singular will has ended. The inheritance scattered across Theia has yet to find its own direction.
