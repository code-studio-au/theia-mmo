**Status: Needs Review.** These are the allied quest sequences and Dominion character stories needed to support the proposed complete [canonical Human 1–80 campaign](Human_1_80_Canonical_Campaign.md). They preserve sovereign institutions and permanent alliance separation. They are not approval of the other races' openings or a claim to have written all eight full campaigns.

The allied sequences below are substantial visits within the 92-quest human route; their `CR-` identifiers group existing quests and do not add duplicate mandatory tasks. Dominion sequences are **12 proposed companion-race quests** for the native alliance's future use. They have local beginnings and endings here, but are not prerequisites, friendly NPC quests or playable additions to the human route. A future Dominion writer can retain the character motive and evidence boundary without adopting every proposed objective.

## Dwarves — An honest measure

**Human visit:** H2-05–H2-07 in Kar Durn. **Optional local continuation:** HS-07–HS-09. **Local protagonists:** Brynja Flintwake and Rurik Anvilmark; working ruler High Thane Bromund Stonevein. All new names and this quest staging need review.

Brynja is accused of cutting costs on fittings that support a Covenant expedition. Her original drawing is sound. A broker substituted a different housing while keeping her workshop's inspection mark. She wants the accusation answered because her craft and her workers' livelihood matter, not because the story needs a dwarf to explain an artifact.

The hold also faces a maintenance failure unrelated to the hidden network. Rurik refuses to let a human investigator bypass the hold's law. Helping recover the measuring assembly and secure the feeder gallery gives the player a role in solving a present local problem. Original plans, tool wear and a separate dispatch show that the suspect consignment left after inspection and was altered downstream.

| Beat | Existing human quest | Playable local action | Decision and closure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CR-D1 — The accused craft | H2-05 | Compare original plans with the disputed housing, interview the workshop and obtain a hold warrant. | Human authority is insufficient by itself; Rurik accepts a bounded local inquiry. |
| CR-D2 — The neglected gallery | H2-06 | Rescue maintenance workers, recover the assembly and secure a pre-existing work route. | A mundane fault is corrected; it is not retrospectively blamed on Nhal. |
| CR-D3 — The hold's commission | H2-07 | Present independent measurements and verified shipment records to the local authority. | The workshop is cleared of the substitution, the broker's route is investigated and Brynja accepts a contracted expedition role. |

**Continuing character:** Brynja begins by demanding proof of workmanship and later demands equally strict evidence for inversion. She trusts a measured limit over a brilliant promise. Her deputy Edda keeps the workshop and local instruction available. The human–dwarf treaty is represented by mutual needs and consent, not human ownership of dwarf craft.

**Other dwarf players:** A future dwarf racial route can lead them to Brynja through their own institutions; it need not repeat the human court case. This packet does not settle what unique treasures the ancient capital contains, the exact leadership system beyond the confederated holds, or the unanswered original late-stage questions.

## Aelari — The living boundary

**Human visit:** H3-01–H3-05 in Sylvaren. **Optional local continuation:** HS-10–HS-12. **Local protagonists:** Caelith Rowan, Irielle Mossward and Queen Seralyth.

Caelith's boundary wards protect inhabited woodland and a nursery, not a sacred abstraction. The alliance's demand for useful living material has exceeded what some local tenders consider safe. Veylan Istren sells observations to an intermediary while arguing that the monarchy's restrictions entrench outside military control. The player must hear the real resource dispute rather than treating every objector as an enemy.

Working with Irielle shows an ordinary predator problem whose solution is safer land use. The deliberately altered research ward is a different case. That distinction lets Seralyth protect her people's position while accepting evidence of an external operation.

**Continuing character:** Caelith becomes a technical observer in the final trial because she knows the local biological effects. She cannot perform every six-field working, and she continues asking who bears the cost of apparently helpful magic. She wants useful research with accountability, not universal suppression.

**Historical boundary:** Conflicting Aelari and Vaelari records can be preserved as conflicting. This draft does not determine the original elven capital's destruction, the detailed schism or a modern friendship between Seralyth and Selrion. The Queen remains an independent monarch, not Corven's provincial official.

## Korrath — The warning in the margin

**Human visit:** H3-10–H3-18 in Drom Kar and its expedition approaches. **Optional local continuation:** HS-13–HS-15. **Local protagonists:** Urodan, Tarek Oss and First Elder Dromak.

The ancient capital has survived because generations repaired it. Urodan sees memory as a civic obligation; a missing person's name matters alongside a famous ward. Tarek maintains practical routes. The player first assists with a water-route problem and an endangered records store, grounding the visit in present needs.

A warning omitted from a widely used copy changes the Anchor investigation. Its omission reflects long-standing restrictions and loss of context, not an instant revelation that all Korrath history is fraudulent. Dromak permits comparison and accepts that keeping an incomplete instruction can be more dangerous than sharing a complete one under safeguards.

## Orcs — A shore worth defending

**Human appearances:** H2-10, H2-16 and H5-06. **Native protagonist:** Captain Rukhan Graal. He is a soldier in the orc Great Clan confederation's military framework, not a new sovereign ruler or a founder of an invented Great House.

Rukhan defends a coast where Covenant soldiers have a fortified landing. He has strategic and personal reasons to oppose them that do not require Unmade orders. His mistakes arise from pressure and incomplete intelligence: a supply report attributes losses to a battle before that battle occurred, and he initially treats Nemi's objections as untimely accounting. His growth is accepting accurate information without becoming friendly to an invasion.

Nemi's expertise is logistics, inspection and precise defensive work. Her accounts are useful because she has maintained them honestly, not because she secretly wants to aid Covenant. Ilyss values discovery. That does not mean she accepts every promising result. A purchased component produces inconsistent observations, and the broker presses for acceptance under wartime urgency. She rejects the substitution and files a copy of her measurements with a military observation station, where the human player later retrieves it.

## Vorrath — What the raid did not take

**Human appearances:** H4-10–H4-13. **Native protagonist:** Warden Shorvek Tal, a military custodian of coastal approaches near sacred routes. His respect for Ascendant is not evidence of serving Nhal.

Shorvek has two concrete responsibilities: keep pilgrims out of a military danger zone and account for missing ward components. A convenient report blames Covenant, yet his inspection shows the crates were moved through an internal depot before the raid. He can pursue that discrepancy while still fighting Covenant intruders. The player sees his boundaries, patrols and a recovered objection, not a friendly explanation of Vorrath history.

## Kel'vari — Permission is not possession

The Kel'vari are a surviving people, not a third playable alliance. Aru'ven, Sel'ora and optional specialist Leth'ari provide local relationships for H4-02–H4-08 and HS-19–HS-21. Their workplaces are limited outer approaches connected to Thuma'rei, Oth'irun and Oru'sava. This does not require the player to tour every enclave.

Their shared local arc is complete: newcomers request entry; residents set terms; the player helps with water, safety and records; a threatened custodian is rescued; the Covenant expedition honours boundaries at its climax and departure. Access rests on observed conduct and negotiated responsibility. Aru'ven can restrict a route after a hazard without becoming an arbitrary quest blocker; a safe alternative or repeatable repair task preserves the principal route.

Sel'ora provides a translation and a bounded piece of independent evidence. She does not surrender the archive, acknowledge Covenant overlordship or reveal Keeper's complete intentions. Leth'ari's cultivation story makes survival depend on ordinary skilled work as well as wards. None of the three speaks for all five enclaves or represents every corrupted inhabitant.

## Shared-world and faction consistency

These proposed native quests supply authors with motives and counterpart evidence, not live cross-faction dependencies. A human player recovering a case copy does not erase Nemi's only record; a Dominion player completing a defensive scene does not delete the human landing. Repeatable NPC or story-instance versions provide each campaign's account without requiring two live player populations to produce a specific historical outcome.

Live territorial events may change supplies, patrol activity and optional rewards within their approved systems. They do not retroactively change which official betrayed Edric or whether a player completed the human story. Dominion's future full campaign must produce its own defensive victory through its own institutions, without needing the human hero's completion flag.

**ARCHIVED, not an approved expansion roadmap.** Source: [Future Saga, draft dated 27 October 2026 with later annotations](https://github.com/code-studio-au/theia-mmo/blob/9f52c53e0bb24ffdb5f231598116138653bd19e4/docs/story/Theia/Campaigns/Future%20Saga.md). **SUPERSEDED source assumptions:** the old Act V launch-finale placement, Vaer'uun-after-Veilbreaker ordering, decisive misalignment attributed solely to Nhal, deliberate self-shattering by Nhal and intact post-launch Shroud assumptions are historical provenance only. ### Historical launch and recovery concepts

- **Captured installations as lasting victories:** an older Act V proposal captured and reconstructed one Major Anchor instead of destroying it, retaining useful magical redistribution while reinforcing local protection. Its configurations, damage and defenses made inversion site-specific rather than a universally repeatable procedure. Maintenance, defense and negotiated custodianship would continue; the Conclave would adapt designs and try to recapture installations. This is not an additional approved launch ending, a closed breach or a claim that the old Shroud persists after Q156.
- **A living homeland to reclaim:** alongside the enclave-negotiation and route concepts in the Chronicle section above, the saga proposed disagreements over sharing archives and reclaiming lost cities, with cultivation and safe water as priorities rather than treating residents only as expedition supporters. Stabilizing routes and wards, recovering evidence and reclaiming a research complex could bring durable local security, then reveal that essential research had moved elsewhere. An additional supporting inverted Anchor remains a proposal.
- **A surviving ancient magical order:** a group with limited knowledge and interests of its own could emerge during recovery. Its identity and relationship to the Guardians are unresolved; it is not approved as their hidden ruler.
- **Veilbreaker development rationale:** the historical “Veilbreaker Anchor” label and construction model proposed rare knowledge, materials and coordinated installations concentrating energy on the boundary while avoiding another uncontrolled continental fracture. One suggested motive was the distributed network's vulnerability to capture/inversion. Neither that causal order nor the device model is locked; why it could not be built during the Age of Silence and what renewed Nhal guidance contributed remain development questions.
- **Consequential successes despite irreversible activation:** evacuating captives and expedition survivors, preserving research, protecting inverted installations and disrupting components threatening prepared refuges could let a failed shutdown still save people and places. These are encounter possibilities, not additions to the current quest inventory.
- **Contested manifestation footholds:** the draft proposed that Nhal required local concentrations of essence to project power across the Physical Realm. Gradual manifestation is established in the campaign; this precise limit, spread rate, costs and relation to the six fields are not. Failing wards, altered terrain, unstable magic and broken routes could motivate rescue and resource defense without treating exposure as willing allegiance.
- **Adapted inversion after the Shroud:** local stabilization against unmaking could require new research and field operations, rather than continuing to strengthen a vanished Shroud. Preserved refuges and infrastructure would make earlier victories matter. This additional capability is unapproved, and separate alliance defensive campaigns do not imply peace or shared government.

### Divine cooperation and mortal stewardship themes

The source proposed recovering and testing history, protecting communities and constructing safeguards before an informed, bounded agreement among independent divine participants. Cooperation need not imply forgiveness; limits, responsibilities and withdrawal would matter. Oth'varen's testimony would remain fallible rather than omniscient. These specific character developments remain proposals, not new creator locks:

- Ascendant acknowledges misuse of Keeper's knowledge while defending the Kel'vari's independent right to exist, without collective guilt.
- Keeper relinquishes the claim to decide another being's sacrifice without consent.
- Seeker shares uncertain knowledge when waiting for certainty would condemn others.
- Shaper accepts losses that cannot be repaired by restoring every ancient work.
- Guardian accepts protection that does not require endless personal sacrifice.
- Warrior distinguishes accountability from vengeance that prevents cooperation.
- Judge develops obligations constraining divine power as well as mortal institutions.

The current campaign already governs the Seven's voluntary participation and survival, Oth'varen's informed fatal correction and Nhal's shattering. No additional finale, divine merger, resurrection or alternate death mechanism is retained as an active option. The source's broader theme was responsibility for inherited harm without inevitable repetition; it does not settle later plot outcomes.

### Unapproved post-launch and long-range possibilities

- **Scattered inheritance:** essence concentrations might develop independent beings, with no approved number, names, emergence mechanism or memories. Proposed conflicting philosophies included ending suffering by ending desire/life, absolute stillness, severing dependence, absorbing individuals into a whole and rejecting physical form as imprisonment. These are historical motives, not portfolios or proof that any fragment develops an identity.
- **Choice rather than ancestry:** a hypothetical fragment-born being could reject destructive inheritance. Aethyr's successors, the Kel'vari and resistance to corruption were thematic precedents, not evidence of a new fragment-autonomy mechanism. The old proposal to seed such an outcome into earlier campaigns is not permission to alter approved revelation timing.
- **A different divine conflict:** hypothetical powers might compete over territory and magical conditions, seek followers, transform places or create a non-playable people suited to hostile environments. Such a people's culture, dependence on makers and capacity to refuse them would need development; non-playable does not mean uniformly hostile or guilty. Alignments need not follow divine ancestry.
- **Unselected future divine deaths:** the source entertained them only as contingent future possibilities tied to established arcs and lasting institutional consequences. It selected no victim; no death among the Seven is approved, and magic would not automatically lose an entire discipline.
- **A distributed protective network:** the long-range concept favored mortal expertise, maintenance, resources, local consent and defended installations, with willing divine contributions under enforceable limits rather than another deceived eternal sacrifice. Terms, enforcement and containment remained undesigned. Separate societies would retain responsibility; neither one world government nor Covenant–Dominion reconciliation was approved.
- **A changed but habitable world:** lost and reclaimed places, dangerous regions, independent threats and ordinary lives continuing beyond primordial crisis formed an aspirational ending, not a settled expansion outcome. Fragment fate, new peoples, expansion count and dates remain OPEN.

For the creator's separate direction to develop substantial recurring bosses, dungeons and optional raids with motives, histories and consequences, consult the dated decision ledger rather than infer a roster, boss count or production scope from this saga. Essential story conclusions remain solo-accessible.

### Distinct regional story proposals

**Permission to Remain** proposed roughly eleven substantial rewarded steps, with briefings integrated into missions. Corven would authorize a bounded multi-people Covenant expedition from Tidehold; ordinary transport reaches a highland hub, followed by foot/mount travel to exceptional Passage access. Nessa sets the field plan, Tarek measures the return path and limits loads and specialist arrivals, Brynja joins technical work, and Caelith observes the environment. The Grey Surveyor advises without revealing his identity.

At Lantern Redoubt, Aru'ven would grant temporary shelter without conceding ownership. A water-maintenance rescue involves territorial wildlife, an ancient defensive installation, a separate control station, protection of a local worker identifying the mechanism and a secured retreat, all sequentially solo-accessible. The Surveyor recognizes machinery before translation, helps prevent injury and avoids explaining his familiarity; Aru'ven notices. A second expedition follows supply-route damage, scavenging and theft to a shared storehouse guarded by a damaged construct following an obsolete protective instruction, not Nhal. Isolating the working restores access without granting a permanent class ability.

An agreement would define Redoubt use, supplies, boundaries and withdrawal, with Nessa accepting deployment limits and Aru'ven granting only local permission. Archive custodians prepare a supervised approach while route work continues, creating a narrative pause rather than a real-time countdown. Three proposed enclave-associated outposts form a district, not new capitals or approved geography; name, distances and adjacency remain open.

- **The Return Route:** a Thuma'rei-associated supply route faces expedition traffic beyond its safe capacity. A scheduled party finds displaced animal nesting near a sheltered crossing, hazardous detours, broken warning markers and scavenged supplies. Eight proposed steps cover surveying alternatives, recovering equipment, protecting workers and a final supply movement against a territorial threat. Outward/return grouping and field reports reduce repeated backtracking. A negotiated local transport stop is possible, not an unrestricted portal. No Conclave exposure, identity reveal or hidden Unmade plot is required.
- **What the Gardens Need:** an Oru'sava-associated cultivation settlement loses harvest while a growth working coincides with symptoms in workers and animals. Neglected equipment, contaminated stores and a configuration mismatched to local conditions interact. Caelith observes and respects local expertise; Anwen may receive authorized medical observations without Guardian status or secret maps. Eight steps culminate in either immediate isolation or bounded safeguarded repair, both preserving harvest and people. Choices alter local responses and follow-up work without withholding essential campaign evidence.
- **The People Who Return:** a Soru'vaeth reclaiming party needs practical materials for homes from a ruined research district. Records of a missing earlier group reveal a mistaken defensive boundary and matter to relatives as well as scholars. Eight steps ask what can be recovered safely; the player disables construct defenses while NPC crews hold cleared stations, without simultaneous multiplayer or raid requirements. The Surveyor interprets a warning but accepts correction from a local worker, foreshadowing shared authority without revealing his history.
