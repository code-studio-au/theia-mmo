# Future Saga

> [!important] Proposed chronological direction
> This draft develops the creator's proposed long-term story with recommended connecting events and an ending. Permission to draft is not approval of these outcomes. Existing history remains governed by its source locks; the future events below remain Needs Review. Campaign movements are not a fixed expansion count, release schedule or numerical timeline.

## The story's destination

The recommended ending is a world sustained through mortal stewardship and cooperation among independent divine beings, rather than dependence on imprisoned gods or one supreme primordial will. Nhal's unified consciousness ends permanently. Its surviving essence does not automatically determine the allegiance of beings born from it.

The central question is whether people and gods can take responsibility for their inheritance without being condemned to repeat it. The final solution grows from the player's earlier work with Anchors, surviving communities and historical evidence.

## 1. Before play — the world begins to awaken

The established historical foundation is [[Age of Silence and Anchor Network Development]], followed by [[Network Threshold and Modern Awakening]]. The Conclave has already developed Minor Resonators, Regional Anchors and functioning Major Anchors. Their cumulative resonance redistributes access to existing primordial magic and restores Nhal's selective communication. [[Shattered Delegation]] precedes the return of that communication; it was independently orchestrated by the Conclave.

The original First Anchor remains entangled with the ancient Veil breach. The Seven sustain the Shroud through [[The Binding]]. Most people do not understand the source of the awakening, and the two playable alliances remain hostile.

## 2. Acts I–IV — discovery, evidence and the first Shroud expedition

The approved five-act framework supplies the launch campaign's foundation. Distinct racial openings lead into alliance warfare, investigation of hidden devices and discovery of the Unmade network. The player encounters evidence of Regional Anchors, the Guardians' unequal knowledge and the difference between willing followers, corrupted victims and bound forces.

Limited Shroud access brings contact with surviving [[Kel'vari]] communities and testimony from [[Oth'varen]]. Independent records establish the six scholars' work, the concealed redirection of the First Anchor and the distinction between Ascendant's misconduct and the innocence of the wider Kel'vari population.

The campaign introduces inversion as difficult reconstruction of six-field workings. A controlled smaller test can establish the principle before the finale. Research, access to suitable equipment and safeguarding supporting connections prepare a meaningful attempt at a Major Anchor.

The smaller test and its exact placement are proposals; they are not already approved launch outcomes.

## 3. Act V — the first Major Anchor inversion

**Recommended launch finale:** the player helps capture and successfully invert one Major Anchor rather than destroying it. The operation requires securing its supporting installations, understanding the particular configuration, evacuating endangered people and completing reconstruction while the Unmade resist.

The converted installation reinforces the Shroud and preserves some beneficial redistribution of magical energy. It does not close the original breach, end the awakening, silence Nhal everywhere or free the Seven.

Success is possible here because the campaign has obtained the necessary plans, specialists, access and control of supporting links. Other Major Anchors have different configurations, damage and defences; inversion is not a universal procedure that can immediately be repeated at every site.

The converted site needs continuing maintenance and protection. Its custodians must negotiate responsibility with affected communities. The alliances pursue their own objectives and claims; this draft does not establish public cross-alliance cooperation or a shared faction-neutral government.

The victory changes the strategic situation. The Conclave can no longer assume that every captured device must be destroyed. It has an urgent reason to defend its designs, change its configurations and reclaim converted installations.

**End state:** a real local victory and a functioning foundation for later protection. The original threat survives, but the player's achievement remains useful throughout the saga.

## 4. Beyond launch — reclaiming a living homeland

The next movement expands Shroud access through carefully restored Passage connections. Covenant and Dominion establish separate approaches and negotiate independently with Kel'vari communities. Existing low-capacity routes are not suddenly equivalent to mass transport.

The central conflict concerns who controls recovery of the homeland. Enclaves differ over opening routes, sharing archives and reclaiming lost cities. Their inhabitants need cultivation, safe water, supplies, trade and defensible homes as well as protection from the Unmade. Their choices are not reduced to whether they support the player's expedition.

The player helps stabilise routes and wards, recover evidence and reclaim a significant research complex. The existing inverted Anchor supports these efforts. Discoveries clarify why the original First Anchor cannot safely be smashed and why restoring every ancient working is not automatically desirable.

The surviving ancient magical order can emerge during this movement with limited, valuable knowledge and interests of its own. Its identity and relationship to the Guardians still require development; this draft does not make it the hidden ruler of either organisation.

**Campaign resolution:** a community or region gains durable security, and a Conclave operation is defeated. Recovered records reveal that essential research has already been moved to another location.

## 5. The Conclave's new undertaking — a deliberate Veil-destroying Anchor

The Conclave develops a new device, provisionally called the **Veilbreaker Anchor**. Its final name and location remain undecided. It is not another ordinary Major Anchor, nor a declaration that the original First Anchor can now be safely replaced or destroyed.

The original catastrophe arose from redirected research whose full consequences were concealed. The new undertaking is deliberately designed to dismantle the Veil. It is more controlled in how it delivers energy: it aims to avoid the original uncontrolled continental fracturing while concentrating its effect on the boundary itself. That makes it more precise, not safe for the world.

Its construction requires rare knowledge, materials and coordinated installations. The proposed causal connection to Act V is that the Conclave seeks a concentrated way to defeat containment after its distributed network becomes vulnerable to capture and inversion. Further technical development must explain why this was not achievable during the Age of Silence and how renewed guidance from Nhal changes its prospects.

The founders pursue the project for different reasons:

- [[Vaer'uun]] sees the culmination of their long obedience and defends the promise of Nhal's arrival.
- [[Thes'var]] believes deliberate control can overcome the failures of the original project.
- [[Su'vareth]] protects logistics and information while preparing contingencies against defeat or betrayal.
- [[Oma'vira]] prepares followers for revelation and transformation, including coercive preparations imposed on captives.
- [[Khor'vun]] faces the consequences of defending a project that may consume the forces and strongholds entrusted to him.

These positions create potential divisions without promising a founder's redemption, death or defection. The campaign should establish each surviving founder's choices through actions, not assume all five will occupy identical roles forever.

## 6. Activation — the Veil falls

The player understands the undertaking but cannot prevent its final activation. The confrontation still allows consequential successes: evacuation, preservation of research, protection of inverted installations and disruption of components that would otherwise destroy prepared refuges. Exact objectives remain to be designed.

**Recommended major turning point:** the Veil is completely destroyed, rather than merely acquiring another local tear. Nhal gains access to the Physical Realm. This is an irreversible change to the world's condition, not a larger repetition of the original breach.

The Shroud fails through a related but distinct mechanism. The Veilbreaker operation also overwhelms the original First Anchor's containment arrangement, breaking the working that binds the Seven. This mechanism needs further definition; the Veil and Shroud do not collapse together merely because both are barriers.

The Seven are released from their commitment. Their individual identities survive, but freedom does not automatically restore their relationships, their knowledge or unlimited power.

**End state:** the old containment is gone. The player's preserved refuges and infrastructure determine how much of the world can withstand what follows.

## 7. The Unveiling — resisting the unmaking of the world

Nhal's entry begins a spreading catastrophe rather than instantaneous universal destruction. The recommended rule is that exercising its power throughout a physical world requires manifestations or concentrations of its essence. Those footholds let unmaking spread, but they can be contested.

This is a proposed limit, not an established property of primordial power. Its costs, rate of spread and interaction with the six magical fields must be defined before this campaign is locked.

Regions suffer failing wards, altered terrain, unstable magic and broken routes. Communities evacuate, defend essential resources and attempt to recover people trapped in corrupted areas. Exposure still does not automatically establish willing allegiance to Nhal.

Inverted installations remain valuable, but their role changes. Before the fall they reinforced the Shroud; afterward, adapted configurations maintain local stability and resist unmaking. This additional capability must be developed through research and field operations. They do not simply continue strengthening a Shroud that no longer exists.

The player helps secure concentrations of survivors and deny Nhal footholds. Earlier victories preserve populations and places rather than being erased by the catastrophe. Both alliances continue their own defensive campaigns; existential danger does not itself establish peace, friendly trade or shared playable allegiance.

## 8. The Seven Unbound — cooperation without concealed sacrifice

The player helps establish the conditions under which the Seven can act together. Evidence of the past must be recovered and tested, threatened populations protected and practical safeguards built. Oth'varen contributes firsthand testimony without becoming an omniscient authority over the gods.

The divine conflict involves several responsibilities:

- [[The Ascendant]] acknowledges misuse of Keeper's restricted knowledge while defending the Kel'vari's right to exist. Creating them was not service to Nhal, and their people do not inherit collective guilt.
- [[The Keeper]] reveals the full truth of the Binding and relinquishes any claim to decide another being's sacrifice without consent.
- [[The Seeker]] shares uncertain knowledge when waiting for complete certainty would condemn others.
- [[The Shaper]] confronts losses that cannot be repaired by restoring every ancient work unchanged.
- [[The Guardian]] accepts forms of protection that do not depend on endless personal sacrifice.
- [[The Warrior]] distinguishes accountability from vengeance that would prevent necessary cooperation.
- [[The Judge]] helps establish obligations that constrain divine power as well as mortal institutions.

They need not forgive one another before cooperating. The recommended resolution is an explicit, informed agreement with shared limits, responsibilities and means of withdrawal. Its relationship to separate alliance societies remains to be designed; divine cooperation does not merge their governments or player factions.

**Campaign resolution:** the Seven agree to a bounded combined working, supported by a mortal-built defensive network. This reverses the moral pattern of the Binding: no participant is deceived about the cost.

## 9. The primordial confrontation — Nhal loses its unified existence

Mortals secure and operate the network that makes confrontation possible. The Seven temporarily combine their energies while remaining independent persons. They do not permanently erase themselves to recreate Aethyr.

The exact method of defeating Nhal remains a design question. The recommended direction is that prepared, distributed workings constrain its physical manifestations while the Seven act together against its coherent presence. The solution must require the discoveries and relationships earned throughout the saga, rather than an unrelated final artifact.

As defeat becomes unavoidable, Nhal deliberately shatters its remaining essence across the world. This is its final destructive act and a deliberate echo of Aethyr's self-shattering, undertaken for a different purpose.

**Recommended permanent consequence:** Nhal's unified consciousness ends. Its fragments cannot simply reunite on command, and there is no hidden intact Nhal waiting to invalidate the victory. The metaphysical reason this fragmentation is irreversible must be established before the confrontation is finalised.

The primordial opponent is genuinely defeated. Its scattered inheritance creates the next conflict.

## 10. The scattered inheritance — corruption and emerging divinities

Nhal's dispersed essence reaches regions beyond its former footholds. The network protects some populations and helps contain or recover other areas, but cannot prevent every consequence. The player returns to places whose histories and survival already matter.

Over time, concentrations of essence develop into independent divine beings. Their number, names and exact emergence remain open. The recommendation is an uneven, internally divided group rather than seven identical evil counterparts to the existing gods.

Possible philosophies include ending suffering by ending desire and life; preserving everything in absolute stillness; severing dependence between beings; absorbing individuals into a single whole; and treating physical form as an imprisonment to overcome.

These are proposed motives, not final divine portfolios. Some beings pursue destructive ends, others oppose one another, and at least one can reject Nhal's intended legacy. None needs to possess all of Nhal's memories or obey an absent master.

To prepare this development, earlier campaigns should establish that fragments of primordial essence can develop autonomy and that neither creation nor ancestry guarantees obedience. Aethyr's successors, the Kel'vari and resistance to corruption provide thematic precedents, not automatic proof of the new process's mechanics.

## 11. The new divine war — creation, allegiance and survival

The emerging gods contest territory, magical conditions and the future of mortal societies. Some seek followers; some transform places; one or more may create a new non-playable people capable of surviving environments hostile to existing populations.

Those created beings can supply organised enemies without being uniformly hostile or inherently guilty. Their cultures, dependence on their makers and opportunities to refuse them need development. Non-playable status is not a moral classification.

The war is not automatically Aethyr-born gods against Nhal-born gods. A Nhal-born being may defend independent life; an original god may support a coercive answer to catastrophe. Individual choices create the alignments.

Deaths among the original Seven remain possible future events, not required milestones. No god is selected to die in this draft. Any such death should complete an established character arc and permanently affect people, institutions and the balance of power. Magic predates the gods; a death does not automatically erase an entire magical discipline.

The player protects populations, contests divine strongholds, builds enforceable alliances and helps beings born for destructive purposes claim other futures. The two playable alliances retain their separate identities and hostile relationship unless the creator explicitly revises that existing constraint.

## 12. The recommended ending — a world sustained by many

The final objective is to make the world resilient without recreating the original Binding. The player and other mortal communities establish a distributed protective network, drawing on the hard-earned inversion and stabilisation research. Willing divine beings contribute through explicit agreements rather than indefinite imprisonment.

Surviving Aethyr-born and Nhal-born gods are judged by conduct and bound by meaningful limits. Those who reject coexistence must be defeated or contained. The terms, enforcement and ultimate nature of containment remain to be designed; the ending must not quietly depend on another deceived or unwilling eternal sacrifice.

Mortal stewardship has real costs: maintenance, expertise, resources, local consent and defence of installations. Responsibility is distributed across communities and separate societies. This is not a proposal for one world government or automatic reconciliation between Covenant and Dominion.

The world does not return unchanged to its opening state. Some places are lost, others reclaimed, and new peoples and powers have a future. Dangerous regions and hostile forces remain, but ordinary lives can continue without an imminent primordial apocalypse.

**Final resolution:** Nhal's singular will is gone; neither primordial inheritance determines every descendant's choices; no small group of prisoners secretly bears the entire world's survival. The player's first successful inversion remains part of the foundation that made this possible.

## Decisions to settle before rewriting the main campaign

1. Approve or revise the successful Act V Major Anchor inversion and its continuing costs.
2. Define the Veilbreaker Anchor's requirements, its location and how it defeats the Shroud's separate binding arrangement.
3. Define limits on Nhal's physical influence so resistance remains credible after the Veil falls.
4. Establish what inversion can do after the Shroud is gone, and what further discoveries adaptation requires.
5. Choose the combined working that defeats Nhal and the irreversible cost of its fragmentation.
6. Develop the emerging gods, their differences and the enforcement of a final divine accord.

Expansion count, exact dates, founder deaths or defections, deaths among the Seven and the new people's identity remain open. The recommended causal arc above should guide those choices without fixing them prematurely.
