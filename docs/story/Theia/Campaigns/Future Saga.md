# Future Saga — historical proposals and deferred possibilities

> **Editorial status (3 November 2026): noncanonical historical proposal archive, not the current launch plan or an approved expansion roadmap.** The [canonical Human 1–80 campaign](Human_1_80_Canonical_Campaign.md), [current campaign index](CURRENT_HUMAN_CAMPAIGN.md) and [story decisions](../../DECISIONS.md) take precedence over any conflicting statements below. The level-80 launch concludes Nhal's coherent threat: Thes'var is defeated, Oth'varen dies correcting alignment, and the coordinated inversion shatters Nhal's unified will and memory; divine essence persists and the Seven survive. The Veilbreaker dissolves the Binding and begins the Shroud's immediate dissipation. **No later Nhal return, new divine identity, expansion plot or fragment outcome is approved.** Historical post-launch placements and proposed future events below are retained as provenance/idea material only, not current canon. The approved Conclave sequence remains part of launch where specified by the current campaign.


## 2026-10-29 — Creator LOCK: Conclave deaths and final succession

**Status: USER LOCKED.** The creator explicitly agreed to Oma'vira dying in Act V, then approved and requested locking the proposed endings for the remaining four founders, including Su'vareth's surviving contingencies and the pre-combat confrontation between Thes'var and Oth'varen. This dated decision supersedes earlier draft statements that all founders survive launch or that these five fates remain undecided.

1. **Oma'vira — Act V.** Her Act IV encounter remains a limited projection. During Act V the player reaches her actual conditioning facility within the Major Anchor complex, breaks control systems, rescues captives where possible and kills her. The Anchor operation remains the act's central objective. Existing conditioned forces do not automatically recover; Khor'vun must rely more on willing troops and surviving forces after losing her specialist work.
2. **Su'vareth — investigation leading to the Veilbreaker.** At her concealed intelligence headquarters, the player combines evidence from several cells to identify her location and dismantle escape arrangements. Independent verification defeats her final deception; the player kills her and recovers incomplete evidence of the project. Her death breaks coordination without exposing every agent. Explicitly established contingency instructions remain active after her death; they are not evidence of resurrection or a false death.
3. **Khor'vun — Veilbreaker activation.** At its fortified approaches, he refuses withdrawal despite danger to his own forces and commits his remaining troops. The player defeats and kills him while opening an evacuation route for captives and expedition survivors. Activation has already passed its stopping point; the victory determines who escapes rather than reversing activation.
4. **Vaer'uun — after Nhal's arrival.** At a major manifestation site, Nhal's actions expose the gap between its purpose and his doctrine. Despite evidence that his followers are expendable, he knowingly chooses obedience and binds himself to the manifestation's communication working. The player dismantles its supports and kills him. His death ends the Conclave's central authority and contributes evidence for constraining Nhal's physical presence.
5. **Thes'var — final alignment.** The last living Conclave founder attacks supporting connections at a critical junction of the inverted network. Before combat, he and Oth'varen confront one another: Thes'var recognises inversion works but rejects its protective purpose and chooses to destroy it. The player defeats and kills him; his interference leaves cascading instability. Oth'varen completes the correction at the cost of his life, sees the alignment hold and dies before Nhal's defeat. The player and distributed Guardian teams sustain the working with the Seven. This supplements, rather than replaces, the locked Nhal disruption: Thes'var's sabotage compounds the disturbance.

**Sequence:** Oma'vira → Su'vareth → Khor'vun → Vaer'uun → Thes'var → Oth'varen → Nhal's final shattering. Oth'varen briefly becomes the last living member of the original six scholars. The five founders receive permanent endings; surviving agents, forces and institutional consequences may continue.

**Scope:** Timing by story phase, broad site functions, causes of death and consequences are locked. Exact site names, expansion counts, levels beyond launch, dialogue and encounter mechanics remain open. Existing permanent alliance separation and solo access to the essential story remain in force. This is narrative canon, not implemented gameplay or approval of every other Future Saga proposal. The older conflicting launch-inversion proposals are superseded by the locked level-80 ending.

**Future development retained:** develop substantial recurring bosses below the Conclave and later Nhal, supporting dungeons and optional raids, with personal motives, histories, territorial or institutional power and consequences for defeat. Independent major threats should also exist. No specific roster, six-to-eight count, named new boss or raid production scope is approved; essential story conclusions remain solo-accessible.

**Affected records:** both decision ledgers, both Character Arcs copies, Future Saga, Historical Timeline and all six scholar notes. Detailed human campaign/quest/encounter packets still require a deliberate revision pass to incorporate Oma'vira's Act V death; older survival statements are superseded by this lock and are retained as draft history.


## 2026-10-29 — Creator LOCK: Oth'varen's final alignment and death

The creator separately proposed that a Conclave founder might interfere; that identity and encounter remain WORKING.

**Approved sequence and limits:**

- During the level-80 launch campaign, Oth'varen helps design the alignment, trains Guardian teams and prepares the inverted Major Anchor network. The Seven remain independent beings coordinating workings across six energies; there is no one-god-per-energy assignment or permanent reunion into Aethyr.
- He expects to survive. During the confrontation with Nhal, Nhal disrupts the alignment, endangering the gods, installations and nearby communities. Oth'varen recognises a failure resembling the First Anchor catastrophe.
- He gives the player and distributed teams the necessary corrections and remains at the central installation to stabilise the transition. His transformed nature permits exceptional endurance, but his magical reserves remain finite.
- The effort kills him after he sees the alignment hold, before Nhal's defeat. He is not resurrected, cleansed of corruption, made a divine vessel or imprisoned as an enduring power source.
- The player and trained Guardians keep the network operating and complete the work with the Seven. His knowledge survives him; his death contributes directly to the threat that forces Nhal to shatter, without making him the sole saviour.
- His permanent loss affects the player, the Guardians and surviving Kel'vari communities. The earlier evacuation-death suggestion is superseded. This historical launch-survival constraint was superseded on 2 November 2026: he dies during the level-80 launch finale.

**Still open:** the central site's name and location, exact dialogue and encounter staging, the possible founder attack and all five Conclave founders' individual death timings, sites and methods. The previously suggested founder-death table was not approved. These locks do not approve the entire Future Saga, fix an expansion count, or override the subsequently locked level-80 launch ending.

**Affected documents:** both story decision ledgers, both Character Arcs copies, Oth'varen's derived note, Future Saga and Historical Timeline. Narrative decisions do not establish implemented gameplay.

## The story's destination

The recommended ending is a world sustained through mortal stewardship and cooperation among independent divine beings, rather than dependence on imprisoned gods or one supreme primordial will. Nhal's unified consciousness ends permanently. Its surviving essence does not automatically determine the allegiance of beings born from it.

**Unapproved thematic direction for future stories:** the central question is whether people and gods can take responsibility for their inheritance without being condemned to repeat it. The final solution grows from the player's earlier work with Anchors, surviving communities and historical evidence.

## 1. Before play — the world begins to awaken

The established historical foundation is [Age of Silence and Anchor Network Development](../History/Events/Age%20of%20Silence%20and%20Anchor%20Network%20Development.md), followed by [Network Threshold and Modern Awakening](../History/Events/Network%20Threshold%20and%20Modern%20Awakening.md). The Conclave has already developed Minor Resonators, Regional Anchors and functioning Major Anchors. Their cumulative resonance redistributes access to existing primordial magic and restores Nhal's selective communication. [Shattered Delegation](../History/Events/Shattered%20Delegation.md) precedes the return of that communication; it was independently orchestrated by the Conclave.

The original First Anchor remains entangled with the ancient Veil breach. The Seven sustain the Shroud through [The Binding](../History/The%20Binding%20and%20its%20Aftermath.md). Most people do not understand the source of the awakening, and the two playable alliances remain hostile.

## 2. Acts I–IV — discovery, evidence and the first Shroud expedition

The approved five-act framework supplies the launch campaign's foundation. Distinct racial openings lead into alliance warfare, investigation of hidden devices and discovery of the Unmade network. The player encounters evidence of Regional Anchors, the Guardians' unequal knowledge and the difference between willing followers, corrupted victims and bound forces.

Limited Shroud access brings contact with surviving [Kel'vari](../Lore/Peoples/Kel'vari.md) communities and testimony from [Oth'varen](../Characters/Oth'varen.md). Independent records establish the six scholars' work, the concealed redirection of the First Anchor and the distinction between Ascendant's misconduct and the innocence of the wider Kel'vari population.

The campaign introduces inversion as difficult reconstruction of six-field workings. A controlled smaller test can establish the principle before the finale. Research, access to suitable equipment and safeguarding supporting connections prepare a meaningful attempt at a Major Anchor.

The smaller test and its exact placement are proposals; they are not already approved launch outcomes.

## 3. Historical Act V proposal — Oma'vira and Major Anchor inversion

**Creator-locked founder outcome:** Oma'vira dies at her actual conditioning facility within the Major Anchor complex after the player breaks control systems and rescues captives where possible. Her Act IV encounter remains a limited projection. The Anchor operation remains the central objective; existing victims do not automatically recover. Khor'vun loses her specialist work and must rely more on willing troops and surviving forces.

**Superseded as a launch-finale placement; retained as a historical proposal, not an approved additional ending:** the player helps capture and successfully invert one Major Anchor rather than destroying it. The operation requires securing its supporting installations, understanding the particular configuration, evacuating endangered people and completing reconstruction while the Unmade resist.

The converted installation reinforces the Shroud and preserves some beneficial redistribution of magical energy. It does not close the original breach, end the awakening, silence Nhal everywhere or free the Seven.

Success is possible here because the campaign has obtained the necessary plans, specialists, access and control of supporting links. Other Major Anchors have different configurations, damage and defences; inversion is not a universal procedure that can immediately be repeated at every site.

The converted site needs continuing maintenance and protection. Its custodians must negotiate responsibility with affected communities. The alliances pursue their own objectives and claims; this draft does not establish public cross-alliance cooperation or a shared faction-neutral government.

The victory changes the strategic situation. The Conclave can no longer assume that every captured device must be destroyed. It has an urgent reason to defend its designs, change its configurations and reclaim converted installations.

**End state:** a real local victory and a functioning foundation for later protection. The original threat survives, but the player's achievement remains useful throughout the saga.

## 4. Historical post-launch proposal — reclaiming a living homeland

**Historical sequencing only, not an approved post-launch Shroud state:** this proposed movement expands access to former Shroud territories through carefully restored Passage connections. The Shroud begins dissipating at the level-70 Veilbreaker event; this proposal cannot assume an intact Shroud after launch. Covenant and Dominion establish separate approaches and negotiate independently with Kel'vari communities. Existing low-capacity routes are not suddenly equivalent to mass transport.

The central conflict concerns who controls recovery of the homeland. Enclaves differ over opening routes, sharing archives and reclaiming lost cities. Their inhabitants need cultivation, safe water, supplies, trade and defensible homes as well as protection from the Unmade. Their choices are not reduced to whether they support the player's expedition.

The player helps stabilise routes and wards, recover evidence and reclaim a significant research complex. A historically proposed inverted Anchor supports these efforts; no additional operational Anchor is approved here beyond the current campaign. Discoveries clarify why the original First Anchor cannot safely be smashed and why restoring every ancient working is not automatically desirable.

The surviving ancient magical order can emerge during this movement with limited, valuable knowledge and interests of its own. Its identity and relationship to the Guardians still require development; this draft does not make it the hidden ruler of either organisation.

**Campaign resolution:** a community or region gains durable security, and a Conclave operation is defeated. Recovered records reveal that essential research has already been moved to another location.

## 5. Veilbreaker concept — approved launch event, historical development proposal

**Approved launch premise:** the Conclave develops the Veilbreaker, which destroys the Veil around level 70. The older label **Veilbreaker Anchor** and its specific engineering, site and construction sequence remain historical design proposals, not additional locked facts. It is not another ordinary Major Anchor, nor a declaration that the original First Anchor can now be safely replaced or destroyed.

The original catastrophe arose from redirected research whose full consequences were concealed. The new undertaking is deliberately designed to dismantle the Veil. It is more controlled in how it delivers energy: it aims to avoid the original uncontrolled continental fracturing while concentrating its effect on the boundary itself. That makes it more precise, not safe for the world.

Its construction requires rare knowledge, materials and coordinated installations. The historical proposed causal connection to Act V was that the Conclave sought a concentrated way to defeat containment after its distributed network became vulnerable to capture and inversion. This causal order has not been separately approved for the current level-70 Veilbreaker. Further technical development must explain why this was not achievable during the Age of Silence and how renewed guidance from Nhal changes its prospects.

Oma'vira is already dead. The four remaining founders have different roles and locked eventual endings:

- [Vaer'uun](../Characters/Vaer'uun.md) sees the culmination of their long obedience and defends the promise of Nhal's arrival.
- [Thes'var](../Characters/Thes'var.md) believes deliberate control can overcome the failures of the original project.
- [Su'vareth](../Characters/Su'vareth.md) protects logistics and information while preparing contingencies against defeat or betrayal.
- [Khor'vun](../Characters/Khor'vun.md) faces the consequences of defending a project that may consume the forces and strongholds entrusted to him.

**Creator-locked Su'vareth ending:** During the investigation leading to the Veilbreaker, the player corroborates her concealed headquarters through evidence from several cells, dismantles escape arrangements and defeats her final deception. She dies in the confrontation. Incomplete project evidence survives; her death breaks coordination without exposing every agent. Established contingency orders continue, clearly distinguished from her survival. This phase retains her contribution to logistics before her death.

## 6. Activation — the Veil falls

**Creator-locked Khor'vun ending:** He commands the final defence at the fortified approaches, refuses withdrawal despite danger to his forces and commits his remaining troops. The player kills him while opening an evacuation route for captives and expedition survivors. Activation has passed its stopping point; breaking his defence determines who escapes. Exact site name and encounter mechanics remain open.

The player understands the undertaking but cannot prevent its final activation. The confrontation still allows consequential successes: evacuation, preservation of research, protection of inverted installations and disruption of components that would otherwise destroy prepared refuges. Exact objectives remain to be designed.

**Approved launch event at approximately level 70 (not a future expansion turning point):** the Veil is completely destroyed, rather than merely acquiring another local tear. Nhal gains access to the Physical Realm. This is an irreversible change to the world's condition, not a larger repetition of the original breach.

**Superseded mechanism:** the old proposal had Veilbreaker separately overwhelm the First Anchor containment arrangement. Current canon instead establishes that destroying the Veil immediately dissolves the Binding because there is no Veil left to contain; the Shroud begins dissipating immediately. Anchors amplify the original tear, not the prison.

The Seven are freed immediately when the Binding dissolves. Their individual identities survive, but freedom does not automatically restore their relationships, their knowledge or unlimited power.

**End state:** the old containment is gone. The player's preserved refuges and infrastructure determine how much of the world can withstand what follows.

## 7. The Unveiling — historical threat-staging proposals within launch

**Creator-locked Vaer'uun ending:** After Nhal arrives, its actions expose the gap between its purpose and his doctrine. At a major manifestation site, he knowingly chooses obedience despite evidence that his followers are expendable and binds himself to its communication working. The player dismantles the supports and kills him. His death ends central Conclave authority and helps establish how Nhal's physical presence can be constrained.

Nhal's entry begins a spreading catastrophe rather than instantaneous universal destruction. The historical proposed rule is that exercising its power throughout a physical world requires manifestations or concentrations of its essence; the canonical campaign establishes gradual manifestation but does not approve this precise technical limit. Those footholds let unmaking spread, but they can be contested.

This is a proposed limit, not an established property of primordial power. Its costs, rate of spread and interaction with the six magical fields must be defined before this campaign is locked.

Regions suffer failing wards, altered terrain, unstable magic and broken routes. Communities evacuate, defend essential resources and attempt to recover people trapped in corrupted areas. Exposure still does not automatically establish willing allegiance to Nhal.

Inverted installations remain valuable, but their role changes. Before the fall they reinforced the Shroud; afterward, adapted configurations maintain local stability and resist unmaking. This additional capability must be developed through research and field operations. They do not simply continue strengthening a Shroud that no longer exists.

The player helps secure concentrations of survivors and deny Nhal footholds. Earlier victories preserve populations and places rather than being erased by the catastrophe. Both alliances continue their own defensive campaigns; existential danger does not itself establish peace, friendly trade or shared playable allegiance.

## 8. The Seven Unbound — launch alignment and historical cooperation proposals

The player helps establish the conditions under which the Seven can act together. Evidence of the past must be recovered and tested, threatened populations protected and practical safeguards built. Oth'varen contributes firsthand testimony without becoming an omniscient authority over the gods.

The divine conflict involves several responsibilities:

- [The Ascendant](../Characters/Divine/The%20Ascendant.md) acknowledges misuse of Keeper's restricted knowledge while defending the Kel'vari's right to exist. Creating them was not service to Nhal, and their people do not inherit collective guilt.
- [The Keeper](../Characters/Divine/The%20Keeper.md) reveals the full truth of the Binding and relinquishes any claim to decide another being's sacrifice without consent.
- [The Seeker](../Characters/Divine/The%20Seeker.md) shares uncertain knowledge when waiting for complete certainty would condemn others.
- [The Shaper](../Characters/Divine/The%20Shaper.md) confronts losses that cannot be repaired by restoring every ancient work unchanged.
- [The Guardian](../Characters/Divine/The%20Guardian.md) accepts forms of protection that do not depend on endless personal sacrifice.
- [The Warrior](../Characters/Divine/The%20Warrior.md) distinguishes accountability from vengeance that would prevent necessary cooperation.
- [The Judge](../Characters/Divine/The%20Judge.md) helps establish obligations that constrain divine power as well as mortal institutions.

They need not forgive one another before cooperating. The recommended resolution is an explicit, informed agreement with shared limits, responsibilities and means of withdrawal. Its relationship to separate alliance societies remains to be designed; divine cooperation does not merge their governments or player factions.

**Campaign resolution:** the Seven agree to a bounded combined working, supported by a mortal-built defensive network. This reverses the moral pattern of the Binding: no participant is deceived about the cost.

## 9. Level-80 launch finale — alignment, loss and Nhal's shattering

**Creator-locked contribution and death — 29 October 2026:** Oth'varen helps design the alignment, trains Guardian teams and prepares the inverted Major Anchor network expecting to survive. The Seven remain independent persons, coordinating their different workings across all six energies without permanently recreating Aethyr. Mortals maintain and defend the installations.

**Current locked sequence:** Thes'var dies in combat; his sabotage misaligns convergence. Only then does Oth'varen realise that correcting the alignment will kill him. He tells Sera and the player, gives the necessary corrections to distributed teams and stays at the central installation to stabilise the transition. Earlier language attributing the decisive misalignment solely to Nhal is superseded. His transformed nature lets him endure exceptional exposure, but his reserves are finite. He physically dies after seeing the alignment hold and before Nhal's defeat.

The player and trained Guardians maintain the alignment and complete the operation with the Seven. Oth'varen does not channel the gods through his body, become an enduring power source, lose his corruption or return from death. His knowledge survives him; his death causes a real loss and contributes directly to the threat that forces Nhal to shatter.

**Creator-locked Thes'var ending:** The last living founder attacks supporting connections at a critical junction of the inverted network, compounding Nhal's disruption. Before combat he confronts Oth'varen, recognises inversion works and rejects its protective purpose. The player defeats and kills him. His sabotage leaves cascading instability that Oth'varen corrects at fatal cost. Oth'varen briefly becomes the last living member of the original six scholars, sees the alignment hold and dies before Nhal's defeat; the player and trained teams sustain the working with the Seven. Exact control layout, dialogue and encounter mechanics remain open.

The remaining technical staging and exact final working require development. Existing inversion research, the distributed network, divine consent and mortal participation must remain causally essential.

**Superseded historical proposal:** Nhal deliberately shattering its own essence is not the approved launch mechanism. In the locked level-80 finale, the player, Seven and distributed Guardian teams complete the coordinated inversion, shattering Nhal's coherent divine identity and will. Indestructible essence remains fragmented without Nhal's unified consciousness or memories; its future is undecided. The distinction between the approved alignment/death sequence and other future proposals remains governed by the dated decision ledger. The irreversible fragmentation mechanism and subsequent divinities require their own detailed development; founder fates are governed by the dated creator lock above.

## 10. Post-launch possibilities — unapproved scattered inheritance

Nhal's dispersed essence reaches regions beyond its former footholds. The network protects some populations and helps contain or recover other areas, but cannot prevent every consequence. The player returns to places whose histories and survival already matter.

**Unapproved possibility:** Over time, concentrations of essence might develop into independent divine beings. Their number, names and exact emergence remain open. The recommendation is an uneven, internally divided group rather than seven identical evil counterparts to the existing gods.

Possible philosophies include ending suffering by ending desire and life; preserving everything in absolute stillness; severing dependence between beings; absorbing individuals into a single whole; and treating physical form as an imprisonment to overcome.

These are proposed motives, not final divine portfolios. Some beings pursue destructive ends, others oppose one another, and at least one can reject Nhal's intended legacy. None needs to possess all of Nhal's memories or obey an absent master.

To prepare this development, earlier campaigns should establish that fragments of primordial essence can develop autonomy and that neither creation nor ancestry guarantees obedience. Aethyr's successors, the Kel'vari and resistance to corruption provide thematic precedents, not automatic proof of the new process's mechanics.

## 11. Unapproved expansion concept — a new divine war

The emerging gods contest territory, magical conditions and the future of mortal societies. Some seek followers; some transform places; one or more may create a new non-playable people capable of surviving environments hostile to existing populations.

Those created beings can supply organised enemies without being uniformly hostile or inherently guilty. Their cultures, dependence on their makers and opportunities to refuse them need development. Non-playable status is not a moral classification.

The war is not automatically Aethyr-born gods against Nhal-born gods. A Nhal-born being may defend independent life; an original god may support a coercive answer to catastrophe. Individual choices create the alignments.

Deaths among the original Seven remain possible future events, not required milestones. No god is selected to die in this draft. Any such death should complete an established character arc and permanently affect people, institutions and the balance of power. Magic predates the gods; a death does not automatically erase an entire magical discipline.

The player protects populations, contests divine strongholds, builds enforceable alliances and helps beings born for destructive purposes claim other futures. The two playable alliances retain their separate identities and hostile relationship unless the creator explicitly revises that existing constraint.

## 12. Unapproved long-range ending concept — a world sustained by many

The final objective is to make the world resilient without recreating the original Binding. The player and other mortal communities establish a distributed protective network, drawing on the hard-earned inversion and stabilisation research. Willing divine beings contribute through explicit agreements rather than indefinite imprisonment.

Surviving Aethyr-born and Nhal-born gods are judged by conduct and bound by meaningful limits. Those who reject coexistence must be defeated or contained. The terms, enforcement and ultimate nature of containment remain to be designed; the ending must not quietly depend on another deceived or unwilling eternal sacrifice.

Mortal stewardship has real costs: maintenance, expertise, resources, local consent and defence of installations. Responsibility is distributed across communities and separate societies. This is not a proposal for one world government or automatic reconciliation between Covenant and Dominion.

The world does not return unchanged to its opening state. Some places are lost, others reclaimed, and new peoples and powers have a future. Dangerous regions and hostile forces remain, but ordinary lives can continue without an imminent primordial apocalypse.

**Final resolution:** Nhal's singular will is gone; neither primordial inheritance determines every descendant's choices; no small group of prisoners secretly bears the entire world's survival. The player's first successful inversion remains part of the foundation that made this possible.
