# Theia level 35 to 40 progression prototype

**Status: Needs Review — 31 October 2026.** This is a narrative and XP-balancing proposal for one five-level interval, not implemented or playtested content. It uses the proposed level-80 arrangement, placing the first Shroud expedition around level 35. Neither that cap nor the changed Shroud-access placement is approved. The existing five-act 1–60 draft remains unchanged in scope.

The prototype applies the approved separation of level-gated story chapters and independent world progression. It also preserves ships and fixed-route flight paths for ordinary transport, onward journeys on foot or by mount, exceptional low-capacity Passage gates, separate hostile alliances, and solo access to the essential story.

## The journey being tested

At level 35, the player has completed the preceding Anchor investigation and has evidence that inversion can work. Oth'varen is still known to them as the Grey Surveyor. His advice has been valuable, but he has not explained his own extraordinary familiarity with the technology.

The next main chapter takes the player into a living Kel'vari homeland. It ends with a small expedition established under local permission. The following main chapter, provisionally unlocked at level 40, opens the archive investigation and Oth'varen's identity revelation.

The intervening adventures should make that revelation matter. Before learning that their adviser witnessed the original catastrophe, the player should know people still living with its consequences.

For this prototype, three enclave-associated outposts and their surrounding routes form a proposed frontier district around Lantern Redoubt. These are not new enclave capitals. Exact distances, adjacency and the district's final name depend on the map; no established coastlines or capital locations are changed here.

## The opening story chapter — Permission to Remain

At Tidehold, Corven authorises a bounded expedition with explicit commitments from the participating Covenant peoples. The player accompanies specialists through the ordinary transport network to the highland assembly point. A flight path reaches an established hub; the remaining approach to the Passage site is made on foot or by mount. The portal is a rare expedition connection, not a substitute for ordinary regional travel.

Nessa checks the field plan with the player. Tarek requires a measured return path before allowing another group to cross. Supplies must fit the gate's capacity, and specialists cannot all arrive at once. Brynja accompanies the technical party while Caelith prepares observations of the living environment. The Grey Surveyor travels as an adviser whose knowledge remains unexplained.

Crossing brings the player to the outer ruin provisionally called Lantern Redoubt. Aru'ven refuses to let military necessity become an assumption of ownership. The arrivals can shelter temporarily, but their presence creates obligations to the people already using the area.

A local work party has failed to return from a water-maintenance route. The player accompanies Kel'vari workers along an existing service path, encounters territorial wildlife and finds the survivors trapped by an ancient defensive installation. Recovering them requires reaching a separate control station, protecting a worker while they identify the correct mechanism and securing the retreat. These are sequential tasks completable by one player with appropriate NPC assistance.

The Grey Surveyor recognises part of the installation before anyone finishes translating it. When pressed, he supplies enough information to prevent another injury but avoids explaining his familiarity. Aru'ven notices. The player has a reason to be grateful and a reason to remain curious.

The work party's records reveal that damage along the supply route has encouraged scavenging, dangerous animal movement and theft. A second expedition follows those problems to a storehouse used by several local groups. Its defender is a damaged ancient construct still attempting to fulfil an obsolete protective instruction. It is not serving Nhal. The player isolates the damaged working and makes the route usable again without requiring a new permanent class ability.

The chapter concludes with an agreement defining Lantern Redoubt's use, supply obligations, boundaries and withdrawal arrangements. Nessa accepts limits on deployment. Aru'ven authorises continued presence and points the player towards local contacts willing to employ outside help.

There is no sudden declaration that all Kel'vari trust Covenant. The player has earned one community's limited permission.

Aru'ven explains that the archive custodians must review the request and prepare a supervised approach. Nessa has work to organise, and Tarek needs further route observations. This creates a natural chapter break, not a real-world countdown.

The chapter contains approximately eleven rewarded quest steps across these expeditions. Briefings and conversations belong within the substantial missions rather than each receiving a full mission reward. For the first numerical test, ten steps award 2,250 XP and the conclusion awards 5,000 XP: **27,500 quest-turn-in XP**. Individual rewards will be redistributed after their real durations are known.

## Regional story one — The Return Route

A Thuma'rei-associated outpost depends on a route carrying food, repair materials and messages between settlements. The expedition's arrival has increased traffic without increasing the route's safe capacity.

A local custodian asks the player to accompany a scheduled supply party. Along the way, they find that an animal nesting area has shifted towards a sheltered crossing and that travellers are diverting onto a longer, hazardous path. The problem is worsened by damaged warning markers and a cache of supplies taken by scavengers.

Across eight quest steps, the player surveys alternatives, recovers equipment, protects workers and helps secure an existing route. Objectives are grouped into outward and return expeditions. Reports can be delivered to a field contact instead of repeatedly returning to the starting outpost.

The concluding encounter protects a supply movement against the area's major territorial threat. The reward is a dependable connection for the community and a negotiated local transport stop where feasible. No unrestricted portal or instant waypoint is unlocked.

The story resolves a real problem. It does not reveal Oth'varen, expose another Conclave founder or turn an ordinary danger into a hidden Unmade plan.

Seven steps award 1,500 XP and the conclusion awards 3,500 XP: **14,000 quest XP**. Content levels and rewards are illustrative; most ordinary tasks are intended around levels 36–38, with the conclusion towards the upper part of the interval.

## Regional story two — What the Gardens Need

An outlying cultivation settlement associated with Oru'sava is losing a portion of its harvest. One working improves growth, but its operation appears to coincide with symptoms among workers and animals.

The first explanation is too simple. Investigation reveals several interacting problems: neglected equipment, contaminated stores and a magical configuration that no longer matches local conditions. The player assists cultivators, gathers comparative observations and protects people inspecting the affected district.

Caelith participates as an observer, not as the outsider who teaches the Kel'vari how to cultivate their own homeland. Local knowledge identifies which changes are genuinely unusual. Anwen can later receive appropriately authorised medical observations without becoming a Guardian or gaining access to secret enclave maps.

The player helps the community choose between isolating the failing branch immediately or conducting a bounded repair with safeguards. Both outcomes preserve a viable harvest and protect the people. The choice changes local dialogue and follow-up work without supplying indispensable campaign evidence on only one route.

The eight-step chain ends with a stable cultivation arrangement and a community able to maintain it. Its lesson is that useful magic requires continuing responsibility.

Seven steps award 1,500 XP and the conclusion awards 3,500 XP: **14,000 quest XP**.

## Regional story three — The People Who Return

A Soru'vaeth reclaiming party is preparing to enter a ruined material-research district. They need tools and salvage for current homes, rather than an expedition solely devoted to spectacular ancient treasures.

The player helps examine the party's plan, accompanies the outward journey and finds signs of an earlier group that never returned. Salvage records establish that its members made a consequential mistake about an old defensive boundary. Their records and possessions matter to surviving relatives as well as scholars.

The party must decide what can safely be recovered without sacrificing people to the expedition's original targets. A dangerous construct protects the final work area. The player disables its supporting defences before the concluding encounter, while NPC crews secure already-cleared stations. No simultaneous multiplayer interaction or raid completion is required.

The final return gives the residents materials they can use and a more accurate account of those who were lost. Oth'varen helps interpret a technical warning, then accepts a correction from a local worker who understands how the district changed. The player sees him begin to share authority without yet knowing his history.

The eight-step chain supplies **14,000 quest XP**, using the same provisional seven standard rewards and larger conclusion.

## Smaller adventures and alternatives

Six standalone local quests offer **1,250 XP each**, or **7,500 XP** in total. They concern ordinary needs such as lost tools, safe hunting grounds, a missing delivery, a damaged warning marker, an endangered researcher and an inhabited route. None contains an indispensable revelation. Some can be completed alongside travel for the larger stories.

The three regional chains and these local quests form a sample route, not a mandatory completion checklist. Other racial stories, eligible adventures elsewhere, combat, gathering or optional dungeons must be able to supply replacement XP. Additional alternatives need to be authored before this becomes a production-ready interval.

The player may also leave the frontier, follow ordinary transport connections elsewhere and return later. They do not lose the main-story opportunity by choosing another suitable region.

## Proposed level requirements

These are invented Theia prototype values, not GW2 values or a decision about the final curve.

| Transition | XP required |
|---|---:|
| 35 to 36 | 20,000 |
| 36 to 37 | 21,000 |
| 37 to 38 | 22,000 |
| 38 to 39 | 23,000 |
| 39 to 40 | 24,000 |
| **Total** | **110,000** |

The sample budget is:

| Source | XP | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Opening main chapter | 27,500 | 25% |
| Three regional chains | 42,000 | 38.2% |
| Six standalone local quests | 7,500 | 6.8% |
| Combat across all activities and journeys | 22,000 | 20% |
| First-time exploration discoveries | 7,000 | 6.4% |
| Gathering and other eligible activities | 4,000 | 3.6% |
| **Total** | **110,000** | **100%** |

Combat includes enemies encountered during story missions and regional quests. It is counted once and is not a separate compulsory grinding phase. Gathering is one possible contribution, not a profession requirement. Discovery XP is awarded once for meaningful discoveries, not repeatedly for using a flight path.

The opening chapter's 27,500 turn-in XP would take a character starting at exactly level 35 to level 36 plus 7,500 XP, before combat. The character still needs **82,500 XP** to reach 40. Actual completion levels depend on the order of activities and combat rewards; the fractional-level description is only approximate.

The 25/45/30 split describes one sample play route, not a restriction applied to individual players. A player who begins overlevelled may already qualify for the next chapter and can progress after meeting the story prerequisites.

## Playtime hypothesis

The initial test target is **8–12 hours** for a first-time player to go from level 35 to 40 using a mixture of story, local adventures and exploration. A middle-case schedule might contain 2.5 hours of main chapter play, 4.5 hours of regional and local stories, 1.5 hours travelling between activity areas and one to two hours of player-directed exploration or other activities. Combat is included within those activity times rather than added again.

This is a hypothesis, not measured evidence. A standard quest lasting ten minutes and awarding 1,500 turn-in XP delivers a different rate from one lasting twenty minutes, even before combat and travel are considered. Eleven story steps, thirty regional/local steps, discovery locations and encounter density must all be measured together.

Travel should be experienced as a journey through useful places. Ships and flight paths connect relevant hubs; the player reaches outlying objectives on foot or by mount. NPC contacts can remain at field stations during expeditions to avoid unnecessary long return trips. No guessed flight duration or portal queue is treated as established geography.

## The next main chapter — The Sixth Scholar

At level 40, after completing Permission to Remain, the next main chapter becomes available. The player does not need all three regional-chain completion flags, every discovery, a specific crafting profession or a PvP victory.

The archive custodians have completed their preparations. The request progresses through the existing expedition agreement, which is sufficient to establish basic access; optional local service can enrich their dialogue without becoming a hidden prerequisite.

At Oth'irun's supervised archive approach, original project records identify six scholars. The Grey Surveyor appears among them as the independent stability verifier, Oth'varen. His account is checked against separately held evidence. The player now understands that the adviser they have travelled with witnessed the catastrophe whose consequences surround them.

For players who explored these regional stories, that discovery concerns people whose water routes they secured, whose cultivation they helped preserve and whose dead they helped remember. Other levelling choices must leave the essential revelation understandable through the main chapter's own context.

This chapter's rewards belong to the next interval and are not included in the 110,000 XP budget above.

## What the prototype must establish

The first playtest should measure elapsed activity and travel time, XP sources, actual quest completion order, downtime and which content players skip. It should check whether fast or slow routes leave the interval enjoyable, whether a player can reach the next unlock through alternative activities, and whether the protected solo path delivers the full essential story.

If rewards leave the player short, the response may be better activity rewards or more worthwhile alternatives. If they reach 40 too early, the response may be reward redistribution or richer optional incentives. Merely stretching travel or reducing all XP until the player grinds is not a substitute for the intended regional adventure.

No XP economy, creature roster, combat tuning, flight-path implementation, map coordinates or eight-to-twelve-hour claim is established as tested behavior by this draft.
