# Theia level 1 to 80 progression prototype

**Status: Needs Review — 31 October 2026.** This is a complete progression proposal for a Human character following the proposed five-act launch story. It includes minimum-level chapter unlocks, regional content, an illustrative XP curve, interval budgets and first-time playtime targets. It is not an implemented or measured levelling system.

The level-80 cap and revised act bands remain proposals. Earlier approved plot outcomes retain their scope, but moving those outcomes into this launch structure—including Oma'vira in Act II and Oth'varen's death at the level-80 finale—still needs explicit approval. This draft does not silently replace the existing level-60 decisions. Working place names and new regional stories remain proposals.

The structure follows the approved minimum-level chapters with independent world progression, solo access to essential story, permanent alliance separation and transport by ships and fixed-route flight paths with onward journeys on foot or by mount. Exceptional ancient Passage gates retain their capacity and narrative limits. No heart interface, downscaling system, mount-unlock level, flight carrier or realm-wide mutable world state is approved by this prototype.

## The intended journey

Act I covers levels 1–20. Acts II–V each cover fifteen levels: 20–35, 35–50, 50–65 and 65–80. A new substantial chapter normally becomes available at 1, 5, 10, 15 and every five levels thereafter. Each chapter also requires its own story predecessors. These are minimums, not windows that expire or deadlines for completing a chapter.

There are sixteen levelling chapters and a separate level-80 finale. The chapter at 75 prepares the final operation. The player reaches 80 through preparations and wider adventures before starting the confrontation. Neither the finale nor the epilogue is used to supply XP needed to unlock itself.

The target is **107–156 hours to reach level 80**, plus **2–3 hours for the finale and aftermath**, or approximately **109–159 hours for the complete launch journey**. These are design hypotheses for first-time, mixed play including purposeful travel, not observations. Familiarity, grouping, class balance, player skill and alternative routes will change actual times. The level cap alone does not create those hours.

## XP and choice

For a representative story-following route, each interval allocates approximately 25% of XP to main-story turn-ins, 45% to regional/racial/local quest turn-ins, 20% to combat during all activities, 6% to first discoveries and 4% to gathering or other eligible activities. Exact integer budgets appear later. Combat includes kills within quests and is counted once. Gathering is replaceable, not a profession requirement. This is not a quota players must fulfil.

The representative route completes **163 main-story quest steps and 456 regional/local steps**, followed by a provisional eight finale/aftermath mission units at 80. These are planning units; short conversations may be part of a longer quest. The resulting 627-unit sample path is not an approved production count.

To make the freedom real, regional alternatives must supply more XP than the sample route selects. The proposed authoring envelope contains **816 regional/local quest-step slots** across accessible choices, rather than just the 456 selected by the example route. Where rewards are comparable, that larger pool must offer at least 80% of each interval's XP, with about 30% obtainable through open-world activities. A non-story route can therefore reach the required XP without using the main chapter's 25%. Counts alone do not prove this: the available reward total and access conditions must be checked.

These regional slots include shared content and alternatives across several maps; they are not 816 quests unique to Human culture or separate zones per interval. Production planning must deduplicate shared quests and account for their level eligibility. Other races use the same proposed XP curve but require their own 1–20 stories and suitable later routes. This draft does not claim to write all eight campaigns.

A player skipping the main story cannot depend upon story-locked Shroud access for ordinary levelling. Eligible homeland, allied-region and conventional military-expedition alternatives must provide enough XP at those levels. Completing the principal story is still required for its own later chapters and revelations.

## The chapters and surrounding adventures

### Act I — The Crown and the Betrayal — Levels 1–20

This act's representative progression requires **108,650 XP**, with a proposed **14–21-hour** playtime envelope.

#### Level 1 chapter — The first road

The player begins in Millhaven under Elara Fenwick. A delayed medical parcel, an unsafe road and Brenna Holt's missing caravan establish the opening investigation. Darian Cole is eager to act before he is ready. Sister Anwen's strengthened healing provides an early, beneficial sign of the awakening. The chapter closes with evidence that the caravan's loss deserves a wider search, not a revelation about ancient gods.

The surrounding Heartlands offer farm disputes, predators, ordinary theft, basic profession work and exploration around inhabited paths. Tomas's seed-store problem and Heddan's need for affordable materials give the player useful work independent of the missing caravan. Early routes are short enough to learn the district on foot; no mount unlock is assumed.

**Interval 1→5:** 3,150 XP; 2–3 hours. The sample route contains 5 main-story and 12 regional/local steps, selected from an authoring envelope of 22 regional/local alternatives. Main turn-ins supply 788 XP, selected regional turn-ins 1,418 XP and combat/exploration/other activities the remaining 944 XP. This describes one possible route, not required completion flags.

#### Level 5 chapter — Beyond Millhaven

The player traces selected stolen goods through the abandoned waystation and into Stoneford's warehouse district. The buyer's demands distinguish an organised operation from ordinary banditry. Elara recognises the limits of her warrant and prepares an introduction to Mara Thorne. The player's first broader journey has a reason grounded in local evidence.

Stoneford's river crossing, Greyfall Quarry and nearby woodland provide rescue, salvage, trade and wildlife stories. Brenna's business connects the settlements without explaining every problem. Local expeditions combine several objectives before returning; a long trip is not required for each individual conversation.

**Interval 5→10:** 14,000 XP; 3–5 hours. The sample route contains 7 main-story and 20 regional/local steps, selected from an authoring envelope of 36 regional/local alternatives. Main turn-ins supply 3,500 XP, selected regional turn-ins 6,300 XP and combat/exploration/other activities the remaining 4,200 XP. This describes one possible route, not required completion flags.

#### Level 10 chapter — The King's account

In Valecrown, Mara requires corroboration before admitting the player to the royal investigation. Aldren recounts Edric Halewyn's death at the Shattered Delegation. Cassian appears helpful and directs suspicion towards Dominion intelligence. Garrick's opposition to the negotiations makes him a credible lead, but independently kept military records begin contradicting the convenient accusation.

The capital is also a centre for civic disputes, archives, professions, supplies and military preparation. Adventures extend into its inhabited approaches and neighbouring districts, giving the player reasons to understand the kingdom before judging its rulers. Brief audience conversations are incorporated into missions and are not each awarded a full adventure's XP.

**Interval 10→15:** 34,500 XP; 4–6 hours. The sample route contains 8 main-story and 24 regional/local steps, selected from an authoring envelope of 43 regional/local alternatives. Main turn-ins supply 8,625 XP, selected regional turn-ins 15,525 XP and combat/exploration/other activities the remaining 10,350 XP. This describes one possible route, not required completion flags.

#### Level 15 chapter — The betrayal within

The player and Mara verify Cassian's complicity through payments, credentials and witness accounts held independently of his office. Aldren orders his detention. The Hidden Passage climax ends with Cassian captured and his handlers' records secured. The racial opening has a complete victory; the remaining shipping references justify the wider investigation. The full Unmade authorship remains unproven.

Returning to the Heartlands shows the effects of recovered supplies and lawful investigation. Village defence, threatened seasonal stores, abandoned work sites and longer local quest chains provide the remaining progression. The player need not earn all intervening XP through royal errands, and the wider alliance campaign waits for the level-20 unlock.

**Interval 15→20:** 57,000 XP; 5–7 hours. The sample route contains 10 main-story and 26 regional/local steps, selected from an authoring envelope of 47 regional/local alternatives. Main turn-ins supply 14,250 XP, selected regional turn-ins 25,650 XP and combat/exploration/other activities the remaining 17,100 XP. This describes one possible route, not required completion flags.

### Act III — Beneath the Shroud — Levels 35–50

The player joins the limited first Shroud expedition. Nessa commands the field party, Tarek protects the return capacity and Aru'ven requires local consent. Two substantial expeditions rescue a missing work party and secure a supply route. Lantern Redoubt gains permission to remain under explicit limits. The Grey Surveyor helps interpret ancient workings but does not yet identify himself. The archive request enters review; the next main chapter opens at 40.

This interval preserves the detailed 35–40 prototype. The Return Route involves a Thuma'rei-associated outpost; What the Gardens Need supports Oru'sava cultivators; The People Who Return accompanies Soru'vaeth reclaimers. Three eight-step chains and six local quests provide the sample route. These outposts are not enclave capitals. Optional service is not a hidden prerequisite for the archive chapter. Players levelling without this expedition need sufficient alternatives elsewhere.

#### Level 40 chapter — The sixth scholar

Records establish the authorised project's concealed redirection, the catastrophe, the five willing scholars' transformation and his resistance. The Six Reunited scene makes their shared history personal. Oth'varen's account remains subject to independent corroboration.

Archive custodians, reclaimers and threatened communities have concerns beyond the six scholars. Regional stories preserve family records, investigate safe salvage and restore supplies under local authority. Encounters distinguish hostile Unmade forces, dangerous ancient defences and innocent transformed residents. Familiarity with those communities gives the historical revelation emotional weight.

Su'vareth's interference turns the investigation into a struggle to protect witnesses and independently held copies. The combined case establishes the Shattered Delegation's Unmade authorship, including the murder of the Dominion High Ruler's brother, an anti-peace Great Clan chief reluctantly attending as an official delegate. The player locates Su'vareth's headquarters, defeats her final deception and kills her. Incomplete records expose the Veilbreaker undertaking.

Regional content addresses protection of records, threatened transport, war damage, independent Kel'vari needs and ordinary disputes. Su'vareth's documented contingency instructions continue after her death; this is not a fake-death twist. Aldren's corrected understanding does not end the alliance war. ## Level 80 — The final alignment and homecoming

The finale requires level 80 and completion of the preceding story. Its provisional eight mission units cover the final approach, control-junction operation, confrontation with Thes'var, network corrections, sustaining the transition, completing the working against Nhal, securing survivors and the homecoming. Dialogue and loss occur within those missions rather than becoming arbitrary separate reward clicks.

Before combat, Thes'var confronts Oth'varen and acknowledges that inversion works. He rejects its purpose. Nhal disrupts the developing alignment while Thes'var sabotages supporting connections. The player kills the final Conclave founder, but cascading instability remains.

Oth'varen gives the player and distributed teams the complete corrections. He stabilises the transition beyond his finite reserves, sees the alignment hold and dies before Nhal's defeat. He is neither resurrected, cleansed of corruption nor made a permanent power source. The player, trained Guardians and independent Seven complete the working.

Threatened with the destruction of its coherent presence, Nhal deliberately shatters. Its unified consciousness ends permanently; scattered power and later consequences remain. The irreversible mechanism, exact sites and encounter mechanics still require development. Those unresolved details cannot be treated as already implemented or finally explained by an XP plan.

The aftermath visits the Guardians, Kel'vari witnesses, Aldren and Mara, and finally the Heartlands. Cassian's lawful disposition and the corrected Delegation account conclude the Human case in the proposed story. Oth'varen's successors must maintain knowledge without him. Elara, Brenna, Anwen, Tomas and Darian have lives after the main campaign. A restrained report of unfamiliar scattered-essence effects introduces future possibilities after the victory has been experienced.

The finale's proposed duration is 2–3 hours including aftermath. It awards appropriate recognition and items, with exact rewards open. No post-cap mastery or extra character level is assumed. Optional raids may expand associated operations, but essential revelations, founder deaths and Nhal's ending cannot depend on them. Separate faction-loyal versions preserve the shared canonical outcome without requiring opposing players to cooperate.

## Reward authoring guidelines

A short delivery or introduction can award roughly 1–3% of a level, a normal regional quest 5–7%, a longer multi-objective quest 8–10%, and a regional conclusion 12–15%. Main missions initially target 8–12%, with major conclusions around 20–25%. These are draft weightings, not a rule that every conversation or mission must fit exactly. Early onboarding and unusual mission lengths need separate calibration.

A conclusion reward replaces that mission's standard reward; it does not stack with another full completion payment. Rewards are authored against intended content levels and measured effort. Exact downscaling, overlevel penalties and dynamic reward scaling remain open. Minimum chapter levels remain enforced regardless of the XP source; prerequisites are separate from level eligibility.

The aggregate interval budget governs the initial test. Individual step allocations must sum to it. A practical authoring pass assigns intended levels and mission durations, weights tasks and conclusions, then rounds rewards while preserving the exact interval total. The prototype does not claim every one of the 987 available planning slots already has a written quest and tuned individual reward.

At 35–40 the existing detailed example remains exact: ten story steps at 2,250 plus a 5,000 conclusion = 27,500; three regional chains each containing seven steps at 1,500 and a 3,500 conclusion = 42,000; six local quests at 1,250 = 7,500. Combat supplies 22,000, discoveries 7,000 and other activities 4,000. Total: 110,000. Extra regional alternatives require separate authoring, not compulsory completion of all three named chains.

For later intervals, the average reward per selected regional step rises from about 2,250 XP at 45–50 to about 4,359 XP at 75–80. Those averages include chain conclusions and are not uniform quest prices. This reward growth explains why raw XP totals cannot be read as playtime percentages.

## Travel, urgency and shared-world continuity

Ships and established flight paths connect a limited network of hubs. The player travels onward on foot or by mount. Quest chains group work into substantial outward journeys, with field contacts where useful; a long trip is not added merely to increase a timing estimate. Discovering a route is valuable, but no instant waypoint network or unrestricted player-controlled flight is assumed.

Ancient Passage gates remain rare exceptions. The first expedition does not open an unlimited transport network or bypass small-group capacity limits. Mount unlocks, carrier species, exact geography and fares remain open. A route may have encounters without every journey requiring an attack.

Chapter breaks use credible changes of pace: verification, surveys, agreements, preparation, recovery and bounded local victories. They do not introduce real-time countdowns or require the player to ignore an explicitly immediate threat while grinding. Faster or overlevelled players can progress through successive unlocked chapters after satisfying story prerequisites; a gate cannot guarantee that every character pauses for the target duration.

Nhal's entry and the founder deaths must remain coherent for characters at different story stages. This plan requires a shared-world presentation pass using suitable instanced story operations and scoped presentation, rather than assuming every character simultaneously changes the whole server. It defines narrative outcomes and XP pacing, not a phasing or persistent-world implementation.

## Validation and next authoring work

The numeric curve covers every transition from 1→2 through 79→80. Every interval budget sums to its level requirements, and the act totals cover the entire route once. No level-80 mission reward is counted towards levelling. The 35–40 ledger agrees with its existing prototype.

Those arithmetic checks do not establish duration, encounter quality or sufficient alternatives. Playtesting must measure all XP sources, elapsed time including purposeful travel, actual task counts, route choices, grouping effects and the point at which players outlevel local stories. Non-story routes and characters without a selected profession must also reach 80 without exhausting eligible content.

The principal authoring task is to turn the regional envelopes into worthwhile, geographically grounded adventures. An interval's authoring pool must be checked for sufficient non-main-story XP, not merely a large quest count. The 816 regional slots plus 171 story/finale units indicate a deliberately substantial Human-compatible content envelope, not a commitment to produce a thousand unique quests for every race.

Start validation with the 35–40 slice, then an early Heartlands slice and a late network-preparation slice. If a journey is too short, enrich the regional content and tune rewards based on observed rates; do not treat empty travel or mandatory repetitive grinding as substitutes. The total 109–159-hour target remains a proposal until those measurements support it.
