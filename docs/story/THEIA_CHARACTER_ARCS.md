# THEIA — CHARACTER ARCS AND MILESTONES

**Status:** Oth'varen and all five Corrupted Conclave founders have **creator-approved overarching character arcs** (14–15 October 2026). Their ancient histories, original roles, names, titles, transformations and loyalties remain established canon. Specific encounters, dialogue, outcomes and production details remain open unless separately approved.

**Sources:** [Interwoven Chronicle](THEIA_INTERWOVEN_CHRONICLE.md) and [Modern Story and Revelation Timeline](THEIA_MODERN_STORY_REVELATION_TIMELINE.md). This is a player-facing development plan, not a replacement for the historical narrative.

## Oth'varen — locked overarching arc

**Core movement:** *Keeper of Secrets → Restorer of Trust*. Oth'varen is the sixth scholar who resisted Nhal during the First Anchor's activation. His transformed body grants exceptional longevity and power, but he remains mortal, vulnerable and subject to the corruption's persistent pull. He helped found the multiracial Guardians, witnessed the five founders' corruption and survived their first post-Binding reunion. His modern identity remains to be designed.

**Inner conflict:** He believes dangerous knowledge must be contained because he saw trusted scholars conceal and misuse it. Centuries later, Guardian secrecy has also destroyed context, isolated cells and allowed the Unmade to exploit gaps in institutional memory. His challenge is to trust others with knowledge that could be used either to save or endanger Theia.

### Eight milestones — approved direction, not fixed levels

1. **The Unseen Scholar — opening.** The player encounters carefully preserved evidence, Guardian warnings and agents following an unidentified authority. Oth'varen observes their conduct without disclosing his identity.
2. **First Contact — early investigation.** Under a concealed identity, he interprets evidence of a Minor Resonator. His knowledge of six-field stability is unusually deep, and particular configurations visibly disturb him. He shares only what he believes safe.
3. **The Cost of Secrecy — Regional Anchor investigation.** A Guardian cell acts upon incomplete instructions and nearly causes a disaster while attempting to destroy an Anchor. Oth'varen recognises that restrictions he supported contributed to the failure. He begins questioning whether concealment is still protecting people.
4. **The Sixth Scholar — major historical revelation.** Recovered evidence links him to the original First Anchor. He acknowledges his identity, his friendships with the five scholars and his unsuccessful attempt to prevent activation. The player must reassess both his authority and his omissions.
5. **The Voice That Remains — Conclave confrontation.** One of the five exploits the corruption still present within him. His resistance remains an active choice, not an immunity granted by his past success.
6. **The Inversion Hypothesis — Anchor research.** Oth'varen recognises that the network's six-field resonance may be reconfigured to reinforce the Shroud. He advocates investigation rather than reflexive destruction, causing disagreement within the Guardians.
7. **The Trial of Trust — major campaign climax.** A possible inversion requires disclosure of protected knowledge and coordinated action by others. Oth'varen cannot personally control every decision. The player and Guardian allies become essential.
8. **A Different Legacy — resolution.** Regardless of whether an initial inversion is complete or partial, he begins changing the Guardians towards shared responsibility and accountable preservation of knowledge rather than dependence on one immortal witness.

### Relationship pressures — approved direction

- **Vaer'uun, the First Voice:** Oth'varen once respected his leadership; Vaer'uun regards his refusal as betrayal.
- **Thes'var, the Architect:** Their shared technical mastery and opposing understanding of the First Anchor make inversion a central intellectual conflict.
- **Su'vareth, the Veiled Hand:** Her knowledge of his habits and secrecy can be used against Guardian networks.
- **Oma'vira, the Harbinger:** She attacks his conviction that he truly rejected the transformation's invitation.
- **Khor'vun, the Warden:** He sees Oth'varen as a former colleague who abandoned those he once protected.

### Three pivotal player decisions — approved direction

- **Destroy or preserve dangerous evidence.** Early evidence could be weaponised, but may also explain the Anchors.
- **Trust the Sixth Scholar.** His testimony is invaluable, but his concealment and lingering corruption are legitimate grounds for scrutiny.
- **Attempt resonance inversion.** He must entrust other people with knowledge he once would have withheld.

**Constraints:** Do not kill Oth'varen, cleanse his corruption or make him the sole saviour during the launch campaign. His greatest change is learning that secrecy alone cannot prevent another First Anchor. Preserve the full ancient reunion as a separate Memory Scene.

## Corrupted Conclave — proposed parallel character arcs

The five are not interchangeable bosses. Each should pursue Nhal's purpose through an established scholarly talent and a personal interpretation of what the catastrophe meant. Their extraordinary longevity makes them recurring antagonists; they remain vulnerable and not divine. The Conclave formed after The Binding, and Nhal's voice returned only after the Major Anchor network crossed the critical resonance threshold.

### Vaer'uun, the First Voice — the burden of authority

**Status: creator-approved and locked (15 October 2026).** The overarching character arc and eight milestones are approved. Exact dialogue, missions, confrontation and ultimate fate remain open.

**Core movement:** The brilliant scholar who coordinated resonance and communication research became the interpreter of Nhal's remembered will during the Age of Silence. He held the Conclave together for centuries, but the return of Nhal's voice challenges whether his authority reflects his master's purpose or his own need to be obeyed.

#### Before the First Anchor

Vaer'uun was the principal scholar of resonance and communication, capable of coordinating researchers across the six disciplines. His work required identifying a recipient, maintaining a stable channel and distinguishing a meaningful response from interference. He was persuasive, capable and respected, including by Oth'varen.

As the hidden influence behind the experiments became apparent, Vaer'uun interpreted it as a discovery beyond the gods' knowledge. His confidence hardened into certainty; objections became threats to the undertaking. He justified concealed alterations and rejected Oth'varen's plea to halt activation, believing hesitation itself a betrayal of the work.

#### Divine War and Binding

The rupture transformed Vaer'uun and allowed Nhal's whispers to reach willing followers more clearly. He helped coordinate Unmade activity during the Divine War while Su'vareth cultivated networks of influence. At The Binding, reliable communication vanished. The Unmade turned to the five transformed scholars, and Vaer'uun assumed responsibility for interpreting remembered instructions.

His earliest interpretations may have been sincere efforts to preserve purpose, but over generations his authority came to depend on their acceptance. Doctrine and leadership became difficult for ordinary followers to distinguish from Nhal's will.

#### Leadership during the Age of Silence

Vaer'uun kept the Conclave's different pursuits aligned. Thes'var's Anchor research required resources and protection; Su'vareth's networks supplied them; Oma'vira maintained recruitment and conditioning; Khor'vun secured strongholds and installations. Vaer'uun established priorities, allocated resources and mediated disagreements. His leadership helped preserve a coordinated organisation rather than five isolated endeavours.

The successful Major Anchor network restored Nhal's selective whispers. Vaer'uun initially treated this as vindication of his centuries of leadership. Yet as instructions became more specific, some could challenge doctrines and priorities he had established. His defining question is whether he can acknowledge that his interpretation was incomplete.

#### Modern personality and relationships

Commanding, persuasive, intellectually formidable, strategically patient, proud and controlling. He believes his judgement is uniquely suited to expressing Nhal's purpose; his flaw is confusing certainty with truth.

- **Thes'var, the Architect:** Values his achievements but fears technical ambition can divert the organisation.
- **Su'vareth, the Veiled Hand:** Depends on her intelligence while knowing she controls information he cannot independently verify.
- **Oma'vira, the Harbinger:** Competes over interpretation: remembered instruction and authority versus devotion and altered consciousness.
- **Khor'vun, the Warden:** Relies on his enforcement and defence, but must give orders that remain practical and coherent.
- **Oth'varen:** Regards the sixth scholar's refusal as a profound betrayal of the research team; their former mutual respect makes the conflict personal.

#### Eight modern milestones

1. **The Unseen Authority — opening.** Separate Unmade cells act upon recurring directives that suggest a central leader without identifying him.
2. **The First Voice — early investigation.** Orders and testimony reveal a title uniting otherwise disparate Unmade operations.
3. **The Doctrine of Silence — historical investigation.** Evidence shows the organisation persisted for centuries without dependable communication from its patron, sustained by Vaer'uun's interpretations.
4. **The Sixth Scholar's Accusation — Oth'varen revelation.** Oth'varen identifies Vaer'uun as the original research leader who refused to stop activation.
5. **The Voice Returns — Major Anchor revelation.** The network's restoration of Nhal's selective communication initially strengthens Vaer'uun's standing.
6. **The Contradiction — internal Conclave conflict.** A renewed instruction appears inconsistent with established doctrine or strategy; Vaer'uun struggles to reconcile the two without admitting error.
7. **The Authority of the First Voice — major confrontation.** Players exploit tensions around conflicting directives to disrupt an operation requiring unified Conclave leadership.
8. **The Voice and the Will — later campaigns.** Clearer communications increasingly expose the distinction between Nhal's purpose and Vaer'uun's personal authority; his response remains undecided.

#### Defining confrontation and major encounter

Oth'varen confronts Vaer'uun with the distinction between extraordinary understanding and the right to endanger others. Vaer'uun accuses Oth'varen of betraying their greatest opportunity; Oth'varen challenges the leadership that elevated certainty above truth.

Reserve Vaer'uun for a major encounter involving coordination, resonance and Mind workings, not an early generic boss. Players may disrupt the systems through which he directs Unmade forces, forcing retreat or abandonment of an objective without necessarily killing him.

#### Long-term possibility

Nhal may eventually show that its purpose does not depend upon the Conclave's survival or Vaer'uun's authority. This should challenge rather than automatically redeem him. Denial, fanaticism, resistance or another response remains open.

**Character-defining principle:** Vaer'uun's strength kept the Conclave united through silence; his weakness is the inability to separate serving a purpose from preserving his own power.

### Thes'var, the Architect — the unfinished masterpiece

**Status: creator-approved and locked (14 October 2026).** The overarching arc and eight milestones below are approved. Exact quest scenes, raid mechanics, dialogue and his ultimate fate remain open.

**Core movement:** From an extraordinarily gifted structural scholar whose innovations complemented Oth'varen's stability expertise to a corrupted visionary whose centuries of independent research made Nhal's return possible. His tragedy is not an absence of intelligence: he can recognise almost any weakness in a magical structure, but no longer sees the fatal weakness in the purpose he serves.

#### Before the First Anchor

Thes'var specialised in the architecture required to sustain interactions among multiple magical fields. Oth'varen understood why a working remained stable and where its limits lay; Thes'var saw how those components could be rearranged to achieve something previously impossible. Their collaboration was productive because each challenged the other. Thes'var respected Oth'varen's criticism when it exposed genuine flaws, while Oth'varen relied upon the Architect's imagination to test assumptions.

Their philosophies differed. Oth'varen believed a structure was not truly understood until its limitations were understood. Thes'var treated limitations as invitations to design something better. Neither position was inherently wrong. Nhal exploited Thes'var's conviction that every sufficiently understood problem must possess a solution.

During the six-field experiments, unexpected results appeared that Thes'var could reproduce without fully explaining. His excitement became a willingness to trust results that seemed to vindicate his ambition. When the five suspected a hidden intelligence, he regarded it as a source of knowledge beyond the gods. He incorporated concealed alterations into the First Anchor and increasingly interpreted Oth'varen's warnings as failures of imagination rather than sound objections.

#### Catastrophe and the centuries of silence

The rupture transformed Thes'var and destroyed his homeland. He interpreted the Anchor's ability to reach beyond the Physical Realm as a real achievement and its devastation as a failure of containment and execution. Nhal's corruption reinforced that interpretation, allowing him to regard the undertaking as unfinished rather than fundamentally mistaken.

After The Binding cut off Nhal's guidance, Thes'var continued reconstructing the original research from memory, damaged records and surviving materials. Failures accumulated over centuries. Minor Resonators eventually demonstrated distant resonance with the breach; Regional Anchors linked installations; Major Anchors synchronised the network and ultimately enabled Nhal's selective communication to return. **Thes'var did not merely wait for Nhal: he helped make that return possible.**

#### Modern personality

He is composed, meticulous, intensely curious, intellectually arrogant and obsessive about precision. He can acknowledge suffering without accepting that it invalidates his objective. He retains selective affection for the original colleagues, especially Oth'varen, but the corruption lets him construct sophisticated justifications for actions the uncorrupted scholar might have condemned.

He speaks as a researcher confident in his evidence rather than as a conventional shouting villain. He respects genuine insight, even from enemies, and becomes most unsettled when another scholar demonstrates a possibility he has not considered.

#### Relationships

- **Vaer'uun, the First Voice:** Respects his authority but resents doctrine when it obstructs research.
- **Su'vareth, the Veiled Hand:** Relies on her for resources and secrecy while disliking political dependence.
- **Oma'vira, the Harbinger:** Values recruitment and devotion as practical supports but trusts reproducible evidence more.
- **Khor'vun, the Warden:** His closest operational ally, especially in defending and stabilising installations; they disagree about acceptable risks.
- **Oth'varen:** His greatest intellectual rival and the former colleague whose judgement mattered most.

#### Eight modern milestones

1. **The Signature — early campaign.** Players discover recurring structural principles among seemingly unrelated Minor Resonators without knowing their designer.
2. **The Inheritance of Failure — Regional Anchor investigation.** Recovered laboratories reveal generations of failed prototypes, refinements and reused designs. The title *the Architect* emerges.
3. **The Scholar's Recognition — Oth'varen investigation.** Oth'varen recognises Thes'var's methods and reluctantly reveals part of their collaboration, initially concealing their shared identity.
4. **The First Encounter — middle-to-late campaign.** Players meet Thes'var during a Regional Anchor operation. He studies their handling of the structure and acknowledges genuine competence before withdrawing or forcing retreat. Exact staging remains open.
5. **The Network Revealed — Major Anchor storyline.** The scale of his achievement becomes clear: centuries of engineering culminated in the network that strained the Shroud and restored Nhal's whispers.
6. **The Impossible Alternative — inversion discovery.** Thes'var immediately recognises the technical validity of Oth'varen's inversion hypothesis but rejects its purpose: reinforcing the prison he has spent centuries trying to weaken.
7. **The Contest of Designs — major confrontation.** Players attempt to capture or reconfigure a significant Anchor while Thes'var seeks to preserve its destabilising purpose. The conflict concerns control and stability of interdependent workings, not merely killing a boss.
8. **The Unfinished Work — aftermath.** His certainty is challenged but he is not automatically redeemed or killed. His survival permits later campaigns and deeper confrontation with Oth'varen.

#### Defining confrontation

When presented with the inversion hypothesis, Thes'var studies it seriously. He sees the elegance of the alternative arrangement and understands why it might work. For a moment, he and Oth'varen approach the same problem as scholars again. Their dispute is not whether the design is possible, but whether strengthening the Shroud is an acceptable purpose for the knowledge they share. Thes'var regards the original catastrophe as an incomplete design; Oth'varen regards it as proof of the danger of pursuing power without understanding its consequences.

#### Major encounter principles

A late operation may centre on a partially activated Major Anchor. Thes'var alters wards, conduits and six-field relationships while players seek to stabilise or isolate the network. A victory can force him to abandon an installation, creating an opportunity to investigate inversion. Do not require his death as the encounter's outcome. Exact mechanics and the location remain undecided.

**Character-defining principle:** Thes'var is correct about the extraordinary technical significance of his achievements and catastrophically wrong about their purpose. He can recognise the validity of inversion yet reject it because it would turn his life's work against Nhal.

### Su'vareth, the Veiled Hand — the web of betrayal

**Status: creator-approved and locked (14 October 2026).** The overarching character arc and eight milestones are approved; specific missions, the first Guardian betrayal, evidence recovered from the Shattered Delegation and her ultimate fate remain open.

**Core movement:** From a trusted Kel'vari research coordinator responsible for records, external relationships and institutional confidence to the architect of a covert network embedded across hostile civilizations. Her greatest talent is not inventing obvious lies, but arranging authentic information so that people reach false conclusions.

#### Before the First Anchor

Su'vareth managed restricted records, coordinated research and negotiated with the scholarly assemblies and federal authorities. She understood how evidence was assessed, how institutional cooperation sustained ambitious projects and how research could be delayed or approved. Her original competence made the First Anchor's extraordinary undertaking possible.

Nhal exploited her belief that great discoveries required discretion. She began withholding minor discrepancies to avoid scrutiny, then restricted access to contradictory results and finally manipulated oversight processes themselves. By the time Oth'varen raised objections, authentic successes had been selected and presented in ways that made his concerns seem less credible. Her betrayal mattered because she understood precisely which safeguards she was undermining.

#### Divine War and Age of Silence

After the rupture, Nhal's whispers helped guide willing followers. Su'vareth mixed genuine evidence with forged accusations to deepen the gods' existing distrust, especially around Ascendant's concealed research. She cultivated intermediaries among the older races; some knowingly served the Unmade, while others believed they were protecting their own institutions.

The Binding silenced Nhal but did not destroy her contacts. Su'vareth made the Unmade's operations increasingly compartmentalised: an agent could move an artifact without knowing its destination, a scholar could share research without knowing its purpose, and an officer could pursue a covert objective believing it served an alliance's security. Her strength is **access, influence and exploitation of opportunity**, not control over every government or universal knowledge.

#### The Shattered Delegation

Su'vareth's agents orchestrated the attack before Nhal's renewed communication. She recognised that peace would remove the wartime secrecy that concealed missing supplies, forbidden research and covert operations. The death of someone cherished by the orc leader and the human ruler's most trusted adviser transformed an already fragile political effort into personal grief. She arranged evidence so that both alliances blamed the other, turning genuine suffering into entrenched certainty. The attack was a major achievement of her own judgement during the Age of Silence.

#### Modern personality

Patient, perceptive, socially adaptable, pragmatic, intellectually proud and emotionally controlled. She is interested in people and remembers what they value. Her corruption has not removed her understanding of suffering; it has removed her reluctance to exploit it. She should be formidable without omniscience and capable of misjudging loyalty, courage or cooperation.

#### Relationships

- **Vaer'uun, the First Voice:** She respects his formal authority while understanding the vulnerabilities of doctrine and controlling much of the information reaching him.
- **Thes'var, the Architect:** He needs her resources, concealment and artifacts; she needs his technology. Their dependence is productive but uneasy.
- **Oma'vira, the Harbinger:** Inspiration and devotion versus leverage and obligation; their methods sometimes reinforce and sometimes undermine one another.
- **Khor'vun, the Warden:** She respects his discipline but finds direct protection limiting. He distrusts sacrifices of loyal followers for uncertain advantage.
- **Oth'varen:** She understands his commitment to secrecy and can use his protective restrictions to isolate Guardian cells and weaken their trust.

#### Eight modern milestones

1. **The Invisible Hand — opening.** Players encounter forged documents, missing supplies and conflicting testimony, initially attributed to ordinary corruption, criminal activity or enemy sabotage.
2. **The Pattern Beneath the Lies — early investigation.** Repeated methods of concealment suggest linked intermediaries across institutions, without identifying a single mastermind.
3. **The First Betrayal — Guardian contact.** A trusted source sends investigators into danger, revealing that legitimate relationships can be exploited by the Unmade.
4. **The Shattered Truth — political investigation.** Players recover evidence that links the Shattered Delegation to a network active within both alliances. Accepted history is challenged, but the leaders' grief and distrust do not simply disappear.
5. **The Veiled Hand Revealed — major revelation.** Ancient records and modern evidence identify Su'vareth as both a founder who concealed the First Anchor's alterations and a centuries-long manipulator of institutions.
6. **The Price of Exposure — Conclave confrontation.** She threatens Guardian cells and vulnerable allies unless investigators abandon an operation. Players and Oth'varen confront the costs of secrecy and selective disclosure.
7. **The Broken Network — major climax.** Players dismantle part of her covert infrastructure and interrupt resources required for a Major Anchor. She sacrifices compromised operations to protect the wider network.
8. **The Enemy Who Remembers — later campaigns.** She adapts to the Guardians' increasing openness and remains dangerous because she understands the relationships and institutional weaknesses of her opponents.

#### Defining confrontation

Su'vareth arranges evidence so that exposing her network without careful verification would also implicate innocent people and Guardian agents. Oth'varen initially favours withholding it until secured. Players must authenticate records, protect witnesses and distinguish genuine collaborators from manipulated intermediaries. They defeat her operation by making truth verifiable, not by becoming better deceivers.

**Character-defining principle:** Her greatest defeat is not simply the loss of agents or a stronghold, but the restoration of trust between people she has spent centuries turning against one another. She should survive the initial exposure of her networks; exact later outcomes remain open.

### Oma'vira, the Harbinger — the shaping of will

**Status: creator-approved and locked (15 October 2026).** The overarching arc, psychological and conditioning role, and eight milestones are approved. Exact encounters, conditioning reversibility and her ultimate fate remain open.

**Core movement:** A scholar who studied the independence and perception of mortal consciousness becomes the Conclave's foremost authority on reshaping that consciousness. She does not merely recruit the Unmade; she develops methods of indoctrination, mental influence and magical conditioning that sustain many of their hostile forces.

#### The original scholar and her corruption

Before the First Anchor, Oma'vira specialised in Mind, consciousness and perception. She investigated how a mortal mind might distinguish meaningful communication from its own expectations and safely interpret an intelligence fundamentally unlike itself. This expertise was essential to the Anchor's publicly authorised purpose of communicating with Ascendant.

Repeated six-field resonances produced impressions that she initially tested cautiously. As the experiences became more convincing, she began treating their apparent coherence as evidence that ordinary methods of verification were inadequate. Nhal exploited her fascination with experiences beyond mortal perception. She came to interpret Oth'varen's objections as inability to recognise a higher truth, abandoning safeguards she had once helped develop.

The rupture transformed her and allowed Nhal's whispers to reach willing followers more clearly during the Divine War. She studied how that influence altered perception, conviction, fear and obedience. Her early methods of reinforcement gradually became the foundation for later conditioning techniques.

#### The Age of Silence — from devotion to conditioning

When The Binding silenced Nhal, Oma'vira preserved remembered impressions and followers' testimony. These became teachings for generations who had never heard the voice themselves. But devotion alone could not guarantee the combat discipline or obedience needed to defend Unmade installations and the expanding Anchor network.

She initially worked with willing followers, strengthening conviction, courage and resistance to psychological disruption through Mind workings. Some experienced benefits; others became dependent upon repeated reinforcement. Over centuries, she developed more coercive techniques capable of imposing compulsions, suppressing resistance and constraining behaviour. She also investigated severe primordial corruption, exploiting altered minds and bodies that were not always willingly aligned with Nhal.

Her work supports the **Willing, Corrupted and Bound** framework already locked as world canon. The Devoted (willing conditioned adherents), the Conditioned (coerced or bound subjects) and the Severely Altered (unstable transformed beings) are overlapping applications, **not additional races or mutually exclusive factions**. Corruption does not automatically create allegiance, and many transformed Kel'vari remain innocent.

#### Limitations and relationships

Conditioning is costly, specialised and imperfect. It may require continuing magical support. Simple compulsions can be more reliable than complex orders; subjects can resist, deteriorate or become unpredictable. Some victims can recover when bindings are broken, others retain lasting changes, and severe transformation may prevent full recovery. No universal cure, perfect obedience or unlimited army is established.

**Khor'vun, the Warden**, commands and deploys organised Unmade defensive forces. He values disciplined fighters able to interpret orders and adapt; Oma'vira believes greater control can overcome uncertainty. Their disagreements concern effective service to Nhal, not secret opposition to it.

She remains outwardly calm, perceptive and capable of genuine empathy. She understands suffering but has come to believe that removing resistance can liberate consciousness from mortal limitations. She is charismatic, patient, intellectually confident and morally corrupted by her willingness to impose that conclusion upon others.

#### Eight modern milestones

1. **The Devoted — opening.** Players encounter organised Unmade fighters whose unusual discipline appears to arise from fanaticism or military conditioning.
2. **The Broken Will — early investigation.** Evidence shows that some hostile fighters are unwilling subjects constrained by magical compulsions.
3. **The Harbinger's Methods — first major Unmade facility.** A laboratory reveals Mind workings, corruption studies, willing researchers and unwilling victims.
4. **The Scholar Behind the Experiments — historical revelation.** Guardian records identify Oma'vira as one of the six original scholars and link her ancient consciousness research to modern conditioning.
5. **The Limits of Obedience — mid-campaign.** Conditioned defenders fail to adapt during an operation; Khor'vun's forces intervene, revealing tension over the reliability of imposed loyalty.
6. **The Sixth Witness — Oth'varen confrontation.** Oma'vira attempts to prove his resistance is a condition that can be overcome. His continued refusal challenges her belief that corruption determines identity.
7. **The Breaking of Chains — major confrontation.** Players defeat willing commanders, disable conditioning workings and rescue captives where possible at a significant Unmade facility.
8. **The Unbound — later campaign.** Survivors provide testimony; some recover, others retain lasting changes or remain dangerous. Oma'vira's research continues to threaten the world beyond one defeated installation.

#### Defining confrontation and encounter

Oma'vira remembers Oth'varen as a scholar who questioned their work. She believes his resistance to Nhal is a limitation to be corrected, not a meaningful exercise of freedom. He does not deny that corruption affects him; he insists that influence does not make surrender inevitable.

A major encounter centres on a conditioning complex defended by willing soldiers, bound combatants, corrupted creatures and researchers. Players must distinguish deliberate enemies from coerced victims while disrupting the facility. Rescue, containment and combat are distinct possible outcomes, not a promise that every victim can be saved.

**Character-defining principle:** Thes'var seeks to perfect magical structures; Oma'vira seeks to perfect consciousness by removing resistance. Her arc exposes the moral horror of that purpose without reducing all Unmade hostility to religious belief.

### Khor'vun, the Warden — the last defender

**Status: creator-approved and locked (15 October 2026).** The overarching character arc, military role and eight milestones are approved. Exact strongholds, encounters, dialogue and his ultimate fate remain open.

**Core movement:** The scholar once entrusted with safeguarding the First Anchor project has become the formidable commander who protects the organisation responsible for his homeland's destruction. His tragedy is that he still considers himself a protector while losing sight of what deserves protection.

#### Before the rupture

Khor'vun specialised in protective workings, containment and the stability of defensive magical structures. He assessed research risks, developed safeguards and coordinated those responsible for the First Anchor team's security. He valued reliability and believed the success of any great undertaking depended on protecting the people carrying it out.

His relationship with Oth'varen was grounded in their complementary responsibilities: Oth'varen independently verified six-field stability, while Khor'vun built protections intended to contain the working's effects. Nhal exploited his sense of duty. As the five increasingly trusted the hidden intelligence, Khor'vun began treating scrutiny as a threat to the project. He accepted compromised safeguards and concealed weaknesses to prevent suspension. At activation, he refused Oth'varen's plea to stop.

#### The catastrophe and the Age of Silence

The rupture transformed Khor'vun, granting extraordinary longevity and resilience without invulnerability. During the Divine War he defended Unmade researchers, surviving records and locations associated with the First Anchor. He interpreted opposition to the work as interference that had prevented its successful completion.

After The Binding severed Nhal's guidance, he gathered surviving followers into disciplined defensive forces. He fortified strongholds, secured research routes and developed layered defences for the Minor Resonators, Regional Anchors and eventual Major Anchors. Over generations, his military organisation operated both within and beyond the Shroud.

His forces comprise willing soldiers, conditioned combatants, defensive specialists, corrupted creatures and constructs where appropriate. Their effectiveness comes from organisation, logistics, layered wards, controlled access, reserves and prepared withdrawal positions, not merely from magical power.

#### Conditioning and military doctrine

**Oma'vira, the Harbinger**, develops the conditioning techniques supporting many Unmade forces. Khor'vun recognises their utility but favours disciplined willing soldiers who can interpret intent and adapt to changing circumstances. Conditioned defenders can obey reliably within narrow parameters yet fail when conditions change; severely altered creatures may be powerful but unpredictable. His objections concern effectiveness and mission safety, **not the morality of coercion**.

#### Modern personality

Restrained, authoritative, observant, patient and ruthless when he judges a threat to his protected charges. He does not need to enjoy cruelty. His protective instincts are real but narrowly applied to the Conclave and its followers, while outsiders are treated as dangers to be contained or eliminated.

#### Relationships

- **Vaer'uun, the First Voice:** Respects formal authority but expects clear objectives and dislikes contradictory doctrinal orders.
- **Thes'var, the Architect:** His closest operational ally; protects research and its supporting structures while questioning unnecessary technical risk.
- **Su'vareth, the Veiled Hand:** Respects her intelligence but dislikes sacrificing loyal agents or withholding essential defensive information.
- **Oma'vira, the Harbinger:** Values her conditioned forces where useful but distrusts their inability to adapt; their dispute concerns military reliability.
- **Oth'varen:** Once a trusted colleague responsible for independent verification, now regarded as one who abandoned the team and betrayed its work.

#### Eight modern milestones

1. **The Unseen Defences — opening.** Players encounter unusually disciplined hostile forces defending ruins, supply routes and concealed research sites.
2. **The Fortress Beneath the Ruins — early dungeon.** A hidden stronghold combines ancient Kel'vari safeguards with modern conditioning and military organisation.
3. **The Warden's Doctrine — military investigation.** Recovered orders reveal a commander who prioritises research protection, personnel preservation and layered defence.
4. **The Guardian Who Fell — historical revelation.** Oth'varen identifies Khor'vun's defensive methods and reveals his original role among the six scholars.
5. **The Limits of Control — conditioning storyline.** Oma'vira's conditioned troops fail to adapt; Khor'vun intervenes with disciplined soldiers, revealing tension over imposed loyalty.
6. **The Siege of the Anchor — major military campaign.** Players assault a fortified Regional or Major Anchor defended by troops, wards, constructs and fallback positions.
7. **The Last Defence — major confrontation.** Oth'varen challenges Khor'vun's claim to have always protected his colleagues, confronting him with the lives endangered by that protection.
8. **The Broken Shield — later campaign.** A major defeat forces Khor'vun to reassess his defensive doctrine without automatically changing allegiance; he remains a possible recurring antagonist.

#### Defining confrontation and major encounter

Oth'varen's challenge is that Khor'vun was entrusted with protecting people, not shielding ambition from consequences. Khor'vun believes Oth'varen abandoned their colleagues. Neither has forgotten their former trust.

His major encounter should centre on a fortified Anchor complex. Players overcome layered defences, disable wards and reinforcements, and prevent the Warden from restoring compromised positions. He may redirect attacks through wards, isolate battlefield areas and reinforce structures, but remains killable and not divine. A victory need not end in his death; the exact outcome remains undecided.

**Character-defining principle:** Khor'vun has become extraordinarily good at protecting something that should never have been allowed to continue.

## Design guardrails

1. Avoid revealing the five founders' modern titles and ancient identities all at once.
2. Keep Covenant and Dominion hostile; separate investigations may uncover complementary evidence without producing a public alliance.
3. Do not make the Unmade responsible for every legitimate local grievance or every benefit of the awakening.
4. Do not treat a single Major Anchor's destruction or inversion as sufficient to end Nhal's communication or free the Seven.
5. Preserve uncertainty around the method, cost and ultimate consequences of inversion.
6. Do not prematurely assign dungeon or raid deaths to the founders. Their survival or defeat requires specific story decisions.
7. Separate historical canon from proposed encounters and dialogue.

## Next development decisions

The most consequential open questions are **Oth'varen's modern public identity**, the first Guardian contact players meet, which founder appears directly first, and whether the initial inversion proof of concept belongs in the launch campaign or a later chapter. These should be resolved before writing quest scripts.

## 2026-10-15 creator LOCK — Unmade hostility, corruption and conditioning

**The Unmade's hostile forces have three overlapping origins, not three new races or exclusive factions:** (1) **Willing** followers who knowingly embrace Nhal's purpose for belief, ambition, knowledge or power, and fight deliberately for Unmade objectives; (2) **Corrupted** mortals, wildlife or other beings whose prolonged exposure to unstable primordial influence has altered magical structure, perception, cognition, instincts or bodies, sometimes producing dangerous aggression without willing allegiance; (3) **Bound** soldiers, captives, creatures and constructs conditioned or constrained through Unmade Mind workings, rituals, altered six-field structures or enforced instructions. A willing follower may become corrupted; a corrupted victim may be bound. Some constructs also continue ancient defensive duties without ever serving Nhal.

**Exposure is not automatic allegiance or guaranteed hostility.** Outcomes vary from temporary symptoms through persistent influence and transformation to profound loss of control. Corruption is not universal mind control; **Oth'varen** is the central example of resistance. Many Kel'vari and transformed inhabitants of the Shroud are innocent or non-hostile. Not every hostile being can be rescued during an attack, but combat, containment, rescue and breaking compulsions should remain distinct possibilities. Avoid making all corrupted inhabitants or all Kel'vari enemies.

**Oma'vira, the Harbinger**, originally a scholar of Mind, consciousness and perception, is the Conclave's leading authority on devotion, mental influence and magical conditioning. Her centuries of work support recruitment and the transformation of willing recruits or unwilling captives into dangerous conditioned forces. Her teachings alone do not explain every hostile NPC. **Khor'vun, the Warden**, commands and deploys organised defensive forces and protects Anchor approaches and Unmade strongholds. He may favour disciplined fighters over unstable conditioned troops, creating a potential methodological tension with Oma'vira. The other founders provide doctrine, research, covert logistics and strategic purpose. Outside the Shroud, players mainly encounter organised operatives, hidden facilities, Anchor defenders and local corruption; within it, older corruption, transformed victims, wildlife, ancient constructs and enduring strongholds produce a distinct ecology of threats.

This is a **locked world and MMO combat foundation**, not approval of particular enemy designs, irreversible corruption mechanics, specific rescue outcomes, or the full previously proposed Oma'vira eight-milestone character arc. Develop those separately.
