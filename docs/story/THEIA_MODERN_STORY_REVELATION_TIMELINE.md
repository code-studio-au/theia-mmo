## 1. The core narrative principle

Players begin as members of Covenant or Dominion, not as initiated members of the Guardians and not as witnesses to the omniscient Chronicle. Their immediate concerns are local obligations, political rivalries, the ongoing alliance war and unusual magical events whose origins are unknown.

The story must not give the player knowledge merely because it appears in the Chronicle. The ancient truth is distributed unevenly among institutions and individuals. A recovered artifact may be authentic without its meaning being understood. An accurate historical record may be dismissed because it contradicts an accepted tradition. A Guardian may possess one part of the truth while another Guardian holds an incompatible fragment.

The **Shattered Delegation** is remembered publicly as evidence of enemy treachery, with Covenant and Dominion holding conflicting accounts. Its orchestration by the Unmade is historical truth, not common knowledge. The orc leader's personal loss and the death of the human ruler's trusted adviser make the event a living grievance, not a remote archival puzzle.

The **magical awakening** is genuine. The resonance network built by the Conclave has changed the distribution and accessibility of existing primordial energy, producing both beneficial developments and instability. Nhal has not created magic and does not control every newly gifted person. Players should encounter legitimate healing, craft and defensive improvements as well as dangerous anomalies.

## 2. Knowledge at the beginning of play

| Group | Likely knowledge at launch | Important ignorance or misconception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary Covenant and Dominion citizens | The war, their people's history, public accounts of the Shattered Delegation, reports of unusual magical events | No reliable knowledge of Nhal, the Corrupted Conclave, the original breach or the true cause of the awakening |
| Local scholars and magical practitioners | The six disciplines, modern magical theory, inconsistent readings and unexpected activity in ancient works | No accepted theory linking distant anomalies to a distributed Anchor network |
| Alliance rulers and militaries | Strategic implications of new magic, enemy incursions, valuable artifacts, suspicious supply activity | Usually attribute unexplained incidents to rival states, criminals or failed experiments; no verified common Unmade conspiracy |
| Ordinary Guardian field cells | A duty to protect dangerous knowledge, fragments of First Anchor history, some Unmade patterns, local warning signs | Their records are incomplete; many do not know Kel'vari enclaves survive or understand the full network |
| Select Guardian custodians and Oth'varen | Much deeper First Anchor evidence, Oth'varen's firsthand testimony, the five transformed scholars, the Shroud's peril | Not automatically a complete understanding of modern Anchor engineering or a proven inversion procedure |
| Peripheral Unmade cells | Local assignments, forbidden research, selected contacts and expectations of a hidden patron | Many lack the full network plan or direct communication with Nhal |
| Corrupted Conclave | The five founders' history, generational development of Minor, Regional and Major Anchors, restoration of selective contact with Nhal | Still cannot reproduce the First Anchor completely or act as though Nhal is free |
| Nhal | Its own purpose and the selective communications now possible through the network | Still constrained by the Veil and The Binding; its influence is not unlimited |

These are **recommended starting knowledge boundaries**, not a claim that every individual within a group possesses identical information. Exceptional witnesses and secret custodians may know more.

## 3. Recommended player revelation sequence

**Historical WORKING framework:** The phases below preserve disclosure reasoning and possible scene approaches. They do not move current Human quests or replace canonical reveal gates. Any use in other racial campaigns requires its own approval; it is not an approved Dominion campaign.

### Phase I — Local lives and the first anomalies

Players begin with conflicts rooted in their own people: frontier security, guild obligations, failed wards, disputed sacred sites, lost mechanisms, threatened settlements or political corruption. The awakening appears as scattered developments, sometimes beneficial and sometimes dangerous.

An early anomaly should have an immediate, understandable consequence. A defensive structure that unexpectedly fails endangers a settlement; a revived device offers a solution but behaves unpredictably; a supply dispute hides the theft of an artifact. None should require an early explanation of Nhal.

**Player learns:** magic is changing; ancient workings matter; people disagree about whether experimentation is safe.

**Player does not learn:** the network's purpose, the Conclave's identity, the source of the Shattered Delegation attack or the fate of the Seven.

### Phase II — Evidence of deliberate interference

Separate investigations reveal that some anomalies involve concealed alterations, stolen components or records removed from otherwise legitimate research. A suspect may be an opportunist, a manipulated scholar or an Unmade adherent; the distinction should matter.

A Guardian contact can offer limited assistance without immediately revealing the entire order. Early cooperation is earned through actions and corroborated evidence, not through an exposition speech.

**Player learns:** some incidents are being engineered; an organised hidden network may exist; not every magical anomaly is malicious.

**Player does not learn:** the full hierarchy of the Corrupted Conclave or the connection to the primordial breach.

### Phase III — Minor Resonators and the first technical pattern

Players encounter a **Minor Resonator** or evidence of its operation. It is a limited working that can cause local disturbances and can sometimes be safely disabled. Comparison with another site reveals recurring six-field arrangements and links to surviving ancient research.

The device should initially be understood through its observed effects and components, not as an item in a complete three-tier taxonomy. The designation *Minor Resonator* may become known only once investigators have reason to distinguish it from larger structures.

**Player learns:** particular devices are responsible for some disturbances; they can be studied and sometimes destroyed; their effects are not identical to all beneficial awakening phenomena.

**Player does not learn:** how the whole network is synchronised, that it restored Nhal's whispers, or that safe inversion is achievable.

### Phase IV — Hidden networks and the Shattered Delegation

Evidence of infiltrated institutions, diverted shipments and forged records begins to connect local investigations to a larger conspiracy. Some findings echo contradictions in the public accounts of the Shattered Delegation.

The personal losses of the orc leader and human ruler remain central. Discovering suspicious evidence should not instantly persuade either alliance to abandon its established interpretation. The Unmade succeeded precisely because the planted accusations aligned with existing grief and distrust.

Covenant and Dominion investigate through **separate protagonists, institutions and operations**. They can uncover different aspects of the same conspiracy while remaining hostile and without sharing a public command structure.

**Player learns:** the Unmade or a comparable hidden network has manipulated political events; the Shattered Delegation's accepted history may be false.

**Player does not necessarily learn:** complete, publicly provable authorship of the attack or the Conclave's primordial patron.

### Phase V — Regional Anchors and the Guardian disagreement

Larger installations reveal that some disturbances are linked through resonance rather than conventional Passage routes. **Regional Anchors** can influence multiple sites, and destroying one without understanding its supporting structures may be dangerous.

The Guardians' fragmented institutional memory becomes a real obstacle. One cell may favour immediate destruction; another may insist on recovering knowledge first. Their disagreement is not a new division between good and evil, but a conflict between urgent protection and the fear of repeating ancient mistakes.

This is a suitable point to introduce deeper evidence of the First Anchor and Oth'varen's unusual expertise, without immediately disclosing his entire identity.

**Player learns:** the Unmade's devices form a developing system; Guardian knowledge is incomplete; the ancient catastrophe has relevance to the present.

**Player does not learn:** a fully working inversion method or the complete fate of the Seven.

### Phase VI — Major Anchors, the Shroud and Nhal

Recovered records, comparative measurements and encounters with larger installations reveal the progression from Minor Resonators to Regional Anchors and **Major Anchors**. Major Anchors synchronise parts of the network, and sufficient alignment placed pressure upon the Shroud.

The player gradually learns that the network's cumulative threshold caused the modern awakening and enabled Nhal's selective communication to resume. This revelation should require evidence from several sources rather than one character explaining everything.

**Player learns:** the First Anchor is the original breach; the Shroud contains it; the modern network strains that containment; Nhal is an intelligence beyond the Veil; the Conclave serves it.

**Player does not automatically learn:** the full primordial history of Aethyr, Keeper's deliberate concealment of The Binding's permanent cost, or a guaranteed way to free the Seven.

### Phase VII — Resonance inversion as a discovery

The possibility of **resonance inversion** emerges from recovered stabilisation principles, technical investigation, and potentially Oth'varen's firsthand knowledge. This is a major revelation, not a routine switch that every Anchor has always possessed.

The underlying six-field structures can potentially be reconfigured so that a captured installation reinforces the Shroud rather than straining it. The redistribution of accessible primordial energy may be partly preserved, but the method requires difficult reconstruction and may have consequences that remain uncertain.

A Minor Resonator can provide a controlled proof of concept; Regional Anchors require coordinated adjustment of supporting structures; Major Anchors may demand simultaneous control of several linked sites. The historical uncertainty about whether large-scale inversion or the Seven's release occurs is **SUPERSEDED** by the canonical Human campaign. Preservation of every magical benefit is not promised. Do not infer that inversion itself releases the Seven; the canonical campaign distinguishes the Veilbreaker event from the final inversion.

The discovery creates a meaningful strategic disagreement: destroy a dangerous working immediately, or risk a harder operation to capture and reconfigure it. Neither choice should be made trivial.

## 4. Reveal gates: what must not be disclosed prematurely

These are **WORKING disclosure rationales**, not a second gate inventory. Exact Human gates follow the canonical campaign; the old general descriptions cannot advance or postpone them.

| Revelation | Earliest recommended point | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The awakening has a common engineered cause | After multiple local anomalies and evidence of hidden devices | Preserve the opening mystery and legitimate benefits of new magic |
| The Unmade operate within both alliances | After credible infiltration evidence | Avoid reducing every local conflict to the same conspiracy |
| The Shattered Delegation was manipulated | After records and witnesses can be compared | Preserve political and emotional stakes |
| Minor, Regional and Major are linked stages of development | Once multiple classes of device have been investigated | Avoid game-system exposition before discovery |
| The network restored Nhal's whispers | After Major Anchor and Shroud evidence | Make the revelation earned |
| Oth'varen was the Sixth Scholar | After trust and significant historical evidence | Preserve his role as a living source of truth |
| The Binding permanently bound the Seven through Keeper's concealment | Later major historical revelation | Preserve the central moral conflict |
| Resonance inversion is possible | After meaningful six-field research and Anchor encounters | Make it a discovery, not an opening mechanic |
| The Seven can be freed | Follow the canonical Veilbreaker revelation and outcome | The former unresolved-ending warning is SUPERSEDED; do not attribute their release to the Anchor network |

## 5. Alliance separation and narrative convergence

Covenant and Dominion should have different starting concerns, locations, witnesses and institutions. The same Unmade network may leave different traces in each alliance's territory. One alliance might first investigate diverted materials from a fortified harbour, while the other uncovers dangerous experiments within a scholarly institution. These are examples, not locked quests.

The stories can converge **in the reader's or creator's understanding** without forcing the characters into a public cross-faction alliance. Each alliance can independently disrupt different parts of a shared Unmade undertaking. Rivalry over artifacts, territory and interpretation of evidence can remain consequential even when both oppose the same hidden threat.

The Guardians can aid individuals from both sides through separate cells. They are not a third playable political faction, and their secrecy should continue to impose practical and moral costs.

## 6. Character disclosure

**Oth'varen:** His established public identity is the Grey Surveyor until the canonical Q097 reveal, not an identity still awaiting design. His knowledge of the First Anchor is firsthand; his transformed nature and struggle with corruption are not public information. His identity as the sixth scholar should be a significant revelation, not a character introduction.

**The five Conclave founders:** Early appearances may be indirect through agents, doctrine, recovered work or unusual magical signatures. After formal identification, use both their personal names and titles: **Vaer'uun, the First Voice; Thes'var, the Architect; Su'vareth, the Veiled Hand; Oma'vira, the Harbinger; Khor'vun, the Warden.** Their ancient personal names need not be publicly known at the start.

**Modern Guardians:** Different cells should not all possess identical archives or interpretations. The player can earn trust without being handed every answer. The conflict between secrecy and disclosure should be an enduring internal tension.

**Alliance leaders:** The Shattered Delegation's losses must influence political judgement. Their grief is not evidence that they are foolish, and new evidence should not automatically erase generations of distrust.

## Creator-approved launch structure — racial stories and shared main campaign (15 October 2026)

**Locked:** Each of the eight playable peoples receives a distinct, substantial **race-based level 1–20 questline**, rooted in its homeland, institutions, local characters and conflicts. Around **level 20**, the wider main campaign begins to become available. This is a narrative convergence point, **not an end to racial stories**: race-based questlines, regional arcs, ordinary combat, exploration and profession activities continue beyond level 20, sharing the player's time with the main campaign. Players can level without following the main story. The eight openings should not be reskins of one generic conspiracy quest, and not every local problem originates with the Unmade. Exact level thresholds and mandatory progression gates remain implementation decisions.

**Locked human opening arc:** An ambitious human court official knowingly cooperated with intermediaries of **Su'vareth, the Veiled Hand**, to compromise the Shattered Delegation, intending to have the human ruler's trusted adviser killed and take that position. He did not know the Unmade's full purpose or anticipate the complete devastation of the negotiations. He subsequently became the King's new adviser, while the Unmade retained leverage over him through evidence of his complicity. His motivations combine ambition, hostility towards peace, political conviction and self-preservation. He is a knowingly culpable human collaborator, **not necessarily magically corrupted or privy to Nhal's identity**.

The human level 1–20 racial arc moves from local supply theft and ordinary human outlaws through suspicious royal correspondence and a justified visit to the human **racial capital** around levels 10–15. The player encounters the King and his new adviser, hears the King's sincerely held but incomplete account of the Shattered Delegation and its personal cost, then follows a misleading trail that seems to implicate Dominion. Corroborated evidence eventually connects the new adviser to the original security compromise and subsequent concealment. The player challenges him **through the King and lawful royal authority**, not by casually attacking a court official. A level-20 climax may involve an attempted arrest, escape and pursuit through a concealed route, with capture preferred in that historical proposal. The canonical Human campaign now fixes Cassian's capture alive; only unapproved presentation details remain OPEN. The King can recognise the adviser's guilt without immediately accepting that Dominion was wholly innocent or that a primordial conspiracy exists. This provides a satisfying local conclusion and a gateway into the larger mystery, **not the complete Shattered Delegation revelation**.

**Political constraint:** The human King rules the inland human kingdom, while Covenant's hereditary High Ruler belongs to a separate branch of the royal dynasty and governs from the distinct multicultural coastal alliance capital. Do not conflate these offices or capitals. King Aldren Vaelmont, Lord Edric Halewyn, Lord Cassian Veyr, Mara Thorne and Garrick Ashford are creator-approved in the royal court character record below. Elara Fenwick and Covenant High Ruler Corven Vaelmont are also approved; precise quest staging and unapproved biographical details remain open.

**Later racial questlines:** Each people retains continuing local and political stories after the shared campaign opens. Covenant and Dominion remain hostile; characters can discover different pieces of the Unmade conspiracy without a public cross-alliance partnership. The seven other opening designs, their precise local antagonists and their relation (if any) to the Unmade are still to be developed.

## Creator-approved five-act launch campaign (15 October 2026)

**Historical approval scope:** The racial openings were Act I, not a separate prologue. The former five-act 1–60 bands, titles and limited-victory endpoint are **SUPERSEDED** for the Human campaign by the canonical 1–80 structure. Their original table remains in the pinned historical source and the [decision ledger](DECISIONS.md); do not reproduce it as a second current campaign outline.

The retained **LOCKED** accessibility principle is that each story arc has a meaningful **solo-accessible story dungeon or equivalent climax**, with eight distinct racial climaxes possible in Act I. Story-mode dungeons and solo quests provide the full essential narrative; organised raids and challenging group dungeons are optional, adding rewards and supplementary lore without gating progression. Racial questlines continue beyond level 20 alongside the main story, and ordinary levelling remains possible independently. Later acts can contain progressively more overall content as XP requirements increase, without requiring proportionally more mandatory story quests. The act bands are narrative planning ranges, not rigid level gates.

The former Act-IV expedition placement is historical, not a current reveal gate. The canonical campaign governs first Shroud access and what the player knows then. WvW in a dedicated Shroud frontier must not gate solo story progression. Exact Passage access, PvP systems, story dungeon locations, bosses and act endings remain open for development.

## Approved campaign travel and cross-continent progression (15 October 2026)

Every player must make meaningful story visits to **all four racial capitals of their own alliance**, plus the distinct alliance capital, during the campaign. The player's home capital features in Act I; the other racial capitals are introduced across later acts for significant quests involving their leaders, institutions and local concerns. The alliance capital should recur rather than appear only at the finale. Visit order may vary by starting race. Players remain free to travel to allied capitals earlier for social, trade, exploration and profession purposes; only formal story visits occur at prescribed milestones. The same principle applies to Covenant and Dominion.

Every character also undertakes **several major expeditions to the opposing continent**, with their Human placement governed by the canonical campaign rather than the old act bands. A proposed sequence is: first exposure to the overseas war and established coastal footholds; a later intelligence or artifact mission; a larger operation exploring the consequences of conflict; and a high-stakes mission involving Unmade activity or the Anchor network. Exact destinations, counts, and missions are open. Enemy-continent travel must respect existing maritime routes, supply lines and military footholds, rather than placing unexplained foreign forces deep inside racial homelands.

**World PvP and Shroud WvW are distinct.** Earlier enemy-continent visits introduce the ordinary Covenant–Dominion war and possible open-world PvP. The historical proposal places a dedicated large-scale Shroud WvW frontier with territorial and supply objectives around level 40; this is **WORKING** placement, not an approved current story gate or implemented PvP system. Required story missions on either continent and within the Shroud must remain solo-accessible without mandatory PvP victories; protected approaches or story instances can support this. WvW participation remains optional for completing the main narrative.

Travel should advance character relationships, cultural understanding and meaningful discoveries, not consist solely of courier errands. Allied capitals must feel like sovereign racial centres, separate from the multicultural alliance capital. These are approved structural principles; specific routes, quests, encounter designs and level placements are not yet locked.
